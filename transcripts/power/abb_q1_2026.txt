ABB Q1 2026 Results Presentation & Q&A — FULL TRANSCRIPT (provided by user)
Results announced 04-22-2026. Speakers: Morten Wierod (CEO), Christian Nilsson (new CFO, ex-Electrification CFO, 9 yrs at ABB), Ann-Sofie Nordh (IR).

=== KEY CONTENT ===

GROUP Q1:
- Orders RECORD $11.3B — first time above $11B; +24% comparable (+7% FX). Revenue $8.7B, +11% comparable (beat own expectations; volumes the biggest driver, price ~1%). Book-to-bill 1.29. BACKLOG RECORD $27.5B (+22%).
- Operational EBITA just over $2.0B, margin 23.5% (includes $377M real-estate capital gain; ex-gains margin +70bps YoY, of which 50bps business performance). Gross margin 39.4% (-290bps YoY: ~2/3 unrealized FX/commodity hedges; rest = Gamesa consolidation, Electrification price-cost gap, project-mix). SG&A down to 19.2% of revenues (from 20.8%). Record Q1 free cash flow $1.3B (~2x YoY; incl $425M real estate). ROCE 27% (~25% ex-gain).
- Middle East conflict: no material change in customer behavior; ME <5% of group revenues (Automation ~1/3 of that; Gulf states ~3.5% of sales). Only Process Automation saw a weaker March in ME (energy-linked customers).
- FY2026 OUTLOOK RAISED: comparable revenue growth now HIGH-SINGLE TO LOW-DOUBLE digit; operational EBITA margin to improve YoY EVEN EXCLUDING the Q1 real-estate gain (prior: "slightly improve"). Q2: positive book-to-bill despite tough comp ($600M automation order last year), high-single-to-low-double-digit revenue growth, margin up YoY.

ELECTRIFICATION:
- Orders +44% comparable to a RECORD $6.6B — "surged". ALL main segments up double digit. DATA CENTERS UP TRIPLE DIGITS; DC order CAGR ~35% (2019-2025); strong in both Q4 and Q1; pipeline good — another strong DC year expected. Non-data-center business still grew double digit ("90% of ABB is not data center").
- Utilities strong (grid build-out, esp. US). Buildings ~30% of EL revenue (2/3 commercial — strong; residential muted but ABB grew anyway).
- Revenue $4.6B (+15% comparable, short-cycle stronger); op EBITA $1.1B (+25%), margin 24.0% (+80bps) — record orders, record earnings, record Q1 margin. Q2 guide: mid-teens comparable growth, margin up.
- Price ~1%; price-cost gap (commodities/tariffs lag) shrinks in Q2, fully closed in H2.
- US: orders +67% (base orders +30%; Americas +48% like-for-like). US EL margin still dilutive to segment but improving (volume + robotization/automation build-out; "peers' main profit pool is the US — not yet the case for ABB" = upside).

DATA CENTER DETAIL:
- Many projects — hyperscalers AND colocation, US/Europe/Asia; NO elephant orders; ABB works with ALL hyperscalers, expanding scope.
- MEDIUM-VOLTAGE UPS gaining traction/share: moves power reliability out of expensive white space to the medium-voltage level (E-house) — cuts opex (better efficiency) and white-space build cost. ABB claims to be the FIRST AND ONLY provider of an MV UPS. MV UPS is a key building block of ABB's 800-VOLT DC ARCHITECTURE "that comes a year or two from now" — NONE of today's orders relate to 800VDC yet; all new projects to come.
- Order delivery horizon: most data-center orders deliver in 12-24+ months. Majority of orders come with DOWN PAYMENTS (commitment). No signs of over-ordering/stockpiling; confidence from customer capex-plan discussions; capacity allocation balanced across segments (not selling everything into DC); "say-do ratio" high — orders only taken with capacity booked.
- Capacity: ABB is NOT the limiting factor in the energy build-out — GAS TURBINES and LARGE POWER TRANSFORMERS are the bottlenecks, not switchgear/components. Example: Senatobia, Mississippi Smart Power factory DOUBLED in size vs 3 years ago, new lines ramping; also scaling via OEM partner network (partners build switchgear/do system integration on ABB's behalf). Higher capex guided this year.

MOTION:
- Record orders $2.5B (+9% comparable +3% M&A +6% FX); B2B 1.19; backlog $6.6B (~3 quarters of revenue). Food & beverage, HVAC, grid stabilization positive; process industries (chemicals, cement, mining) softer; rail timing dip.
- Revenue $2.1B (+7% comparable); op EBITA +11% but margin 18.5% (-110bps): Siemens Gamesa acquisition dilutes 70bps (~$60M revenue/qtr at a small loss; dilutive through 2026; plan to reach double-digit profitability in a couple of years), High Power division inefficiencies ~15bps (resolved by H2), adverse project mix. Q2: mid-to-high single-digit growth, margin down YoY.

PROCESS AUTOMATION:
- Orders +5% comparable; marine & ports strong; oil & gas down on tough comp (O&G ~9-10% of group revenue, ~2x that within PA); nuclear active; mining up; machine automation up from low level; chemicals/pulp&paper soft. Backlog $10.4B (~5 quarters of revenue). Margin 14.7% (+50bps) on cost control despite project mix.
- Launched "Automation Extended": evolution of DCS (world's largest installed base) — core control environment + digital/AI environment securely interconnected with external clouds; lets customers modernize 24/7 plants without disruption; tracked like ARR (service/upgrade revenue per customer); service margin accretive; pulls EL and Motion content ("power of ABB").

CAPITAL ALLOCATION:
- $2.1B dividend (~1.3% yield), up-to-$2B annual buyback (~1.2% mcap). Net debt/EBITDA 0.3. Comfortable levering to 2x EBITDA -> ~$13B firepower + ~$5B robotics divestment proceeds in H2 = ~$18B M&A capacity. Base case small/mid bolt-ons + somewhat bigger deals (Thomas & Betts/Baldor-scale ~$4B would be welcome). M&A focus areas: MORE DATA-CENTER SHARE, North America, utilities, grid automation/resilience. "Rather no deals than bad deals."
- Section 232 tariffs: limited exposure (content mostly below the 15% threshold).
