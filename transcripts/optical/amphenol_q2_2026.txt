[Craig Lampo — CFO]
Record sales $8.8B: +55% USD, +54% local currency, +30% ORGANIC. Sequential +15% USD (+13% organic). RECORD ORDERS $10.732B — +94% YoY (+63% ORGANICALLY), +14% QoQ — book-to-bill 1.23:1, POSITIVE in EVERY end market. GAAP op margin 29.5%; ADJUSTED OP MARGIN RECORD 29.8% (+420bps YoY, +250bps QoQ) — includes an $80M ($0.04) net IEEPA tariff-recovery benefit; ~29% excluding it; drivers = operating leverage on volume + CommScope profitability actions. Segments: COMMUNICATIONS SOLUTIONS $5.4B (+85% USD, +42% organic), op margin 33.6%; Harsh Environment $1.9B (+28%/+22%), 30.1%; Interconnect & Sensor Systems $1.5B (+17%/+13%), 21.0%. Adjusted EPS record $1.35 (+67% vs $0.81). OCF $1.6B; FCF $1.2B. Net debt $13.4B; net leverage 1.3x; EBITDA $3.0B. CommScope amortization of acquired backlog $24M.

[Adam Norwitt — CEO]
Beat high end of guidance. Two acquisitions closed: El.Com Srl (Italy, ~$150M sales — industrial/defense/comm-air interconnect + HV cable assemblies) and Wilder Technologies (WA, ~$15M — high-performance TEST AND MEASUREMENT for high-speed interconnect; was a key Amphenol supplier). COMMSCOPE UPGRADED: now expected to deliver $4.6 BILLION of 2026 sales and $0.30 of accretion — up from $4.1B and $0.15; running >20% op margin INCLUDING amortization after two quarters in the family; team 'just getting started.'

Markets: Defense 8% of sales (+24% organic; Q3 +low-DD). Commercial air 4% (+21% organic). Industrial 20% (+18% organic; all subsegments + all 3 geographies double-digit; CommScope building-connectivity added). Automotive 10% (+6% organic; EV-drivetrain pickup). Communications networks 11% (+55% USD on CommScope; ORGANIC -6% on operator/wireless moderation — CommScope itself GREW YoY here; Q3 -mid-teens QoQ). Mobile devices 4% (+14% organic; Q3 +~20% QoQ on new program launches; hinges/antennas/mechanisms leadership). IT DATACOM = 43% OF SALES: +89% USD, +63% ORGANIC, +22% SEQUENTIAL — 'virtually all of this growth was driven by sales of AI-RELATED PRODUCTS'; Q3 guided to a FURTHER +mid-teens sequential increase. CommScope's IT datacom business (ALL advanced optical interconnect) essentially DOUBLED YoY; IT datacom to be just under HALF of CommScope's 2026 sales (vs ~1/3 in 2025).

Q3 guide: sales $9.3-9.4B (+50-52%); adjusted EPS $1.40-1.42 (+51-53%); no further net tariff recoveries assumed.

[Q&A highlights]
- Steve Volkmann (Jefferies): CommScope strength broad-based; IT datacom = all optics, doubled YoY; benefiting from Amphenol's relationships 'up and down the stack' (DC builders, systems, chip architects).
- Joe Cardoso (JPM): CommScope accretion doubling = operational execution (factory + SG&A + vendors), not pricing; >20% op margin incl. amortization. Norwitt: a team whose potential was untapped under prior ownership; embraced the entrepreneurial culture.
- Amit Daryanani (Evercore): copper-vs-fiber debate — 'the one consistent thing we hear from customers is THEY NEED MORE OF EVERYTHING: more high-speed copper, more fiber-optic solutions, and more power solutions'; AI growing FASTER than the +63% IT-datacom organic; analyst frames the AI revenue RUN RATE AT $10.5-11B (not disputed); Amphenol = strong high-speed copper + world-leading optics scale (CommScope) + leading AI power interconnect.
- Luke Junk (Baird): POWER portfolio — decades-old franchise from board-level power to bus bars to complex power cables, 'deep in the rack connecting directly to the chip all the way to the switchgear'; trends: higher voltages, liquid cooling, SENSORS EMBEDDED IN CONNECTORS (smart power); framing: 'AI is the conversion of ELECTRONS INTO TOKENS — everywhere along that equation Amphenol is a partner.'
- Will Stein (Truist): cabled spine-backplane vs PCB conversion debate — ARCHITECTURE-AGNOSTIC: 'whether a backplane versus a cable versus a printed circuit board is not the core question. The core question is: is there MORE OVERALL CONTENT of interconnect? They're all moving in the direction of MORE content, not less' (Amphenol makes cable, PCBs, PCB assemblies, cable assemblies).
- Joe Giordano (TD Cowen): model-agnostic, training-vs-inference agnostic ('inference seems to grow at an even faster pace, requires an intensity of interconnect that is unprecedented'), geography-agnostic.
- Asiya Merchant (Citi): FIBER SUPPLY — CommScope 'has managed to secure all that they need, including fiber'; no constraint on results.
- Wamsi Mohan (BofA): CapEx at the high end of the normal 3-4%-of-sales range, MODESTLY ABOVE it in H2 given growth; GM-level capacity decisions by general managers.
- Guy Hardwick (Barclays): SCALE-UP — CommScope known as scale-out/scale-across (where optics lives today); 'to the extent customers are thinking about integrating optics within their SCALE-UP architectures, CommScope — especially given Amphenol's unique relationships across the AI ecosystem — has a GREAT SEAT AT THE TABLE and they are certainly part of that discussion in a very significant way'; hybrid architectures expected.
- Andrew Buscaglia (BNP): bottlenecks — 'we don't see any significant bottlenecks... else we wouldn't have produced 30% organic growth'; 150 decentralized GMs manage supply; company DOUBLED in size in two years (~1/3 acquisitions, ~2/3 organic).
- Mark Delaney (GS): mobile devices — hinges (2+ decades), antennas, mechanisms; wearables proliferation.
- Joe Spak (UBS): power ELECTRONICS (inverters/DC-DC) — deliberately NOT in active power management; interconnect-true strategy.
- Scott Graham (Seaport): industrial — instrumentation (semi build-out), factory automation, electrification/battery storage, heavy equipment, alt energy all strong; CommScope opens construction/building-connectivity + distribution into ~150-180 countries.
