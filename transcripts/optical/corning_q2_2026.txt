THE THREE TECHNICAL DRIVERS OF OPTICAL CONTENT PER GPU (enterprise MAP grows at GPU growth rate as the base case; these drivers add on top):
1. CLUSTER SIZE: clusters >130,000 GPUs exceed what a 512-radix switch can span in two layers -> a THIRD OPTICAL LAYER is required -> 3/2 = ~50% MORE CONTENT PER GPU for very large clusters (fast-growing segment of AI factories).
2. BANDWIDTH: GPU/ASIC bandwidth doubles ~every 2 years via lane rate x lane count. Hopper -> Blackwell: SerDes stayed 100G so fibers went 8 -> 16 (content DOUBLED). Rubin era: SerDes jumps to 200G, lane count constant -> NEUTRAL. Feynman (~2029-30, primary system timing): if SerDes stays 200G, fibers DOUBLE again; if 400G arrives, neutral; BiDi/WDM could reduce fiber count. Springboard ASSUMES NEUTRAL (conservative).
3. SCALE-UP (today 100% COPPER): NVIDIA Vera Rubin Ultra NVL576 = 8 racks x 72 Rubin Ultra GPUs — copper INSIDE the rack, DIRECT OPTICAL rack-to-rack = a HYBRID transition step; % of optical ports not public (confidential). BRACKET THE OPPORTUNITY: per-GPU scale-up bandwidth 14.4 Tbps / 200G SerDes = 72 lanes x 2 fibers = 144 fibers; scale-out 1.6 Tbps / 200G = 8 lanes x 2 = 16 fibers; FULL-OPTICAL SCALE-UP TOTAL = 160 FIBERS PER GPU = 10x TODAY'S 16-fiber scale-out-only content. Reality lands between 0% and 100% optical ports.
COMBINED: optical content per GPU in enterprise +1.3x-1.5x BY 2028, 'could head much, much higher' into 2030.

PHOTONICS MAP (new, $10B by 2030): optics move INSIDE THE BOX (CPO/NPO) for latency, faceplate density, power, reliability — light creation/modulation/delivery moves to the silicon-photonics optical engine, and Corning supplies the PASSIVE PHOTONICS to move/manage light (incl. FAU harnesses) where Corning historically had ZERO content. Likely starts with scale-out; SCALE-UP supercharges it; exponential adoption curve, timing hard to call — based on 'assumptions and discussions AND AGREEMENTS with customers.'
