Arista Networks (ANET) used Tuesday, 18 August 2026, at Rosenblatt’s 6th Annual Technology Summit: The Age of AI (Part II) to present a broadening AI networking story, with strong demand across customer groups and a fast-growing Etherlink portfolio. Management also pointed to near-term supply constraints, tougher competition and a scale-up market that is still early, even as the company keeps margin targets intact.

Arista did not report quarterly earnings in the call, but management gave a detailed update on growth and margins.

Management said the margin outlook reflects mix rather than pricing power. The company’s actual gross profit margin of 63% in the last twelve months aligns closely with its guided range, while its return on equity stands at 31%, reflecting strong profitability. With a market capitalization of $242.55 billion and revenue growth of 33% over the past year, Arista continues to scale rapidly. However, InvestingPro analysis suggests the stock is currently overvalued relative to its Fair Value, placing it among companies on the Most Overvalued list.

The strongest message from the call was the speed of Arista&rsquo;s expansion in AI networking, especially through Etherlink.

Chantelle Breithaupt, chief financial officer, said, &ldquo;We had this Etherlink portfolio June 2024, and I think it is just indicative of how we feel Arista was almost born for this AI moment. First, we had the cloud, and now we have AI.&rdquo;

She added that the broader customer mix shows the company can serve many AI use cases. &ldquo;The fact we have been able to go from those large customers to a breadth of customers, I think is super exciting and showing that for a pure play networking company, we can serve any of those use cases and continue to drive the innovation.&rdquo;

Management framed the AI opportunity around three networking layers.

Scale-out remains the base layer and, according to management, is where Arista has already proven itself with large backend training clusters. Brendan Gibbs, vice president of product line management, said, &ldquo;Before you can take a data center and an AI deployment and scale it across, you actually need to win first that back end scale-out cluster. Arista is one of the most successful vendors in deploying large scale multi-customer back end training clusters, period.&rdquo;

Scale-across is already a meaningful business line.

Scale-up is earlier, but management sees a large future market.

Arista also outlined a fast-moving product cycle across speeds and packaging approaches.

Management said XPO was invented by Arista and then opened through an open MSA, with more than 100 companies signing on.

Executives said Arista&rsquo;s EOS operating system remains a central part of the company&rsquo;s value proposition.

Gibbs said AI customers are moving quickly and need a stable software base. &ldquo;AI is essentially an arms race. All of these customers are racing to get to market, racing to sign up customers, racing to innovate at the frontier of models and cloud expansion and GPU as a service, et cetera. So having that software foundation for the network from us really gives them peace of mind.&rdquo;

Management said the recent growth is driven by demand, not by customers rushing to beat supply limits.

On supply, the tone was cautious but not alarmed.

Arista described enterprise and campus networking as a steady but important growth engine.

Management said capital spending remains focused on the business itself.

Breithaupt said, &ldquo;We&rsquo;re not distracted with other things. We&rsquo;re trying to prioritize. I think that&rsquo;s our responsibility and our excitement as a company.&rdquo;

During the question-and-answer session, executives returned to several recurring investor concerns.

Arista&rsquo;s message was that AI networking is still early, but the company believes it has a strong position across the main use cases. The near-term story is still scale-out and scale-across, while scale-up may become a larger business later in the decade.
