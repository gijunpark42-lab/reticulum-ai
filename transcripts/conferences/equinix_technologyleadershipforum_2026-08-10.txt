On Monday, 10 August 2026, Equinix (EQIX) used its Technology Leadership Forum 2026 to outline a more confident long-term view, backed by stronger demand from AI, cloud and enterprise customers. Management said the main challenge is not a lack of interest, but a shortage of available capacity, even as the company faces higher power needs, tighter supply chains and a talent crunch.

Equinix said its updated long-term outlook reflects deeper customer engagement and a stronger view of demand durability across its portfolio.

Ryan Burke, head of investor relations, said the company&rsquo;s guidance is based on both bottom-up and top-down work at the regional level. He said Equinix is seeing demand from traditional enterprise customers as well as newer AI-related users.

Burke said, &ldquo;We achieve mid 20% yields on our developments, which is high relative to the market for sure. A big part of that is just getting the mosaic right, property in and property out.&rdquo;

Management said capacity management is the top operational priority, especially in the Americas West region, where Guy Danskine recently took over as managing director.

Danskine said the company is using natural replacement cycles for equipment such as power distribution units and CRAH units, which usually refresh every 10 to 15 years. He said newer equipment can be twice as efficient or twice as capable in a smaller footprint, helping Equinix raise density without waiting for entirely new builds.

He also said the company is seeing customers become more flexible about location as capacity gets tighter. Enterprise users are splitting workloads across multiple facilities in the same metro area, while AI and cloud customers are changing how they think about where compute should sit.

Equinix spent much of the call on metro-level strategy, with Dallas emerging as the clearest example of how the company wants to grow.

Danskine said, &ldquo;For Equinix, I would think about it more like Dallas, not Texas. We have an incredible asset in the Infomart in Dallas that we acquired 7 or 8 years ago now.&rdquo;

Chicago was another重点 market, with strong financial services demand and a planned expansion to the Manuka hybrid xScale campus. Management said high-frequency trading firms still need downtown access, while back-office workloads can move to suburban sites.

Silicon Valley remains expensive to build and power, but Equinix said customer demand continues, especially from startups and new AI companies that want a visible presence in the market. Los Angeles, Seattle and Colorado were also cited as growth markets, with Los Angeles benefiting from media, content and subsea cable activity.

Interconnection was presented as one of Equinix&rsquo;s most important competitive advantages, especially as AI changes how customers use data centers.

Danskine said neocloud and LLM customers are increasingly focused on inference and distribution rather than training alone, which supports more networking activity around Equinix facilities. He said the company is intentionally building customer lists by metro to attract the network side of these businesses at minimum.

Management said there is often a lag between space and power deployment and the related interconnection revenue, which could support future growth as new capacity comes online.

Equinix said its strategy is built around a multi-year expansion cycle that combines asset optimization with selective new development.

Burke said the company remains focused on core co-location, enterprise serving and critical workloads rather than the broader data center market. He said that focus gives Equinix barriers to entry from both operations and new supply.

Danskine said the company&rsquo;s long customer conversations have increased confidence in the outlook. &ldquo;We&rsquo;ve spent a lot of time with our customers looking at the demand that we&rsquo;re all seeing and you&rsquo;re hearing about, but really thinking deeply and talking to those customers about the durability and how robust that demand is,&rdquo; he said.

He added that the company&rsquo;s demand view is supported by both enterprise customers and AI-related users, with the strongest pull coming from distributed computing and interconnection-heavy workloads.

Management also addressed the main execution risks, saying they remain manageable but important.

Management said power is largely under control for announced projects and that the company is not relying on immediate grid expansion to support its near-term plans. It also said Equinix is not creating sudden demand shocks for utilities, but rather working through long-term, predictable partnerships. InvestingPro rates the company’s overall financial health as "GOOD," with liquid assets exceeding short-term obligations despite a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.62.
