On Monday, 10 August 2026, Micron Technology (MU) used the KeyBanc Capital Markets Technology Leadership Forum 2026 to argue that artificial intelligence is reshaping memory demand faster than the industry can add supply. The company described a market with strong pricing and deep customer demand, but also persistent capacity limits that it expects will keep conditions tight well into 2027 and beyond.

Sumit Sadana, Micron&rsquo;s executive vice president and chief business officer, said the current cycle is being driven by a broad AI buildout that is still early in its life. He said the company is seeing stronger demand signals since its latest earnings report and now expects calendar year 2027 to be even tighter than 2026.

He said the market is being shaped by a structural gap between fast-growing digital demand and the slower pace of physical supply growth. In his view, the difference is especially clear in memory, where customers need more capacity, more bandwidth and more reliable supply than in prior cycles.

Micron did not present a full earnings update in the forum, but Sadana pointed to very strong recent performance and said the company is operating at high profitability levels.

Sadana said Micron is balancing customer enablement with returns on capital. He said the company still expects pricing opportunities ahead, while revenue and profit should also benefit from a better product mix and higher shipment volumes as supply expands. The company’s financial performance reflects this strategy, with revenue reaching $90.3 billion in the last twelve months and an impressive gross profit margin of 72.6%. According to InvestingPro analysis, the stock is trading near its Fair Value, suggesting the market has largely priced in the current strong fundamentals.

Micron said it is investing heavily in manufacturing and supply chain capacity, with a focus on both the United States and overseas operations.

The company said major projects include Idaho 1 and Idaho 2, which are expected to come online in the middle of next year and at the end of 2028, respectively. It also pointed to New York 1 and a cluster of additional New York fabs, along with Virginia investments tied to long-life-cycle technology and 1-alpha DRAM production.

Outside the U.S., Micron highlighted work across Japan, Taiwan, Singapore and India, especially in back-end manufacturing.

Sadana said demand is strong across data center, consumer and industrial markets, but the pressure is most acute in data center AI. He said many customers cannot meet more than half of their demand needs from Micron supply.

He also said customers are now making multi-year demand commitments even at elevated pricing levels. In his words, the number one constraint for many customers is not power, real estate, data center space or logic wafers. It is DRAM. The bullish sentiment extends to Wall Street, where InvestingPro data shows 27 analysts have revised their earnings estimates upward for the upcoming period. With a market capitalization of $992 billion and a one-year price return of 639%, Micron has become a prominent player in the semiconductor industry. Investors seeking deeper insights can access 15+ additional ProTips on the platform.

Sadana said the company&rsquo;s customer relationships are now deeper than at any point in his career. He said Micron has visibility into customer roadmaps beyond 2030 through engineering partnerships that go well beyond a normal supplier relationship.

Micron said its product portfolio is helping it win business in a market where performance, power efficiency and supply security matter more than before.

Sadana said memory bandwidth and capacity are now central to system performance. He said processors often sit idle about 50% of the time while waiting for data, which makes memory a key constraint in AI systems.

Micron said its Strategic Customer Agreements, or SCAs, are becoming a major part of its business model and customer strategy.

Sadana said the company had $22 billion in cash and cash-like commitments tied to the agreements at the time of its earnings announcement, with $18 billion in cash on the balance sheet. He said the structure is likely to become evergreen, with customers able to extend deals by adding years to the back end.

He added that the agreements are not just about volume and pricing. They also support deeper collaboration on product roadmaps and research and development.

Micron said it sees a long period of tight supply ahead, with no clear sign of when supply will catch up to demand.

Sadana said robots may need hundreds of gigabytes of DRAM and terabytes of SSD storage per unit. He said the robotics market is still in its infancy, but could grow quickly later this decade and then accelerate further in the early 2030s.

He also said customers are increasingly treating memory as a strategic asset. In his view, the market is moving toward single-source or dual-source arrangements, similar to the way ASIC programs are structured in some parts of the chip industry.
