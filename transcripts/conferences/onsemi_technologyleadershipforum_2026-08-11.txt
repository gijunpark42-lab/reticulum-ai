Tuesday, 11 August 2026 &mdash; onsemi (ON) used the Technology Leadership Forum 2026 to outline a business that is gaining scale in AI data center power systems while also managing a temporary shift in capacity away from automotive and industrial demand. Management said the company is seeing stronger margins, better visibility and a larger strategic role in power conversion, even as it works through pricing pressure and integrates a major acquisition.

Chief Executive Hassane El-Khoury and Chief Financial Officer Thad Trent said onsemi&rsquo;s recent performance reflects a stronger operating base and a better mix of demand. The company&rsquo;s AI data center revenue more than doubled from $250 million in the prior year to more than $500 million this year, topping the company&rsquo;s earlier expectation for a simple doubling.

Trading at a market capitalization of $31.5 billion with a P/E ratio of 52.19, onsemi currently trades below its InvestingPro Fair Value estimate, suggesting the stock may be undervalued despite its strong performance. The company said several factors should support gross margin over the next 6 to 12 months:

Management also said input cost pressure is being addressed through two rounds of price increases, with the first on April 1 and a second round now in progress. Full pricing benefit is expected to take several quarters to work through the business.

El-Khoury said onsemi temporarily prioritized data center demand over automotive and industrial orders, but described the move as an operational adjustment rather than a long-term strategy. He said the company is using the flexibility of its manufacturing system to redirect capacity where demand is strongest.

He explained that capacity is fungible at the fab and technology level until about two weeks before a product is complete and receives its marketing part number. That gives the company room to shift in-process wafers between end markets, even though automotive and industrial orders can carry lead times of more than 30 weeks.

&ldquo;When you get orders in short time and you have to make priority calls, as long as those priority calls are on a 2-week. If it is packaged and it is kind of ready to go, you cannot just change the mailing address. That is the flexibility. Compare that 2-week change in end product to a little over 30 weeks of lead time, we are able to make those decisions while we start those new wafers for the auto and industrial orders that we got,&rdquo; El-Khoury said.

The company also said automotive demand has not shown a meaningful restocking cycle, even as sales volumes are flat or slightly lower. Instead, some original equipment makers are buying directly from onsemi rather than through tier-one suppliers, reflecting concerns about inventory levels and balance-sheet pressure at the supplier level.

On technology, the company said its vertical gallium nitride program is moving ahead. The high-voltage version is a monolithic 1,200-volt device, not a stacked-die design, and has already been sampled to AI data center and automotive customers. Qualification is progressing as expected, and management said yield and performance have met internal expectations.

onsemi also said it is participating across the full AI data center power tree, from high voltage to medium and low voltage, across customers and platforms including Flex Power, Delta, Lite-On, Great Wall, AWS and Nvidia. Management said that breadth mirrors the company&rsquo;s approach in automotive, where diversification across customers helped build scale.

Management spent much of the call on the strategic case for the Synaptics acquisition, which it framed as a move to build an AI-first compute platform through the Astra product line. El-Khoury said the deal is not a response to weakness in the core business, but an expansion from a position of strength.

&ldquo;We&rsquo;re coming at it from a position of strength. We have proven the strength of our core business, not just in the reported quarter, but in our outlook. We have proven that it&rsquo;s the right asset... we&rsquo;re getting an AI first franchise. All of these make the strategic deal strategic,&rdquo; he said.

Trent said the transaction is expected to be accretive to gross margin and cash flow positive. Management said it expects $200 million in synergies within 18 months of closing, with additional upside from manufacturing integration, distribution leverage and revenue growth beyond that period.

Trent said, &ldquo;It&rsquo;s accretive to our gross margin target today. We believe we can take our scale and leverage that platform. So we can take our distribution network, our thousands of customers, and we can take that product broad very quickly, which is something they&rsquo;ve struggled with.&rdquo;

Management said the next several quarters should bring continued margin expansion as utilization gains flow through the income statement and as pricing actions take hold. The company also said customer visibility has improved materially, extending into 2027 and, in some cases, into early 2028 through backlog commitments.

El-Khoury said the broader market is unusual because several secular growth areas are converging at once. He pointed to AI data centers, automotive electrification and industrial energy storage, all of which are measured in gigawatts and rely on power conversion.

&ldquo;The fact that every secular growth market in every end market that matters is being measured in a unit of power is unique. It puts us in a much unique and a favorable market, because that is what we do. We do power, and we do power conversion, translating that gigawatt overall into whatever end use cases,&rdquo; he said.

In questions from analysts, management returned to the issue of manufacturing flexibility. El-Khoury said finished goods are not interchangeable, since each product is built for a specific board or application. But he stressed that the company can still redirect wafers and work in process across end markets before the final two-week completion window.

That flexibility, he said, matters because new wafers for automotive and industrial orders can be started while the company serves faster-moving data center demand. Management said this is one reason onsemi can respond to short-term shifts without changing its long-term strategy.

Analysts also pressed management on the lack of an automotive restocking cycle. Trent and El-Khoury said the cycle looks different from past ones because some OEMs are now buying directly from onsemi rather than relying on tier-one suppliers. They said that trend is still limited to EV makers and certain disruptors, but it could become more common if traditional automakers draw lessons from the current cycle.

Questions about the Synaptics deal focused on whether the acquisition reflected weakness in the core business. Management rejected that view and said the company&rsquo;s recent results and outlook show strength in the base business. They also said the new Synaptics business is different from the legacy perception of the company and fits onsemi&rsquo;s push into AI-first compute.

onsemi&rsquo;s message at the forum was that power semiconductors are becoming more central as data centers, vehicles and industrial systems consume more electricity and require more efficient conversion. The company said it is positioned to benefit from that shift through manufacturing flexibility, higher utilization, new technology and the Synaptics acquisition.
