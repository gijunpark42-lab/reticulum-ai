On Monday, 17 August 2026, Penguin Solutions (PENG) used Rosenblatt’s 6th Annual Technology Summit: The Age of AI (Part II) to outline a broader shift from high-performance computing hardware toward a full-stack AI factory platform. Management said the company is benefiting from years of cluster experience and a growing software and services mix, while also facing tougher competition, enterprise adoption hurdles and the challenge of proving the value of owning AI infrastructure versus renting it.

Rosenblatt Securities maintained a buy rating on Penguin Solutions and an $80 12-month price target. The stock closed the regular session at $63.08, down 1.13% from the prior close of $63.80. In after-hours trading, the shares were unchanged at $63.08.

The platform notes that six analysts have recently revised their earnings upwards for the upcoming period, with net income expected to grow this year. Mark Adams, senior vice president of global marketing, said Penguin&rsquo;s history goes back more than 25 years, including early work on NASA&rsquo;s Beowulf cluster efforts. He said the company was recently inducted into the Space Technology Hall of Fame, underscoring its heritage in scalable parallel computing.

That background, he said, now supports Penguin&rsquo;s push into AI infrastructure. The company described itself as operating at the intersection of advanced memory systems and AI factories, with a focus on workloads that need specialized infrastructure, software and operational support.

Penguin highlighted four main offerings that it says form the base of its platform strategy.

Penguin said its business is organized around three main customer groups, each with different needs.

Management said Penguin tries to stay technology-agnostic while still being opinionated on behalf of the customer. That means it will work with multiple silicon and system options if they fit the workload.

Adams said Penguin&rsquo;s differentiation comes from combining deployment, monitoring and automatic remediation, while many competitors address only one or two of those areas. He also pointed to open-source tools such as Slurm and Kubernetes, saying they can help with basic setup but often do not provide the tuning and performance optimization needed for complex AI systems.

Penguin said its longer-term plan is to move further up the software and services stack, especially in Kubernetes, AI orchestration and application development. The company wants to keep the infrastructure layer strong, but it sees more value in helping customers build purpose-built applications for specific workloads.

For investors, the key question is whether Penguin can keep turning its infrastructure expertise into recurring software and services revenue. Rosenblatt&rsquo;s view suggests confidence that the company is still early in that transition.
