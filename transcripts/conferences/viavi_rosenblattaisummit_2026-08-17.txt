On Monday, 17 August 2026, Viavi Solutions (VIAV) used Rosenblatt’s 6th Annual Technology Summit: The Age of AI (Part II) to outline a business that is gaining momentum in data center testing and aerospace and defense, even as some of its newest markets remain early in development. Management said the company is benefiting from the shift to faster optical networks and resilient defense timing systems, while also facing the usual risks of long adoption cycles and changing customer spending patterns.

Viavi said its fiscal fourth quarter of 2026, the June quarter, showed continued strength in the Network and Software Enablement segment, or NSE, with the mix shifting toward faster-growing end markets.

The company said the business is benefiting from operating leverage as revenue rises. Management said a $500 million quarterly revenue run rate could support operating margins in the high-40% range, compared with prior guidance of about 30%. The company’s revenue surged 40% in the last twelve months to $1.52 billion, while maintaining a robust gross profit margin of 61%. Despite current valuation concerns&mdash;InvestingPro analysis suggests the stock is trading above its Fair Value&mdash;analysts forecast earnings of $1.81 per share for fiscal 2027.

Net income also benefits from a near-zero incremental tax rate, which management attributed to the company&rsquo;s U.S. tax structure and substantial net operating loss carryforwards inherited from the Lumentum separation. Viavi also said its balance sheet is strong after a recent equity offering and the payoff of a term loan.

Other financial and segment points included:

Much of the discussion focused on optical testing, where Viavi said the move from 400G to 800G and then to 1.6T is changing both the complexity of testing and the amount of content the company can sell.

CEO Oleg Khaykin said the shift is not just a speed upgrade but a new testing model. &ldquo;As you go to 1.6T, it is a whole new ballgame,&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;You are getting much more optical, and you have a more extensive test, and now you are actually doing a lot more functional test on the line. So the content for test is actually increasing.&rdquo;

Management said 1.6T equipment carries about a 50% average selling price uplift versus 800G equipment, largely because the components are more expensive and the test requirements are broader. Viavi said it has been selling 1.6T systems for more than a year, mainly to developers and system vendors, with early production beginning in early 2026. The company described adoption as being in the second to third inning, depending on the customer group.

Viavi also said its 1.6T platform is backward compatible, allowing customers to test 400G, 800G and 1.6T modules on the same equipment. Management said that improves economics on a cost-per-bit basis, even if the box itself is more expensive.

Beyond transceivers, Viavi said it is expanding into co-packaged optics and network package optics, or CPO and NPO, where it competes more directly with semiconductor test companies such as Advantest, Teradyne and FormFactor.

On the optical circuit switch market, Viavi said it is the dominant merchant equipment supplier and sees a significant content opportunity. Management pointed to Lumentum guidance suggesting the OCS market could rise from a current quarterly run rate of about $100 million to more than $200 million within several quarters. Viavi estimates that test and measurement content could represent about 10% of the total addressable market.

The company also said it is seeing growth in new fiber testing markets, including hollow-core, multi-core and multi-ribbon cables. Those technologies require interferometry-based surface quality analysis because high-speed connectors are expensive and customers are highly cost sensitive.

In field instrumentation, Viavi said hyperscalers are increasingly using edge-based fiber monitoring and qualification across entire network build-outs. Management said this is helping avoid failures in dark fiber deployments. Khaykin compared the issue to buying a network that may not work when turned on. &ldquo;If you are a data center hyperscaler, buying a fiber network ended up being like a box of chocolates,&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;Sometimes the fiber worked with buying a lot of dark fiber. You do not use it, but when you turn it on, you find it does not work.&rdquo;

Management said it remains focused on reaching a $500 million quarterly revenue run rate, and suggested that target may arrive in the December 2026 quarter or early 2027, earlier than the company&rsquo;s previous fiscal 2028 framing if demand stays strong.

Viavi said CPO and NPO represent an important long-term opportunity because they require more test content per port than traditional pluggable transceivers. Management said it expects a higher share in CPO because of the company&rsquo;s integrated platform approach and broad module lineup.

The company also sees the optical circuit switch market as a meaningful growth driver over the next several quarters, with the possibility of a larger merchant market as AI data center architectures evolve.

Looking further out, management said the next major growth wave after data center may come from wireless and radio access network spending, or RAN, as AI infrastructure pushes demand for zero-latency connectivity at the edge. That opportunity is still estimated to be two to three years away.

In aerospace and defense, Viavi expects continued growth through its design-win model, which creates multi-year revenue streams rather than one-time sales. Management said the business has more than doubled over the past two years and is growing at a pace that is higher than telecom and more sustainable than data center.

The company highlighted resilient positioning, navigation and timing, or PNT, as a key defense theme. That technology is aimed at reducing reliance on GPS in military applications, including drones, intelligent munitions and autonomous vehicles.

On the OSP side, Viavi said it expects steady low single-digit growth, with opportunities in 3D sensing, autonomous driving, especially in China, and advanced photonics integration.

For mergers and acquisitions, management said it prefers deals with immediate strategic value and clear economics. Khaykin said the company walked away from a recent transaction where a competitor paid about twice Viavi&rsquo;s valuation. &ldquo;We prefer you pay me today and I will give you hamburger on Tuesday,&rdquo; he said, underscoring a focus on tangible near-term returns rather than speculative synergies.

During questions, management gave more detail on market share, timing and product economics.

On CPO and NPO, management said NPO demand should precede CPO by about one quarter, based on customer ordering patterns and capacity use. Viavi said it has been building capacity ahead of demand, with typical deployment at 60% to 70% utilization before adding more.

Management also explained why CPO testing is more complex than testing pluggable modules.

On resilient PNT, Viavi said the goal is to address GPS vulnerabilities that matter in military systems. The company said the need is growing as drones, intelligent munitions and autonomous platforms become more common.

Management also said the field instrumentation business is benefiting from hyperscaler spending on fiber monitoring. The company said customers want to qualify networks at the edge before deployment, rather than discover problems after installation.

On wireless, Viavi said the opportunity is still a few years away but could be large. Management said AI inference at the edge will require zero-latency connectivity, which should support a new wave of RAN spending after the current data center cycle matures. The company’s growth prospects have caught Wall Street’s attention, with seven analysts recently revising their earnings estimates upward, according to InvestingPro data. Finally, on acquisitions, Viavi said it favors disciplined pricing and deals that can add value quickly. Management pointed to prior acquisitions such as Cobham, Spirent and smaller transactions that produced outsized results.
