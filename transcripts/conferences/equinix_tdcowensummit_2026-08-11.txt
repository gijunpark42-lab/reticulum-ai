On Tuesday, 11 August 2026, Equinix (EQIX) used the TD Cowen 12th Annual Communications Infrastructure Summit to outline a fast-growing but harder-to-execute expansion plan. The data-center operator said demand from AI and cloud customers remains strong, yet power, labor and supply-chain limits are making new capacity slower and more expensive to deliver.

Raouf Abdel, Equinix&rsquo;s executive vice president of global operations, said the company is planning years ahead as it expands across major markets. He described a business that is selling more capacity before projects are finished, while also dealing with a much more complex development process than in the past.

Equinix said its capital spending plan has moved sharply higher as it builds for future demand. Abdel said the company&rsquo;s updated CapEx guidance of $5 billion to $7 billion reflects the scale of the opportunity and the cost of securing capacity in a tighter market.

Abdel said power infrastructure now carries a much larger price tag than it once did. He said it can cost tens of millions of dollars to deliver power to a site, compared with little or no cost in earlier years.

The aggressive expansion comes as Equinix, with a market capitalization of $102 billion, trades at a P/E ratio of 67. The company also said pre-sales are at record levels, which means customers are committing to space before projects are complete. That has increased pressure to deliver on time and has made project execution more urgent.

Abdel said Equinix&rsquo;s role has expanded beyond operating data centers. The company now manages the full development cycle, including real estate, energy procurement, design, construction and operations.

Abdel said the company has become more disciplined about project timing. In the past, teams could allow more schedule &ldquo;float&rdquo; early in a project. Now, he said, Equinix manages that slack from the start to reduce delays later.

He also said the portfolio gives the company flexibility. With more than 100 projects in the pipeline, Equinix can shift resources toward sites where power is confirmed and delivery looks more certain.

As a prominent player in the Specialized REITs industry, Equinix has delivered revenue growth of nearly 11% over the last twelve months, generating $9.9 billion in total revenue. Power was the central constraint in the discussion. Abdel said Equinix has changed its site-selection process so that it now seeks a clear path to power before buying land.

He said this shift reflects the reality of the market, where utilities and local infrastructure can take years to catch up with demand. The company&rsquo;s approach is now built around long-term planning and early coordination with utilities.

Abdel said Equinix is comfortable with the strategy because it has already banked land and power in advance for future delivery. &ldquo;We&rsquo;re planning today what we&rsquo;re going to deliver in 2028,&rdquo; he said, adding that the company is already working on 2028 and 2029 capacity.

Equinix said its new facilities are being built for higher-density workloads, especially those tied to AI. Abdel said the company&rsquo;s standard new-build template is a 60 megawatt block size.

Abdel said cooling is now being planned on a one-for-one basis with power. In other words, if a site has 5 megawatts of power, it needs 5 megawatts of cooling capacity.

He added that not every workload needs to go into the newest buildings. Existing data centers are not fully drawn down, which gives Equinix room to place higher-density customers in the right locations. When customers churn out, the company can often backfill space with more demanding workloads.

Construction labor was another major theme. Abdel said the company faces shortages in trades such as electricians and plumbers, especially in markets with heavy data-center activity.

Abdel said the industry is responding by using more prefabrication and offsite work to reduce the amount of labor needed on site. He said the wage pressure is forcing companies to rethink how they build.

He also said the broader resource picture has become more difficult. &ldquo;Our world has gotten a lot harder,&rdquo; he said, pointing to people, manufacturing, real estate and energy as the main areas of strain.

Despite the constraints, Abdel said Equinix remains confident in its ability to deliver the planned capacity. He said the company expects to &ldquo;glide into&rdquo; delivery of 14,000 to 15,000 cabinets per quarter.

Abdel said the company is not expecting the power problem to go away. Instead, it is building a process that can deal with long lead times and shifting priorities. That includes choosing among more than 100 projects based on where power is most likely to arrive on schedule.

Despite the elevated capital spending, Equinix maintains a current ratio of 1.13, indicating liquid assets exceed short-term obligations. The company carries a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.62 as it funds expansion. He said older data centers, some 10 to 20 years old, will continue to serve as cash-generating assets, with selective upgrades rather than full rebuilds.

During the discussion, Abdel returned several times to the scale of the buildout and the way it has changed Equinix&rsquo;s operating model.

He also said the company&rsquo;s tighter link between bookings and capacity has changed how it manages projects. Because customers are pre-buying more space, delays are less acceptable and execution discipline matters more.

Abdel said the company&rsquo;s portfolio gives it room to move. If one site faces delays, another may be able to move ahead if power is available. That flexibility, he said, is important in a market where demand is high but infrastructure is uneven.

For now, Equinix is betting that its larger land bank, stronger utility planning and more flexible design standards will let it keep pace with demand. The company&rsquo;s message at the summit was clear: growth is still there, but so are the obstacles.
