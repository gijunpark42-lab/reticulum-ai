Tuesday, 11 August 2026 &mdash; AMD said at the Technology Leadership Forum 2026 that its server and data center businesses are entering a new growth phase, helped by rising demand for AI inference and agentic computing. The company described strong customer interest and major product progress, but it also said supply chain capacity remains the main near-term constraint.

AMD Corporate Vice President of Financial Strategy and Investor Relations Matt Ramsay said the company&rsquo;s server business has been growing at a pace that was difficult to imagine a year ago.

Ramsay also said the company&rsquo;s early view of 2027 server revenue is roughly 20% larger than the whole server market was in 2025. He said AMD&rsquo;s x86 server market share has risen from 0.4% to the high 40% range.

The company said it is aiming for more than 50% share of an expanded server CPU total addressable market of $220 billion by 2030. AMD said agentic workloads will account for about two-thirds of that market.

AMD spent much of the presentation on Helios, its AI rack system, and on Venice, its next server CPU platform.

Ramsay said customer feedback on Helios has been &ldquo;phenomenal.&rdquo; He said sampled systems are running full model code and performing as promised. He added that AMD has overcome the major technical hurdles and is now focused on execution and scale.

On Venice, AMD said the product is sampling to all customers and will ship broadly across server markets. The chip supports up to 256 cores and 512 threads, and it will be used both in Helios racks and across the wider server portfolio. AMD also said a Florence CPU lineup is planned for 2028.

AMD said supply chain capacity is the main near-term challenge, especially at TSMC and in advanced packaging.

Ramsay said AMD is still pursuing additional manufacturing capacity through the rest of 2024. He said the company is more comfortable with longer lead times for 2027 and 2028 than with near-term supply needs, because the longer horizon gives it more room to adjust. With a market capitalization of $761 billion and analyst consensus rating of strong buy, AMD’s supply chain execution will be critical to maintaining investor confidence. AMD described its ROCm AI software stack as one of the biggest improvements in the company&rsquo;s recent product cycle.

Ramsay said the software team has made &ldquo;phenomenal&rdquo; progress over the last 18 months, with faster gains in the last 6 to 9 months as AI tools helped improve code generation and optimization. He said software friction has been removed for major customers doing advanced model work.

AMD&rsquo;s long-term message was that the company sees a much larger market than it did before, and that its product lineup is built to serve several different kinds of AI and server workloads.

Ramsay said agentic computing is not one workload but many, and that the right performance target is often agents per megawatt or threads per megawatt. He said AMD&rsquo;s architecture is suited to serving a wide range of optimization points, from medium-core-count systems to the highest-core-count racks.

AMD also said its product roadmap continues beyond Venice. Florence is planned for 2028, and Ravenna is also in development with close customer involvement. The company said it expects to keep refreshing ROCm with each new GPU generation.

During questions, Ramsay returned to three main themes: supply, customer validation and execution risk.

Ramsay said AMD is taking a methodical approach to Helios, starting with select ODM partners and making sure racks are shipping with customer code already running. He said the company has not found any major design &ldquo;smoking guns&rdquo; and now sees the work as largely operational.

On software, he said Anthropic&rsquo;s work with ROCm showed that the platform is now good enough for sophisticated model development and deployment. He said the company no longer hears the same software objections from top customers that it did in the past.

AMD framed its progress as part of a broader shift in AI computing, away from training-heavy systems and toward inference and agentic workloads. The company said this shift favors flexible server CPUs and rack-scale systems.

Ramsay said AMD&rsquo;s goal is to build the best server parts for the job, regardless of whether the discussion is about x86 or Arm. He said the company&rsquo;s long experience with enterprise and hyperscaler customers gives it an edge as AI infrastructure becomes more varied.
