=== SATYA KUMAR (CVP, Investor Relations and Treasury) ===
Welcome to Micron Technology's fiscal third-quarter (Q3) 2026 conference call. On the call: Sanjay Mehrotra (chairman, president and CEO) and Mark Murphy (CFO). Today's discussion of financial results is presented on a non-GAAP basis unless otherwise specified.

=== SANJAY MEHROTRA (Chairman, President and CEO) ===

Opening:
Micron delivered an exceptional fiscal Q3, with significant records in revenue, gross margin and EPS — all exceeding the high end of our guidance. Data center revenue exceeded $25 billion in fiscal Q3, an annualized run rate of over $100 billion. Data center SSD revenue exceeded $5 billion, more than doubling sequentially. DRAM and NAND industry demand continues to significantly exceed industry supply. We expect tight conditions to persist beyond calendar 2027 as a result of AI-driven demand across all segments coupled with structural supply constraints. We have now signed 16 strategic customer agreements (SCAs), which we expect will fundamentally transform our business model.

Industry trends:
The memory industry has been structurally transformed by the proliferation of AI. Data center-driven growth will be increasingly complemented by AI-enabled features in smartphones, high-end PCs and new consumer devices, as well as automotive, industrial applications and robotics. Even as we expect industry supply to improve gradually in 2028, we currently do not have line of sight as to when memory supply will be able to catch up with increasing demand. Memory industry supply growth is dependent on significant greenfield fab expansions. HBM's growth and increasing trade ratio with every new generation further pressures non-HBM supply. In NAND, industry suppliers redirecting cleanroom space from NAND to DRAM and overall limited cleanroom space constrain NAND bit supply growth. AI systems are powered by GPU, ASIC and CPU designs from an increasingly broad set of suppliers, but all share one characteristic — AI system performance is architecturally dependent on memory subsystem performance and capacity.

Business model transformation and strategic customer agreements (SCAs):
We have completed 16 SCAs with customers across the data center, consumer and auto market segments. Typically these agreements have a five-year term, from calendar 2026 through the end of calendar 2030 (automotive agreements generally three years). The 16 signed agreements represent roughly 20% of our DRAM volume and a third of our NAND volume over this period. These SCAs include four very large customers and three medium-sized customers; the remaining agreements relate to smaller automotive customers. When completed, we expect approximately half or more of company revenue to be under these SCAs. These SCAs are structured as take-or-pay agreements, with binding commitments to purchase specific volumes. The largest agreements generally have a ceiling price for existing products at the current CQ2 (calendar Q2) market price, and a floor price through the term. When all planned SCAs are executed, agreements with either fixed prices or price ceilings at or close to current CQ2 market prices are expected to be approximately 40% of revenue. For SCAs with price bands, the floor price enables a very robust gross margin for Micron, well above our peak quarterly margins in any past cycle. Fourteen of the 16 SCAs have a cumulative revenue at minimum price of approximately $100 billion over the remaining agreement term. Under the SCAs signed so far, we project to receive cash deposits and related financial commitments of $22 billion. They provide committed DRAM, including HBM as appropriate, and NAND supply over a multi-year horizon.

Technology and product leadership:
Our 1γ (1-gamma) DRAM node and G9 NAND node are both ramping well and on track to become the highest-volume nodes in Micron's history. Next-generation DRAM and NAND nodes are on track to begin volume production in the second half of calendar 2027. HBM4 12-high volume ramp is tracking twice as fast as HBM3E 12-high, and we have already shipped over $1 billion in HBM4 revenue. We expect to reach mature yields on HBM4 12-high significantly faster than HBM3E 12-high. We expect future memory demand to skew toward higher performance, higher value products (LP5→LP6, DDR5→DDR6, newer HBM), raising blended DRAM cost per bit.

End markets:
Data center — AI is driving unprecedented growth; industry data center DRAM and NAND bit shipments in calendar 2026 expected to more than double from two years ago. Calendar 2026 industry server units now expected to grow high-teens percent (above prior low-double-digit view). PC and mobile — industry revenue expected to grow despite unit volume declines (high-end mix). Automotive and robotics — L2+ and above vehicles have over five times the memory/storage content of an average vehicle; humanoid robots carry 10x the memory of an average L2+ vehicle.

Market outlook:
Supply-demand conditions for both DRAM and NAND to remain tight beyond calendar 2027. Calendar 2026 industry DRAM bit shipments to grow low- to mid-20s percent; NAND bit shipments to grow approximately 20%. Micron DRAM supply to grow approximately in line with industry; Micron NAND supply somewhat less than industry in calendar 2026.

Micron supply efforts:
Recently concluded a multi-year EUV supply agreement with ASML, supporting increased EUV adoption at the 1δ (1-delta) node and future generations. U.S. leading-edge DRAM manufacturing: ID1 and ID2 fabs in Idaho (ID1 first wafer output mid-calendar 2027, ID2 late calendar 2028); first New York fab broke ground January 2026; launched first production starts of 1α (1-alpha) DDR4 in Manassas, Virginia. Newly acquired Tongluo site in Taiwan: meaningful product shipments from existing 300,000-sq-ft fab in mid-calendar 2027 (a quarter earlier than prior); second cleanroom (EUV) under construction. Singapore site will become another center of excellence for advanced packaging, contributing meaningfully to Micron's HBM packaging capacity beginning first half of calendar 2027.

=== MARK MURPHY (EVP and CFO) ===

Opening:
Micron delivered exceptional fiscal Q3 results, with revenue, gross margin and EPS exceeding the high end of guidance.

Strategic customer agreements:
For SCAs with defined price (fixed or floor/ceiling), we are disclosing remaining performance obligations (RPO) starting this May quarter. RPO at end of fiscal Q3 was over $5 billion. For all SCAs entered into so far, including ones executed after the end of FQ3, RPO is approximately $100 billion (minimum committed volumes and minimum pricing; conservative; revenue expected to well exceed RPO). We project cash deposits and related financial commitments of $22 billion; the overwhelming majority (~$18 billion) will be cash deposits. These deposits show up on the balance sheet more in FQ4, appear in financing-related cash flows, and will not affect free cash flow; cash returned to customers toward the latter half of the term.

Revenue:
Total fiscal Q3 revenue was $41.5 billion, up 74% sequentially and up 346% year over year — fifth consecutive quarterly revenue record. The $17.6 billion sequential increase is the largest in our history, eclipsing last quarter's $10.2 billion record.

DRAM:
Fiscal Q3 DRAM revenue was a record $31.3 billion, up 343% year over year, representing 76% of total revenue. Sequentially DRAM revenue increased 67%. Bit shipments up low-single-digit percentage range. Prices increased in the low-60s percentage range.

NAND:
Fiscal Q3 NAND revenue was a record $9.9 billion, up 361% year over year, representing 24% of total revenue. Sequentially NAND revenue increased 99%. Bit shipments increased mid-single-digit percentage range. Prices increased in the mid-80s percentage range.

Gross margin:
Consolidated gross margin for fiscal Q3 was 84.9%, up 10 percentage points sequentially. More than doubled from a year ago; a new company record.

Financial performance by business unit:
- Cloud Memory Business Unit (CMBU): record $13.8 billion, 33% of total; up 78% sequentially; gross margins 83%.
- Core Data Center Business Unit (CDBU): record $11.5 billion, 28% of total; up 103% sequentially; gross margins 87%.
- Mobile and Client Business Unit (MCBU): record $11.5 billion, 28% of total; up 49% sequentially; gross margins 87%.
- Automotive and Embedded Business Unit (AEBU): record $4.6 billion, 11% of total; up 71% sequentially; gross margins 79%.

Opex / operating income / taxes / EPS:
Operating expenses $1.5 billion (up $97 million QoQ). Operating income $33.7 billion, operating margin 81.2% (up 12 pts QoQ, 54 pts YoY). Taxes $5.1 billion, effective tax rate 14.9%. Non-GAAP diluted EPS $25.11, up 106% sequentially.

Cash flow / balance sheet:
Operating cash flows $25.4 billion; capital expenditures $7.1 billion; free cash flow $18.3 billion (quarterly record). Ending inventory $8.6 billion, 120 days of inventory (DRAM below 120 days). Record cash and investments of $30.2 billion at quarter-end; reduced debt by $4.4 billion; closed with $5.7 billion debt and net cash of $24.4 billion; upgraded to BBB+.

Guidance (FQ4 2026):
Revenue a record $50.0 billion ± $1.0 billion; gross margin approximately 86.0% (reflects meaningful moderation in rate of price increases); operating expenses approximately $1.65 billion; share count approximately 1.15 billion; EPS a record $31.00 per share ± $1.00. FY2027 opex to increase by approximately $1 billion. FQ4 and FY2026 tax rate around 15.0%. FQ4 capex around $10 billion, bringing full-year fiscal 2026 capital spending to approximately $27 billion; FY2027 quarterly capex above FQ4 levels. From Dec. 9, 2026 (second anniversary of definitive CHIPS agreements) intend to increase capital return; over time return 100% of excess cash to shareholders.

=== SANJAY MEHROTRA (Closing) ===
AI has elevated the value of memory. Strategic customer agreements are ushering in an exciting era for Micron, expected to significantly enhance the durability and predictability of strong financial performance.
