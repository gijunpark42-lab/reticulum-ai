Samsung Electronics 2026 First Quarter Financial Results Conference Call
Date: April 30, 2026
Source: Samsung Electronics IR (samsung.com/global/ir)

=== CFO Soon-Cheol Park — Q1 2026 financial results & outlook ===
- Highest-ever quarterly revenue and operating profit, driven by AI tech innovation and proactive market response.
- Total revenue: record KRW 134 trillion (+43% QoQ).
- Operating profit: all-time high KRW 57 trillion (+185% QoQ); operating margin expanded from 21% to 43%.
- Net profit: KRW 47 trillion (2.4x previous quarter). EPS KRW 7,123 (common), KRW 7,124 (preferred).
- DS Division: revenue up QoQ on stronger high-value AI product sales and higher ASPs. Memory posted its second straight quarterly earnings record. Operating profit rose sharply led by memory; system semiconductor improved on flagship SoC sales.
- DX Division: revenue up QoQ on new flagship smartphone launches; limited profit decline despite higher costs.
- FX: USD and major currency appreciation had ~KRW 1.8T positive QoQ impact on operating profit, mainly in components.
- Q2 outlook: semiconductor demand expected strong; memory price to stay on upward trend driven by AI infrastructure expansion. Memory to increase HBM4, high-density DDR5, and eSSD sales. System LSI offsets pressure via volume project with key customer. Foundry drives improvement via advanced processes and 2nm orders.
- 2H outlook: mixed — semiconductor demand growth from AI vs rising IT product costs; geopolitical/tariff uncertainty. Pursue future growth including M&A.

=== CapEx & shareholder returns (Daniel Oh, IR) ===
- Q1 CapEx total KRW 11.2T (-KRW 9.2T QoQ): DS KRW 10.2T, Display KRW 0.6T.
- Memory CapEx down QoQ (front-loaded last year incl. new Pyeongtaek clean room); equipment spend to rise significantly as new capacity deployed; full-year CapEx to rise.
- Foundry CapEx down QoQ (base effect from Taylor Fab Q4 2025 infra); Taylor Fab ramp investment to rise from Q2.
- 2026 full-year CapEx: substantial YoY increase driven by AI demand; expand R&D in next-gen processes.
- Dividend: KRW 372/share quarterly; 3-year (2024-2026) policy min KRW 9.8T annual regular dividends.
- KRW 10T buyback (announced Nov 2024) completed Sep 2025; remaining shares cancelled (73.4M common, 13.6M preferred = 1.2%/1.7%), valuation ~KRW 14.6T; retained KRW 1.6T for employee compensation.

=== Memory (Jaejune Kim) ===
- Q1: robust server-application demand from hyperscaler CapEx expansion + initial agentic AI demand. Started shipping industry's first mass-produced HBM4 and SOCAMM2. Developed PCIe Gen 6 SSD on time, in customer qualification with positive feedback.
- Server bit shipment growth: DRAM +low teens % QoQ, NAND +low 20% QoQ (record-high server segment sales). Met DRAM bit guidance, exceeded NAND (high single-digit % QoQ).
- Blended ASP: DRAM +low 90% range QoQ, NAND +high 80% QoQ.
- Memory set another all-time high quarterly performance (second consecutive record).
- HBM4E: first samples in Q2 (16 Gbps pin speed, 4.0 TB/s bandwidth), built on 1c nano.
- Multi-year contracts / LTA: customers approaching for mid-to-long-term supply volume commitments; already signed finalized multi-year contracts with some customers; higher binding commitment than past.
- HBM: sales expected +>3x YoY in 2026; 1c nano process; production-ready capacity fully booked / sold out. World's first commercial HBM4 shipment in February 2026, ramping as scheduled, volume to scale in 2H. HBM4 >50% of total HBM sales from Q3, ~half on full-year basis.
- Demand fulfillment rate at record low; customers pulling forward 2027 demand; supply-demand gap to widen further in 2027.
- Q2 guidance: DRAM bit growth single-digit % QoQ; NAND low single-digit % QoQ.
- HBM vs conventional DRAM: conventional DRAM margins now higher (quarterly price negotiation vs annual HBM pricing → margin inversion); expect differential to narrow significantly in 2027. Will keep balanced HBM/conventional DRAM mix.
- NAND for AI: NVIDIA at GTC proposed CMX architecture extending AI inference data storage beyond HBM to NAND-based storage → rising demand for TLC-based PCIe Gen 6 SSDs. Production-ready for Gen 5 and Gen 6 super-high-performance NAND; positive feedback on Gen 6 early samples. Focus on AI server / data center; QLC demand growth, V9 migration; finished 2Tb QLC development in March; 256TB server SSDs lineup.

=== System LSI (Jason Shin) ===
- Q1 earnings improved QoQ on flagship SoC sales + seasonality. Exynos 2700 in development (builds on Exynos 2600), enhanced AI performance. Focus on custom SoC, 200MP image sensors; expanding from mobile into AI (data center to on-device inference solutions).

=== Foundry (Sukchae Kang) ===
- Q1 earnings down QoQ (seasonal 1H-low) but double-digit YoY revenue growth. Expanded customer base across HPC. Secured strategic project from a leading optical communication module player — milestone for silicon photonics business.
- Q2: advanced nodes to reach full utilization; HBM4 base die (on 4nm) demand. 1.4nm development on plan. Active 2nm engagement with major customers; 2nd-gen 2nm mass production in 2H. Expanding 4nm for memory and APU/AI applications. Targeting double-digit revenue growth.
- Diversifying beyond mobile into AI HPC, automotive, aerospace.
- Turnkey memory+foundry demand rising. In active talks with multiple large AI/HPC customers on 2nm projects. HBM4 base die on 4nm gaining recognition. Silicon photonics + CPO (co-packaged optics) development on advanced processes and 3D packaging; mass production for a major optical communications module player starting 2H 2026.
- Taylor Fab: Fab 1 equipment move-in ceremony April 23; operations 2026, mass production 2027. Fab 2 in early review. Mature lines: focus on high-value specialty (CIS, DDI migrate to 7nm; PMIC/DDI/CIS on 8-inch phased closeout).

=== Samsung Display (Charles Hur) ===
- Mobile display down QoQ (seasonality, memory price pressure); large display stable on gaming monitors. Premium focus (QD-OLED, 8.6-gen IT OLED), MDP privacy tech.

=== Mobile Experience (Seong Cho) ===
- Q1 MX revenue KRW 37.5T; combined MX+network operating profit KRW 2.8T. S26 series momentum; memory cost pressure. Galaxy A57/A37 launched April. Galaxy AI, foldables, AI glasses.

=== Visual Display (Won-uk Kim) ===
- Premium TV focus (Neo QLED, OLED, 75"+, Micro RGB). World Cup demand driver in Q2. TV Plus / advertising services, OS licensing.

=== DA / Cooling (Sang-Jik Lee) ===
- Data center cooling market projected to grow from $4.7B (2024) to $16.6B (2030), ~24% CAGR.
- Acquired FläktGroup (German HVAC specialist) last year → strategic foothold for data center cooling market. Operating centered on FläktGroup in Europe; expanding to North America (largest DC market); establishing Korean subsidiary/factory.

=== Company-wide ===
- Corporate value enhancement plan: invest over KRW 110 trillion in facilities + R&D (Pyeongtaek, US Taylor Fab, Yongin semiconductor cluster) + next-gen R&D. Pursue M&A / venture / equity investments. Future drivers: HVAC, automotive, electronics, medical, robotics.
- Physical AI / robotics: under Jun-ho Oh's leadership; internalizing robotics components; humanoid robots; two-track (own capability + global partners), strategic investments/acquisitions.
- Middle East risk: production normal; some processed gases sourced from Israel/Middle East but sufficient safety stock + diversified suppliers (US, Japan). Monitoring power/logistics costs.
- Labor union: rally at Pyeongtaek April 23; general strike announced May 21 - June 7; company to minimize production disruption.
