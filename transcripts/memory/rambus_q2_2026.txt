AI driving more diverse, persistent, MEMORY-INTENSIVE workloads; infrastructure becoming heterogeneous (traditional + AI servers), accelerating demand for CPU-based servers for orchestration, data management, real-time execution — raising memory capacity, bandwidth, power-efficiency requirements.

Chips: record product revenue $99M (+22% YoY); another double-digit-growth quarter expected in Q3. DDR5 roadmap: complete chipsets for DDR5 9600 CLIENT and SERVER modules — server chipset built around the SIXTH-GENERATION RCD + PMIC5030 (Gen5 not yet in market; Gen6 already introduced); client chipset for AI PCs as data-center tech waterfalls down. Novel memory architectures: complete chipsets for MRDIMM and LPDDR5X SOCAMM2, on track to intercept as adoption comes; expanding roadmap of memory-subsystem solutions -> increased chip content and sustained growth in 2027 and beyond.

Silicon IP: strong quarter; key design wins across hyperscalers, custom-silicon companies, emerging AI semiconductor developers. Growing number of DEEP ARCHITECTURAL ENGAGEMENTS AHEAD OF STANDARDS BEING FINALIZED. 'This includes an exciting DESIGN WIN WITH A TIER-1 U.S. HYPERSCALER FOR NEXT-GENERATION HBM IN FUTURE AI CHIPS.' Custom silicon for acceleration/connectivity = important long-term trend; security IP foundational for trusted data movement. Expanded AI IP with PCIe 7 SWITCH IP at 128 GT/s for next-gen scale-up/scale-out.

[Q&A highlights]
- Kevin Cassidy (Rosenblatt): no capacity issues in Q2; supply chain TIGHT, LEAD TIMES INCREASING; strong supplier relationships. CXMT: 'every company that builds memory has to have a LICENSE AGREEMENT with us, and they're one of them' — CXMT's rise is a long-run positive for Rambus patent licensing.
- Sebastian Naji (William Blair): MRDIMM — material opportunity, timing platform-dependent (AMD Venice in production, servers shipping from Q4); Q4 contribution MINIMAL, MATERIAL CONTRIBUTION IN 2027 when both CPU vendors' platforms ramp in earnest; won't overcall the adoption curve. Customer over-ordering: NO signs of customer inventory buildup; Rambus builds strategic inventory itself as lead times lengthen.
- Gary Mobley (StoneX): visibility building — CPU usage in AGENTIC AI is a demand driver; Q3 guide = another ~20% YoY product growth; H2 > H1; guiding one quarter at a time (platform-ramp timing + supply tightness). Silicon IP: 10-15%/yr growth with CONFIDENCE BUILDING (inference/agentic AI driving custom silicon using Rambus interconnect/security/memory IP); early engagement = long-term visibility.
- Aaron Rakers (Wells Fargo): CXL — an interconnect PROTOCOL, not a shape/product; relevant to IP business (memory-tiering cold->hot for agentic AI); at the PRODUCT level the market looks FRAGMENTED and ASIC-like, so Rambus keeps product investment in STANDARD products; enabling the ecosystem via IP. Hyperscaler dynamic: hyperscalers increasingly define their own architectures on complex subsystems (e.g. MEMORY subsystems), engaging Rambus BEFORE standards are complete; the chosen implementation then PROLIFERATES into their ecosystem (whoever builds the silicon licenses it) — a key driver of IP-growth confidence.
- Kevin Garrigan (Jefferies): companion chips (new products) = low-double-digit % of product revenue now -> MID-DOUBLE-DIGIT % BY END OF Q4 (on a growing base). LPDDR/SOCAMM: NOT cannibalizing RDIMM/MRDIMM — DDR stays dominant for server-grade scale/capacity/reliability; LPDDR/SOCAMM complementary where power efficiency rules; SOCAMM2 = 'seat at the table'; content increases with complexity.
- Tristan Gerra (Baird): unit-based business (no memory/CPU-style pricing dynamics); ~20% YoY product growth again in Q3; SERVER UNIT VIEW RAISED — was mid-to-high single digit, NOW DOUBLE-DIGIT growth (Gartner ~12%; 'we believe we're growing faster than that') on channel counts + new products; channel roadmap: AMD at 12, Intel 8->12, MARKET MOVING TO 16 CHANNELS/CPU — secular, not a step function. 2027 setup: Gen5 DDR5 grows in earnest at 16-channel; MRDIMM kicks in; companion chips + client growing — demand positive, but SUPPLY CONSTRAINTS PERSIST IN 2027 and platform ramps 'typically take a little longer than anticipated'; NO price increases on standard products (stay competitive, keep/gain DIMM share).
- Mark Lipacis (Evercore): three CPU-deployment frameworks — (1) head nodes next to GPUs: candidate for high-bandwidth/low-power (SOCAMM origin) and potentially MRDIMM-class solutions near HBM; (2) standalone agentic-AI CPU servers: LATENCY-CRITICAL — use MAXIMUM CHANNEL COUNT (not necessarily max capacity) to keep KV-cache pulls fast; (3) legacy/standard servers: standard solutions. HBM hyperscaler win = IP (LICENSE) win — hyperscaler defines the SoC architecture, Rambus IP inside; license-based, multi-license as the architecture proliferates (not volume-based). PCIe 7 switch IP: license revenue lands 'in the coming quarters,' WELL AHEAD of end-product ramps (12-24 months later) — early-engagement model doubles as market intelligence.
- Mehdi Hosseini (SIG): growth-confidence stack for 2027: all-DDR5 market with accelerating sub-generations (Gen6 introduced before Gen5 even ships = share-gain opportunities each generation), companion-chip headroom, MRDIMM 4x silicon content per module, client platforms; plus hyperscaler-defined custom silicon on the IP side. Product GM: model 60-65% intact (recent 60-63%); quarterly fluctuation on mix/supply.
