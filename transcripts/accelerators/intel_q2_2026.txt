[Lip-Bu Tan — CEO]
Q2 was another quarter of solid execution — revenue, gross margin, EPS all above guidance; SEVENTH consecutive quarter of exceeding expectations. Core message: strong demand continues to OUTPACE growing supply. Strongest revenue growth in more than 15 years. Google Cloud collaboration announced to accelerate AI-first transformation of operations. Industry facing one of the most SEVERE SUPPLY CONSTRAINTS in its history across leading-edge logic, silicon wafers, memory, and substrates — shortages to persist for the foreseeable future. Three strategic assets: x86 CPU franchise, advanced packaging, vast wafer foundry network. As AI expands from training to inference to agentic/multi-agent, general-purpose server CPU density keeps increasing; core server CPU franchise growing faster than ever.

Intel Foundry: factories across Intel 7, Intel 3, 18A EXCEEDED internal volume targets (yields, cycle times, wafer starts). 18A output increased meaningfully; yields tracking ahead of expectations; ramping multiple new products on 18A (Panther Lake, Wildcat Lake). 18A-P entered RISK PRODUCTION (adds performance/power, IP-compatible with 18A) — positioned for external customers. Intel 14A: defect density and transistor performance OUTPACING 18A development; PDK 0.5 complete, PDK 0.9 on track for October; increasing customer-engagement momentum; on track for 14A risk production (internal products) H2 2027; DECISION MADE IN Q2 TO FULLY COMMIT TO HIGH-VOLUME RAMP IN 2028. Advanced packaging: EMIB-T customer interest very high; GROWING EMIB-T BACKLOG; yield and reliability hitting targets; ramping to high volume to support CUSTOMER RAMPS IN 2027.

Products: PC business renamed CCPG (Client Computing and Physical AI Group). 18A in volume production across multiple products. DCAI: demand accelerated across cloud + enterprise; Q2 YoY server growth STRONGEST ON RECORD; Xeon 6 one of the fastest-ramping products in Intel history; additional strategic customer wins and LONG-TERM AGREEMENTS in Q2. Heterogeneous AI: multi-year SambaNova collaboration (disaggregated inference perf/power). Design services (ASIC) revenue growing nearly 3x YoY; Fortinet security-processor collaboration announced; roadmap from networking to compute to eventually accelerators.

[David Zinsner — CFO]
Q2 revenue $16.1B — $1.8 BILLION ABOVE the guidance midpoint. AI-driven businesses grew >70% YoY and contributed ~70% OF REVENUE. Wafer outs exceeded expectations, yet demand still outstrips supply. Non-GAAP GM 41.8% (+280bps vs guide; higher revenue, better yields, higher ASPs on mix + pricing actions). Non-GAAP EPS $0.42 vs $0.20 guided. Operating cash flow $7.0B; ~$30B cash + short-term investments ($40B liquidity incl. $10B revolver).

CCPG: $8.9B, +15% QoQ, above expectations — largely ASP-driven (mix + like-for-like price increases passing on cost inflation). AI PC revenue +26% QoQ, now TWO-THIRDS of client mix; edge ~10% of CCPG revenue. Op profit $2.3B (26%), down $173M QoQ on INVENTORY CHARGES (match-set stranded inventory written down to pivot output). 400+ Core Series 3 designs on 18A; 40+ Arc integrated-graphics designs; Arc G-Series for handheld gaming; vPro activations +1,500% over four quarters; 130 Series 3 edge-AI design wins incl. robotics brain/control. Edge + physical AI opportunity likely to AT LEAST MATCH the client TAM over time.

DCAI: $6.3B, +24% QoQ, +59% YoY — meaningfully ahead. Purpose-built silicon +~20% QoQ, nearly TRIPLING YoY. Op profit $2.5B = 40% OF REVENUE, up ~$1B QoQ (revenue, product margins, lower opex). Launched Xeon 6+ (Clearwater Forest) — first server product on 18A; rack-scale + disaggregated-inference innovations with SambaNova and Foxconn; new connectivity controllers/adapters 10GbE-200GbE.

Intel Foundry: revenue $5.8B, +6% QoQ; 18A output ~25% ABOVE TARGET and +>50% QoQ. External foundry revenue $293M. Op loss $2.1B, improved $348M QoQ (yields, cycle times, factory scale across Intel 4/3/18A). PANTHER LAKE primary SKU cost DOWN ~50% YEAR-TO-DATE, on track for ANOTHER 20% this year, further meaningful reductions in 2027.

Guidance: Q3 revenue $15.8-16.8B (mid $16.3B), GM 42%, tax 11%, EPS $0.38. Opex ~$16.5B for the year. Supply linearity skewed to END of Q3 and into Q4, ESPECIALLY SERVERS. PC consumption sub-seasonal H2; PC market down LOW-DOUBLE-DIGITS % for 2026 (memory prices + constraints) — in line with peers. Server CPU outlook improved AGAIN: forecasting strong double-digit INDUSTRY unit growth THIS YEAR AND NEXT, momentum extending into 2028.

CapEx: 2026 raised to MORE THAN $20 BILLION (up significantly vs plan; ~+$3B per analyst). Aggressively locking in tool POs, accelerating clean-room build-outs, securing substrates and memory. 2027 capex to be SIGNIFICANTLY ABOVE 2026, vast majority in the US. 2021-2026 US capital spending in tools + space approaching $100 BILLION — more than any other semiconductor company in that window. Emerging multibillion-dollar annual opportunities: physical AI, purpose-built silicon, advanced packaging, external wafers.

[Q&A highlights]
- Ben Reitzes (Melius): capex increase broad-based incl. advanced packaging (EMIB-T), skewed to front end; increased investment = confidence signal backed by LONG-TERM AGREEMENTS; discipline: capex only when confident of return; longer node lifetimes (Intel 7 pattern) drive significant returns. On AMD's $220B-by-2030 server CPU TAM: won't put a number out, but CPU:GPU ratio 'almost in PARITY at this point and could eventually even skew MORE TO CPUs on a unit basis'; growth will be significant.
- Joe Moore (MS): server share — demand strong, challenge is growing supply; roadmap Clearwater Forest -> Diamond Rapids -> Coral Rapids (adds SMT); Arm 'a great partner' (IP + ASIC foundry). Gross-to-net capex: AMIC investment tax credit = $0.35 back per $1 invested in the US (low-single-digit $B now, growing; delayed timing). Packaging: significant backlogs already; PREPAYING for substrates.
- Stacy Rasgon (Bernstein): client upside largely ASP (mix + pass-through pricing); Q3: client flattish, CCPG up a bit on edge; possible Q3 channel CPU build, Q4 digestion — 'good news: we need it, we'll pivot as much production as possible to data-center CPUs.' Inventory charge = stranded match-set write-down; Q3 GM flat as Panther Lake/Granite (early lifecycle, below corporate-avg margins) scale in mix — later become a TAILWIND, Panther Lake to rise ABOVE corporate average as 18A yields improve. Goal was GM 'comfortably in the 40s' every quarter — achieved.
- Tim Arcuri (UBS): undershipping (> $1B/qtr) continues; supply improves late Q3 into Q4 → Q4 lift expected but 'we will NOT catch up — we will be behind in Q4.' Choke points: advanced packaging, substrates, T-GLASS, memory — 'probably our most challenged parts of the supply chain'; front-end wafers more linear, the rest chunkier. GM fall-through rule of thumb: 40-60% on incremental revenue.
- Vivek Arya (BofA): external-foundry confidence path: 18A-P risk production ready before end-2026 (+5% performance); 14A PDK 0.9 October + 256Mb SRAM yield/defect density/performance ahead of an aggressive internal schedule; risk production H2 2027; committed volume 2028; customers get excited once they see PDK 0.9 + yields — capacity discussions underway. 2026 tooling spend +40% vs 2025 (space largely built; spend now tooling: Intel 3, 18A, 18A-P). 2027 number not yet set — 'expect it to be up.' Balance sheet: $40B liquidity, ~$10B non-core assets monetizable, customer PREPAYS already unlocking capacity, may tap capital markets if 'super successful.'
- C.J. Muse (Cantor): all server wafers internal; Intel 3 the critical node (Granite Rapids 'EXTREMELY TIGHT' — reception fantastic); good Intel 3 ramp planned rest of 2026 + 2027; back-end/substrate expansion the harder part; units-based market growth + rising core counts price on ASP-PER-CORE basis → 'well NORTH of a double-digit CAGR' for DCAI revenue over next few years. Capex framework: investment follows customer commitments ('until we really know we've got the customers, we don't put significant capital' — inverse read: current POs imply significant customer confidence); back-end third-party investments could drag 2027 cash flow.
- Aaron Rakers (Wells Fargo): ASIC business ~approaching $2B RUN RATE now -> $4B RUN RATE 'in the not too distant future'; $100B+ TAM; differentiators: CPU/XPU design, IP portfolio, advanced packaging + process; Fortinet next-gen security processor; IPU for hyperscalers. MEMORY: hired SEOK-HEE LEE (former SK Hynix CEO); memory = the big supply constraint, collaborating with all three big memory vendors; exploring compute-memory integration, stacking, higher memory utilization (analyst cites Intel 'Z-Angle Memory' / 'Cross-Batch Memory' development work) — 'stay tuned.'

[End of call]
