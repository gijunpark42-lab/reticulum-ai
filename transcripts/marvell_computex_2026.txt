COMPUTEX 2026 Keynote Summary — Marvell Technology
Date: June 1, 2026

Opening insights and industry context
Taiwan's transformation into a global technology hub has made it central to AI infrastructure development.

AI infrastructure performance is increasingly defined by connectivity, not just compute or memory.

The next wave of innovation in AI will be driven by advances in connectivity, especially as systems transition from copper to optical connections.

This transition is already underway and will accelerate over the next year.

Strategic transformation and growth
Over the past decade, a deliberate shift was made from consumer products to data infrastructure, focusing on moving, storing, processing, and securing data at scale.

Strategic M&A and divestitures, totaling $36 billion in investments, built a leading technology platform.

Revenue from data center business grew from less than 10% to over 75%, with rapid acceleration in recent years.

Current and projected revenues show significant growth, with consensus estimates of $16.4 billion next year.

Connectivity as the new bottleneck
As AI models and workloads scale, connectivity has become the primary challenge for infrastructure.

Hyperscalers are redesigning network architectures to address bandwidth and latency demands.

Optical connectivity is increasingly recognized as essential for scaling AI infrastructure.

Deep partnerships with compute and memory companies position the company as a neutral, strategic enabler in the ecosystem.
