=== PREPARED REMARKS ===

Richard Blickman: Thank you all for joining this call. [forward-looking disclaimer omitted]

For today's call, we'd like to review the key highlights for our first quarter ended March 31st, 2026, and update you on the market, our strategy, and outlook. First, some overall thoughts on the first quarter. BESI reported strong first quarter results and advanced packaging orders in an improving industry environment. Revenue of EUR 184.9 million increased 28.3% versus the first quarter of 2025 due to higher shipments for high-end mobile and 2.5D AI photonics and data center applications. Q1 2026 orders of EUR 269.7 million more than doubled versus the first quarter of 2025 due to broad-based growth across all BESI's end user markets, with particular strength in hybrid bonding, mobile, and photonics applications. Orders increased 7.7% versus Q4 last year, due primarily to a significant increase in bookings for hybrid bonding systems from multiple customers and end-user applications. Increased revenue growth this quarter favorably influenced BESI's profitability.

Net income rose 20.6% and 63.8% versus Q4 2025 and Q1 2025, respectively, with net margin increasing to 27.9% versus the 21.9% in the first quarter of 2025. Improved profitability this quarter was due primarily to enhanced revenue growth, disciplined expense management, and the benefits of operating leverage in BESI's business model. We realized a gross margin of 63.5% in the first quarter this year as increased prices helped offset increased component and energy cost inflation. In addition, our liquidity position improved significantly, with net cash growing by 186.9% versus the fourth quarter last year to reach EUR 103.3 million. Growth in our net cash position reflected improved profit and cash flow generation from operations of EUR 93 million in the first quarter 2026, which more than doubled versus the comparable period of the prior year.

During the quarter, BESI repurchased for approximately EUR 14.2 million of its shares, which brings the total purchases to EUR 25.5 million under the current 60 million buyback program. Next, I'd like to discuss the current market environment and our strategy. We've noticed an important improvement in market conditions since our last report, driven primarily by strong growth in AI demand and, to a lesser extent, additions to mobile and automotive capacity. The latest TechInsights forecast calls for 21% assembly market growth in 2026 and 75% between 2025 and 2030. We expect to significantly exceed such projected growth rates given our leadership position in advanced packaging and wafer level assembly, particularly in flip chip, multi-module die attach, hybrid bonding, and next generation TCB systems. Favorable order trends in the first quarter of this year reflect the strength of BESI's advanced packaging market position, particularly for next generation 2.5D and 3D AI applications.

Unit orders for hybrid bonding systems more than doubled versus the fourth quarter last year and exceeded the prior quarterly peak reached in Q2 2024 with respect to total units and order value. Growth was due primarily to a larger than anticipated capacity build this quarter by a customer and to a lesser extent, repeat orders from a memory customer for HBM applications. In addition, we shipped 2 evaluation tools to a second memory customer for HBM applications, and adoption increased to 20 customers overall. Progress also continued on our TCB Next agenda, with 2 new orders received and adoption increasing to six customers. Basic business prospects for 2026 were also enhanced by renewed growth for high-end mobile and automotive applications in this first quarter.

Our business strategy is currently focused on supporting customer adoption of our wafer-level assembly and 2.5D AI product portfolio and ramping the supply chain and production personnel necessary to meet increased order levels. We are also developing additional Vietnamese production capacity for mainstream assembly applications in order to free up incremental capacity in Malaysia for wafer-level assembly production. Further, BESI is increasing its service and support efforts in Taiwan and Korea in anticipation of increased hybrid bonding activities in such regions. Our favorable outlook for hybrid bonding growth in 2026 is also supported by a series of new products and use cases announced this year for logic, memory, co-packaged optics and consumer applications. Such announcements suggest that the pace of hybrid bonding adoption is increasing as we approach the timing for the introduction of many new AI-related products anticipated in the 2027 till 2030 period.

Now, a few words about our guidance. Based on our backlog and feedback from customers, we anticipate that BESI's Q2 2026 revenue will grow by 30%-40% versus the first quarter of this year, as strong revenue and order growth continue versus the prior year period. In addition, gross margins are anticipated to increase to a range of 64%-66%. Operating expenses are anticipated to be flat to up 10% due to increased revenue and customer support activities. As a result, we anticipate a significant expansion of our net income and profit margins relative to Q1 2026 and Q2 2025. As a result, we forecast for H1 2026 that revenue will increase by 49% versus the first half of 2025, assuming the midpoint of our second quarter 2026 guidance with a substantial improvement in operating and net income.

=== Q&A ===

Didier Scemama (Bank of America): On hybrid bonding, those orders in Q1, you mentioned that you're a bit surprised by those orders... Does that change anything about the profile of the ramp for this year at your main customer? Or is that leading to higher deliveries already this year because of AP7?

Richard Blickman: What is happening, you can follow easily in the bigger picture provided by Taiwan customer, is that we see an acceleration in adoption of hybrid bonding, and the order scheduled for installation in the first round in AP7 has been pulled forward somewhat from Q2 to Q1. In addition, the program has been enlarged for two reasons. One, for the overall timeline to fill in the anticipated 100 bonders. That number, we are told, may be significantly higher. Plus orders placed for co-packaged optics. Overall, you can say good news in acceleration of placing orders, and to some extent also an outlook for increased number of bonders required.

Didier Scemama: ...2027 you could start to see some new AI logic customers coming on board?

Richard Blickman: We all know that AMD was the first to adopt the hybrid bonding for several families, and they continued to do that. We also knew Broadcom, and one of the positive developments was also the Apple M5. We see broader adoption. At the same time, we've been told roadmaps from another very big customer in the data center modules, that we can expect more hybrid bonding adoption going forward. That is why we make the statement that we see accelerated and broader adoption. Also the number of customers: a quarter ago it was 18, now we are at 20. On the logic front, the adoption is broadening and increasing.

Didier Scemama: On the HBM front... repeat orders from a memory customer... Is that validation of your view that HBM4E is the real insertion point for hybrid bonding?

Richard Blickman: Evaluation programs and the end-customer engagement have increased very well in this first quarter. That has resulted in several more orders, and if all goes well, that should lead to mainstream adoption for certain HBM devices. It's still following the timeline: this year will be a major qualification year, based on the success of the qualification, setting up production capabilities towards the end of this year for mainstream volume production in 2027. That roadmap stands... one of the end customers is very clear also on their website on their adoption strategy of hybrid bonding for HBM.

Didier Scemama: Rule of thumb 150 hybrid bonding systems deployed by logic customers, that would mean the TAM for HBM could be 600?

Richard Blickman: That still stands. If you compare a capacity of 50 bonders for logic... you can easily see a processor surrounded by 3 or 4 memory stacks. That explains one ratio. The other ratio: when you have 16 dies in a stack, you need to do that 16 times, as opposed to one logic device. You need much more capacity for HBM than for logic. The rule of thumb is intact... One of the interesting recent documentation from TSMC is about the advanced packaging roadmap.

Alexander Duval (Goldman Sachs): US looked comparatively low... and China robust orders, key drivers?

Richard Blickman: US: we had a big round for the initial capacity for hybrid bonding received already about 1.5 years ago. That capacity is being filled in, qualified, tested; based on the results that customer can expect more bonders or not. At the same time, the onshoring programs: TSMC to the US, Amkor, also Micron — in the next 2 years (2026, 2027) preparation, building fabs, then as of 2028, volume production. We are engaged in those programs. China: several robust orders — 2.5D CoWoS-like capacity expanding, photonics (all the pluggables), recovery in modules for high-end smartphones, careful turning for industrial automotive. Also: more future capacities built outside China — Malaysia, Philippines, Thailand, Vietnam (our facility; by end of this year the first bonding system, not hybrid but proxy bonding). India: five major customers setting up production capabilities.

Ruben Devos (Kepler Cheuvreux): The 2 evaluation tools at the second memory customer — full tool-of-record evaluation? Timeline?

Richard Blickman: The timeline is 2026-2027. 2026 development, setting up pilot (small volumes for end-market qualification), then production for mainstream market adoption 2027 onwards. Yes, full tool-of-record evaluation.

Ruben Devos: JEDEC thickness standards relaxation for 20-high (775 micron towards 825/900)?

Richard Blickman: It does not change the advantage of using a hybrid process over a reflow process — faster circuitry, less power, less heat. The only reason that height should be available is for a process for ONE of the three, which simply requires that height. The other two are not impacted. We see increased engagement, activity, announcements — look at the Samsung website about hybrid bonding for HBM. The benefits are every day more proven in the logic application; that supports adoption of hybrid bonding in HBM stacking.

Charles Shi (Needham): You said 2 evaluation units to a second memory customer, but you already have 2 memory customers — is this the third?

Richard Blickman: You're right. We had two — one US, and one Korean who started in a lab to develop a similar hybrid solution two years ago. The change is they have moved this to the forefront. That customer has two applications: one is logic, the other HBM stacking. Adding the third one, we now have ALL THREE who have our hybrid bonders to further evaluate and define the adoption of hybrid bonding for HBM stacking.

Charles Shi: Timeline for the third?

Richard Blickman: The timeline is 2026. The first customer aiming towards the mid of this year, June/July, and the second one a bit behind — end of Q3, Q4. That will determine the adoption in volume for 2027. The third customer is ready to go; they are all evaluating along the same parameters, for ONE SPECIFIC END CUSTOMER. The whole world knows that customer has invited all three to have these hybrid bonded stacks available by the end of 2026 to be used in end market applications in 2027.

Charles Shi: Integrated vs standalone for memory? Economics difference?

Richard Blickman: We sell bonders and AMAT sells Kinex automated lines; both have a sales value... Currently the overwhelming majority is standalone because of the initial phase — multiple customers, different die sizes, flexibility. In an integrated line you achieve better process specs (particles, timing, die-to-die integrity) — cluster-tool concept like front end. Hybrid process requires zero particles, so an automated line achieves the best environment. For high volume with minimum changeover -> integrated; more changeover -> standalone (like the Taiwanese customer today, likely to change to automated lines in future). For us, the bonder value is the SAME standalone or integrated. For HBM: INTEGRATED, because HBM is dedicated high-volume production.

Robert Sanders (Deutsche Bank): Yield numbers in HBM development? Partial vs wholesale transition at HBM4E?

Richard Blickman: We don't receive detailed yield numbers, but as a rule, if a process is not well up into the 99.9%, it becomes very difficult long term. Yields in interconnect are at those levels; with Logic the yields are very well up in the 99.99%+. For HBM stacking one still has to prove that — currently happening. HBM vs logic: far less I/O (easier, bond pad pitch less critical) but vertical stacks of 16 thinner devices (different process). Quality classes possible (best chips hybrid bonded). Why all this sudden effort: proven performance upgrades are driving adoption BY AN END CUSTOMER, and that's a very big customer. On TCB displacement: same end volume with hybrid means less TCB; we should see more confirmation from the Samsung side by the middle/end of this year.

Martin Marandon-Carlhian (Oddo BHF): The biggest GPU vendor disclosed 3D stacking for their next GPU coming in 2028 — hybrid bonded? Timing?

Richard Blickman: That's exactly one of the major game changers, and it IS forecasted to be hybrid bonding. That fits the acceleration we explained — anticipating adoption of hybrid bonding in that family of next-generation products. There is more equipment to be ordered and installed in 2027, ordered in 2026, for volume production as of 2028.

Martin: Chip-on-panel — square panel opportunities?

Richard Blickman: The 310 by 310 panel is a very clear development coming to market in the next 1-2 years. We already received orders for certain applications. Our bonders can handle the 310x310. Hybrid is a bit early, but with larger die sizes, more module-type designs, 2.5D and even more 3D, the panel will be used as a carrier more and more. More at the Investor Day mid-June.

Martin: OpEx discipline?

Richard Blickman: Simply controlling cost. With increased revenue you have enormous operating leverage.

Nigel van Putten (Morgan Stanley): Photonics before CPO — silicon photonics and higher-throughput pluggables benefit?

Richard Blickman: Started middle of last year, a significant expansion of that market segment with multiple customers building those pluggables. Next-generation pluggables require MORE bonding steps. Don't mix with co-packaged optics — different application and process; on CPO we made significant progress: we delivered the hybrid bonding for accomplishing those contacts. More at Investor Day.

Nigel: Order intake outlook — backlog ~EUR 400M by March end?

Richard Blickman: Continued momentum, continuing trend. We're in an upcycle; as long as no saturation in end markets, expect it to continue. We've ramped capacity 50% quarter-over-quarter in past upcycles; so far this quarter no change.

Nabeel Aziz (Rothschild & Co Redburn): Service business pickup with HB?

Richard Blickman: Certainly — production lines loading, more spares/service/upgrades; hybrid bonding follows a front-end model: ~900 defined spare parts for hybrid bonders available within four hours, 24/7. Service was ~15-16% of revenues; with HB installed base growing it may move toward 18-20% (front end is 20-25%, with long-term service contracts). It is a gross-margin TAILWIND.

Martin Jungfleisch (BNP Paribas): Current hybrid bonding capacity and expansion? Lead times?

Richard Blickman: We were at 15 bonders theoretically per month (~180/yr). With increased floor space and a model adjusted to several customers, we can expand to 250 PER YEAR. Lead time for the 100-nanometer tool: 6 months standard. We can satisfy any model presented by the big five using the expanded capacity, plus field installation/support infrastructure. It's all part of our EUR 1.5-1.9 billion revenue model for the next 3-5 years (roughly 2.5-3x current revenue).

Martin: Intel EMIB relevance?

Richard Blickman: We are involved since the very beginning in placement of these EMIB modules. They have significant capacity installed which we delivered. It's good news when it increases — but with Intel we first need to see more evidence.

Madeleine Jenkins (UBS): China on 3D/hybrid bonding — discussions, would you sell?

Richard Blickman: We only sell what regulations (US government, tested every six months) allow — current-level die bonders and hybrid bonders are allowed in China. End applications still distant; there could be a philosophy to use hybrid bonding in 3D stacking to LENGTHEN NODE-SIZE LIFE — increase device performance with 3D hybrid structures where they cannot buy next-gen litho. They are engaged in development, very aggressive in studying hybrid benefits. The current big China market is 2.5D mass-reflow flip chip — our flip chip has the best cost of ownership in China too. Expect local Chinese device structures using a hybrid process — could be a very significant market.
