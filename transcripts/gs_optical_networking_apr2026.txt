Goldman Sachs Global Tech Research
"Optical Networking: The next mega trend in AI infrastructure"
Published: April 17, 2026

=== EXECUTIVE SUMMARY ===

Networking is the next frontier in AI infrastructure. Key findings:
- Dollar content increase by 16x / 45x in Scale Out / Scale Up per computing unit from GB300 NVL72 to Rubin Ultra NVL576
- 13x larger addressable market for optical modules / optical engines expanding from scale out to scale up
- 10x larger value market for pluggable optical modules in scale out per computing unit from GB300 NVL72 to Rubin Ultra NVL576 (even with 29% CPO penetration)
- Pluggable optical modules (1.6T equivalent) per computing unit: 216 units in GB300 NVL72 → 2.5k units in Rubin Ultra NVL576
- Aggregate dollar content per computing unit: US$315k in GB300 NVL72 → US$9.4bn in Rubin Ultra NVL576 (29x increase)
- Aggregate value TAM across scale up and scale out: US$15bn in GB300 NVL72 (mainly 2026) → US$154bn in Rubin Ultra NVL576 (mainly 2028), a 9x increase
- Among US$154bn TAM: 69% goes to scale up (US$106bn); CPO contributes US$91bn (59% of total TAM at 29% CPO penetration)

=== LIGHT SOURCE SUPPLY CHAIN (Section 9) ===

Supply tightness remains in 2026 across both EML and CW lasers due to:
1. Strong demand driven by AI server ramp-up, speed migration, optical connections expansion
2. InP substrate supply constraints (used in both EML and CW lasers, across PD receivers and LD transmitters), under geopolitical tension / export controls by China government
3. Time required for capacity expansion and ramp-up

VPEC (Visual Photonics Epitaxy): Plans to expand InP MOCVD from 60 units to 64 units in 2H26
Landmark: Commits to significant capacity expansion in 2026 (specific figures not disclosed)
YJ Semi: Commits to significant capacity expansion in 2026 (specific figures not disclosed)
Lumentum: Plans to expand capacity by 40% from CY3Q25 to CY2Q26; likely to bring more capacities online in 2026
Coherent: Committed to doubling the capacity (from Feb 2026 statements)

GS expects light source supply to remain tight through 2027, turning more balanced in 2H28 post supply chain capacity expansion (or slowdown in AI server spec upgrades as industry shifts to inferencing, or ease of geopolitical tension on China export controls).

Light source technology options for CPO:
- SiPh + CW Laser: Energy efficiency lower, effective reach >1km, cost per bit medium, technology readiness high
- VCSEL: Energy efficiency high, effective reach <100m, cost per bit low, technology readiness high
- MicroLED: Energy efficiency high, effective reach <20m, latency low, technology readiness low

=== OPTICAL CIRCUIT SWITCH (OCS) ===

OCS development timeline:
- 2015: Google launched Apollo OCS project
- 2023: Google announced TPU v4 first supercomputer to deploy OCS, with 4,096 chips interconnected by 48 internally-developed OCS
- 2025: Google's TPU v7 SuperPod adopts OCS to interconnect 9,216 chips
- 2025: OCP announced new OCS project with participants: Lumentum, iPronics, Google, NVIDIA, Coherent, Microsoft
- 2026: Lumentum OCS backlog exceeded $400 million (announced Feb 2026); scaling rapidly to meet extraordinary customer demand
- 2026: Coherent expects OCS revenue to ramp through 2026 and 2027, with over 10 customer engagements as of Feb 2026; shipments include 64x64 and 320x320 systems

Key OCS players and progress:
- Lumentum: OCS backlog >$400M in Feb 2026; MEMS-based OCS (mass production)
- Coherent: 10+ customers, 64x64 and 320x320 OCS systems shipping/backlog; expects sequential revenue growth
- Innolight: Targets SiPh-based OCS in 2027
- Robotechnik: Obtained order for "fully automated OCS packaging line" from European customer; order worth EUR 7.7M
- Accelink: OCS switch manufacturer (Wuhan, China); MEMS-based OCS

OCS technology options:
- MEMS: Mature, mass production (dominant solution), switching time <100ms, insertion loss ~3dB, drive voltage ~100V
- LC/LCoS: High reliability, no moving parts, slow switching >100ms, insertion loss ~4dB, drive voltage ≤10V
- Piezo/DLBS: More reliable than MEMS, lower loss, harder to scale ports; in qualification
- Silicon Photonics (SiPh): Fast switching (nanosecond), high insertion loss ~6dB; in qualification

OCS ASP: $50k-$200k (vs traditional switch $10k-$100k)

Google's Palomar OCS: MEMS-based technology; TPU v7 SuperPod interconnects 9,216 chips via OCS

=== SILICON PHOTONICS (Section 8) ===

SiPh advantages over discrete optical transceivers:
1. Higher level of integration and smaller size
2. Lower power consumption
3. Lower costs (especially at higher speeds: 26% BoM advantage at 800G, 32% at 1.6T)

SiPh penetration in datacom: 6% in 1Q24 → 45-46% in 4Q28E

SiPh chip components: Modulator, Photo detector, Waveguide/Coupler, MUX/DEMUX
Paired with: DSP (driver/TIA), FAU (fiber array unit), CW laser (external)

TSMC manufactures silicon photonics PICs (photonic integrated circuits) - implied by industry structure

=== CONNECTION TECHNOLOGY SPECTRUM ===

PCB traces: <1m, scale-up only
PCB midplane: <1m, scale-up (Rubin Ultra NVL144 Kyber rack), M9 CCL materials, 78+ layers, ASP ~$112,500
DAC (Direct Attach Copper): 0.5-3m, scale-up
ACC (Active Copper Cable): 7-15m, scale-up (inter-rack), includes Redriver
AEC (Active Electrical Cable): 5-30m, scale-up (inter-rack), includes Retimer; AmazonTrn2-Ultra64 uses AEC
AOC (Active Optical Cable): 30-100m, scale-up inter-rack and scale-out, multimode fiber + VCSEL
Transceiver + Fiber DR: 30-500m, scale-out (ToR to Spine, Leaf to Spine)
Transceiver + Fiber FR: 500m-2km, scale-out inter-building

Copper trend: evolving from DAC → ACC → AEC (toward longer distance)
PCB trend: evolving from intra-tray → intra-rack
Optics trend: evolving from scale-out → scale-up, with AOC and CPO switches covering shorter distances
