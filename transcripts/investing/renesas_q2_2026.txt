---
Renesas Electronics said second-quarter fiscal 2026 revenue and profit came in ahead of its own guidance, helped by strong demand in automotive and data center chips, better margins and favorable foreign exchange. The Japanese semiconductor maker reported revenue of 453 billion yen, operating profit of 132.7 billion yen and gross margin of 58.1%. The stock rose 7.7% to $3,442 from a previous close of $3,196, putting it near the middle of its 52-week range of $1,656.5 to $5,284.

Key Takeaways

Revenue rose 24.8% from a year earlier on an apples-to-apples basis and increased 8.8% from the previous quarter.Gross margin reached 58.1%, 100 basis points above guidance.Operating margin was 32.7%, beating the company’s 29.0% guidance midpoint by 370 basis points.Automotive and IoT demand both exceeded expectations, while data center demand was described as exceptionally strong.The company said earthquake damage at its Kumamoto sites should have a limited effect on earnings.

Company Performance

Renesas posted a strong quarter across its main businesses, with management saying automotive and IoT results were better than expected and data center demand remained especially firm. Chief Executive Hidetoshi Shibata said the company exceeded expectations in both automotive and IoT, while also benefiting from a favorable product mix and yen weakness.

The company’s performance also reflected disciplined spending. Operating expenses fell by more than 7 billion yen, helped by delayed hiring, postponed ERP work and other timing effects. That helped push operating margin well above guidance.

Renesas is benefiting from broader trends in semiconductors, especially the rise in AI-related and data center demand. At the same time, its automotive business remains stable, with growth driven by new products such as the Gen 4 R-Car SoC and 28nm microcontrollers.

Financial Highlights

Revenue: 453 billion yen, up 24.8% year over year on an apples-to-apples basis and up 8.8% sequentially.Gross margin: 58.1%, 100 basis points above guidance.Operating margin: 32.7%, versus 29.0% guidance midpoint.Operating profit: 132.7 billion yen.EBITDA: 154.3 billion yen.Profit attributable to owners of parent: 113.3 billion yen.Net profit: 149.2 billion yen, helped by 36 billion yen in financial income tied to Wolfspeed-related assets.Input utilization rate: 58%.Q3 revenue guidance: 430 billion yen, implying 28.7% year-over-year growth.

Earnings vs. Forecast

Renesas beat its own guidance on the top line and margins. Revenue of 453 billion yen was about 60.2 billion yen above the implied forecast of 392.8 billion yen, a beat of roughly 15.3% if the figures are compared on the same currency basis. The company did not provide a comparable EPS figure in the supplied data, so an EPS surprise cannot be calculated.

The size of the beat was meaningful. Revenue came in 4.5% above guidance, and gross margin beat by 100 basis points. The larger surprise was operating margin, which exceeded the midpoint of guidance by 370 basis points. That suggests Renesas had both stronger demand and better cost control than expected.

The result also looks stronger than a routine quarterly beat. Management pointed to broad demand strength, especially in automotive and data center chips, rather than a one-time lift alone. However, it also said more than half of the revenue upside came from foreign exchange, which may not repeat at the same level.

Market Reaction

Renesas shares rose 7.7% to $3,442 after the report, up $246 from the previous close of $3,196. The move suggests investors welcomed both the earnings beat and the company’s comments on demand. The stock is now trading above the midpoint of its 52-week range, though still below the high of $5,284.

The reaction was likely helped by the company’s message that earthquake damage should be limited and that production at the Kawashiri plant is expected to resume on Aug. 5, with full operation about three weeks later. Investors also appeared to respond to the company’s confidence in data center demand and selective price increases.

Outlook & Guidance

For the third quarter, Renesas guided for revenue of 430 billion yen, up 28.7% from a year earlier and 6.1% from the second quarter. It expects gross margin of 57.5% and operating margin of 32.5%, both slightly below the second quarter as manufacturing costs rise with energy, maintenance and seasonal factors.

Management said it implemented selective price increases on July 1 for some products, and that the effect should begin to show in second-half results. The company stressed that the increases are moderate rather than broad-based.

The company is also closing its Takasaki 6-inch factory over time, while keeping employment in place and shifting resources toward research and development.

Executive Commentary

Shibata said the second quarter was stronger than expected across key businesses. “In looking back to second quarter versus our guidance for automotive and IoT, both has been very strong, and we have exceeded our expectation,” he said.

On the company’s data center position, he said Renesas is in a strong spot but must keep executing. “As of now, I think we are in a very good position. I’m confident about that. In order to maintain our good position, we have to do a very strong execution,” he said.

He also said demand remains tight across the market. “Everywhere you look, everything seems very tight,” he said, adding that the company is working on step-function supply increases for later this year and next year.

Risks and Challenges

Earthquake recovery: Damage at the Kumamoto sites could still affect production in the near term, even if management expects the impact to be limited.Tight supply: Strong demand is positive, but limited capacity can prevent Renesas from fully meeting customer needs.Higher manufacturing costs: Energy, maintenance and seasonal costs are expected to pressure margins in the third quarter.FX dependence: More than half of the revenue beat came from foreign exchange, which may not continue.Legacy manufacturing shutdown: Closing the Takasaki 6-inch plant reflects structural decline in older process technology and could create transition costs.

Q&A

Analysts focused heavily on the earthquake, margins, pricing and demand trends.

On the earthquake, management said the impact was smaller than the 2016 Kumamoto quake and that countermeasures such as seismic equipment and better facility placement helped speed recovery. The company said the Nishiki back-end plant resumed full production quickly, while the Kawashiri front-end plant is targeted to restart on Aug. 5.

On margins, the company explained that third-quarter gross margin is expected to fall 0.6 percentage point sequentially because a 50-basis-point gain from utilization will be offset by about 100 basis points of higher production costs.

On pricing, Shibata said Renesas raised prices selectively on July 1, but not in a large or headline-grabbing way. He said the effect should be gradual rather than immediate.

Analysts also asked about the Takasaki closure. Management said the plant’s 6-inch manufacturing process is becoming harder to support as the industry moves away from older technology. Renesas said it will maintain employment, support relocation and keep the site active for R&D work.

In the automotive business, management said demand remains stable and that new products are helping drive content growth, especially in Japan and China. In data center chips, the company said demand from NPU customers remains very strong and supply is still tight.
