SCREEN Holdings Co. said first-quarter profit fell sharply from a year earlier, even as the Japanese semiconductor equipment maker raised its full-year outlook on strong demand for chip tools tied to artificial intelligence and memory investment. The company reported net sales of JPY 121.7 billion for the quarter ended June, down 10.3% from a year earlier, while operating income dropped 41.1% to JPY 14.3 billion. The stock fell 10.82% to $14175 in recent trading, after closing at $15895, leaving it well below its 52-week high of $19770.

SCREEN&rsquo;s first quarter was marked by a clear split between near-term weakness and a stronger longer-term outlook. The company&rsquo;s core Semiconductor Production Equipment, or SPE, business accounted for 76.5% of sales, but segment revenue fell 16% from a year earlier as customers in China and Taiwan delayed some foundry and logic projects.

That pressure weighed on group profitability. Operating margin fell to 11.8% from a much higher level a year earlier, while net income declined to JPY 10.4 billion. Management said the decline was largely the result of lower sales, not a broader deterioration in the business.

Other segments were more resilient. Graphic Arts sales rose modestly, Field Test and Display sales increased, and Process Equipment remained small but showed signs of recovery in demand for memory packaging investment. Still, the quarter underscored how dependent SCREEN remains on the semiconductor cycle.

The company&rsquo;s forecast table did not include actual EPS or revenue figures in the usual comparable format, so a direct consensus beat or miss could not be measured from that data. Still, the quarter was clearly weaker on a year-on-year basis, with sales down 10.3% and operating income down 41.1%.

Management framed the result as broadly expected. Chief Executive Masato Goto said the profit decline reflected lower sales and timing issues, including a delayed project that is now expected to be booked in the second quarter. Chief Financial Officer Manabu Ishimura said the drop in profit was driven mainly by sales, with no other major factor.

The more important signal for investors may be the full-year guidance increase. That suggests management sees the first-quarter weakness as temporary rather than a sign of a deeper slowdown. Compared with recent quarters, however, the magnitude of the profit decline was large and likely stood out to investors.

SCREEN shares fell 10.82% to $14175, down from a previous close of $15895. The stock is now about 28.2% below its 52-week high of $19770, though still roughly 165.9% above its 52-week low of $5335.

The decline suggests investors focused on the weak first-quarter profit trend and on the company&rsquo;s heavy spending plans, even as management raised its full-year outlook. The market may also be reacting to the timing of delayed sales, which adds uncertainty to near-term results.

No trading volume data was provided, so it is not possible to confirm whether the move came with unusually heavy activity.

The company also lifted segment guidance for SPE, which remains the main earnings engine. SPE sales are now seen at JPY 620 billion, up JPY 20 billion from the prior forecast, with operating income of JPY 159 billion. Graphic Arts was also raised slightly, while Field Test and Display was cut to JPY 43 billion from JPY 47 billion because of a reassessment of advanced packaging demand.

Management said the order book for the year is almost fully fixed, giving it confidence in the revised outlook. It expects the second half to run at or near full capacity, with the fourth quarter stronger than the third. A delayed China and Taiwan memory project is expected to contribute in the second quarter, with about half of the related sales already shipped at the time of the call.

The company also said post-sales revenue should exceed JPY 100 billion for the first time this fiscal year, supported by consumables, maintenance and replacement parts. That business is becoming a bigger part of the company&rsquo;s strategy.

&ldquo;The order for the sales of this fiscal year is almost fixed. Based on that, we are making upward revision,&rdquo; Goto said, explaining the higher full-year outlook.

He also pointed to pricing power tied to product performance rather than market strength alone. &ldquo;We are not making negotiation because we are in a strong position, but we are now improving performance of the equipment, and those are evaluated, and those are reflected in the price,&rdquo; he said.

On the broader market, Goto said, &ldquo;Foundry, AI-driven leading edge investments to be the primary driver of WFE growth.&rdquo; He added that memory makers are asking for faster deliveries and that demand is now stronger than current capacity.

CFO Ishimura said post-sales will be a key support for earnings. &ldquo;For this fiscal year post-sales, the post-sales will exceed JPY 100 billion and will further enhance this field of business to ensure the profitability,&rdquo; he said.

Analysts focused on lead times, the strength of demand in China, and the company&rsquo;s ability to meet its full-year targets.

In response to questions about delivery timing, Goto said average lead times are about 4.2 months and that the fiscal-year order book is almost fully fixed. He said fourth-quarter sales should exceed third-quarter sales, while the second half should be near full capacity.

On China, he said demand for advanced semiconductor tools remains strong, especially for AI-related infrastructure and cutting-edge devices. He described inquiry levels as record high and said the company remains well positioned in advanced products, even as competition stays intense.

Analysts also pressed management on the sharp drop in first-quarter profit. Ishimura said the decline was mainly the result of lower sales, while Goto said the business remains on track for the full year and that any upside would likely be limited by the need to keep investing in capacity and growth.

Questions about pricing showed that SCREEN is trying to pass through higher costs by tying price to better equipment performance and rising raw material costs. Management said customers are increasingly accepting those adjustments.

The call also touched on post-sales revenue, which management said will rise as older equipment enters the maintenance and consumables phase. SCREEN said that business should become a more important source of recurring income over time.
