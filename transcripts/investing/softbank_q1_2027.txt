SoftBank Group said its net asset value rose to a record JPY 72.3 trillion in the quarter ended June 30, 2026, as investment gains reached JPY 1.8 trillion and net income came in at JPY 347 billion. The company highlighted strong performance at Arm Holdings, growing demand for its planned AGI CPU, and rapid adoption of OpenAI products such as Codex. Shares fell 4.41% to $5695 from the previous close of $5958, putting the stock near the middle of its 52-week range of $3116.3 to $9074.

SoftBank Group described the quarter as one of exceptional performance, driven mainly by gains in its investment portfolio rather than by a traditional operating business. The company said net asset value, or NAV, climbed to JPY 72.3 trillion at the end of June, the highest level in its history. That figure later eased to about JPY 58.3 trillion on a pro forma basis as markets moved after quarter-end.

The group&rsquo;s investment gain of JPY 1.8 trillion was up JPY 1.3 trillion from a year earlier. Intel was a major contributor after its share price rose sharply during the April-to-June period, while ByteDance also lifted results through fair-value gains. SoftBank said its loan-to-value ratio stood at 13%, well below its 25% policy threshold, and that cash of JPY 2.3 trillion still covered more than two years of expected bond redemptions.

The company&rsquo;s results again showed how closely its performance tracks market values of holdings such as Arm, ByteDance and OpenAI-related assets. That makes SoftBank different from many technology companies, since changes in stock prices and private valuations can have a large effect on quarterly results.

SoftBank shares fell 4.41% to $5695 from $5958, a drop of $263. The stock remains well above its 52-week low of $3116.3, but it is also far below the high of $9074, showing how volatile the name can be.

The decline suggests investors were not fully reassured by the strong NAV and investment gains. SoftBank also said Arm&rsquo;s AGI CPU has attracted more demand than expected, with confirmed orders now above $2 billion, up from an initial combined revenue guide of about $1 billion for fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2027. The company expects clearer revenue visibility in the next one to two quarters as wafer capacity constraints ease.

For the broader group, Masayoshi Son set a 15-year goal of lifting NAV to JPY 1,000 trillion, a target that reflects the company&rsquo;s long-term ambition rather than a near-term financial guide.

Yoshimitsu Goto, SoftBank&rsquo;s board director and chief financial officer, said NAV is the company&rsquo;s main yardstick. &ldquo;Net asset value is the most important indicator to see and value our enterprise value,&rdquo; he said.

On AI infrastructure, Goto argued that supply remains too tight to meet demand. &ldquo;The supply is very much short, and that is why this project is announced one after another, which I believe is a healthy situation,&rdquo; he said.

He also stressed the company&rsquo;s role as a strategic investor rather than a passive holder. &ldquo;We are a holding company, and we are a strategic investment holding company, so from our perspective, our job is to make sure that our portfolio company work together so that we can help them to raise their corporate value,&rdquo; he said.

Mark Agni of SB Investment Advisers said SoftBank is concentrating its large language model bets on OpenAI. &ldquo;We consider them [OpenAI] more of a partner than an investment,&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;It&rsquo;s our choice to put all of our eggs in OpenAI as far as LLM is concerned, rather than spread it around.&rdquo;

Analysts focused on whether SoftBank is overinvesting in data centers, how it views OpenAI&rsquo;s IPO timing, and whether the company is comfortable with its financing strategy.

On data center spending, Goto said the company sees a real supply-demand gap and does not view the market as a bubble. He said AI services will slow if computing power is not secured.

On OpenAI, management declined to comment on timing of any IPO but said it expects the company to go public eventually.

Questions also centered on the Ohio project and reports that NVIDIA may have guaranteed JPY 250 billion to help support OpenAI&rsquo;s lease. SoftBank declined to discuss those details.

Analysts asked about Arm&rsquo;s AGI CPU demand, and Arm CFO Jason Child said confirmed orders have already grown to more than $2 billion, with more clarity expected as supply constraints improve.

Another question addressed SoftBank&rsquo;s 15-year NAV target and succession planning. Goto said Masayoshi Son remains highly motivated and that the company wants to support his efforts as he pushes the group&rsquo;s long-term strategy forward.
