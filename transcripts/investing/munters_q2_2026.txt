Munters said second-quarter demand accelerated sharply, with organic order intake rising more than 140% and backlog climbing 151%, even as supply chain problems and tariff pressure weighed on margins. The Swedish climate-control company did not provide the actual EPS and revenue figures in the material reviewed, but the market appeared to focus on the strength of future demand: the stock rose 5.67% to $175.05 in pre-market trading from a previous close of $165.65.

Munters delivered a quarter that was strong on demand but uneven on execution. Net sales rose 6% organically, while order intake and backlog grew much faster, suggesting the company is building a larger revenue base for coming quarters. The strongest momentum came from Data Center Technologies, where demand remained exceptionally high, and from AirTech, where components and services also performed well.

Profitability was mixed. AirTech benefited from cost-saving programs and better volumes, but DCT margins fell because of product mix changes, a planned production ramp-up and supply chain constraints. FoodTech remained healthy, though management described some of the quarter&rsquo;s weakness there as temporary.

Overall, the quarter reinforced Munters&rsquo; position in data center cooling and air treatment markets, both of which continue to benefit from structural demand trends. The company said its broad product portfolio helped it shift production and deliveries as needed, limiting the impact of shortages.

Munters&rsquo; reported EPS and revenue were not included in the provided figures, so a direct comparison with the forecast is not available. The market therefore appears to have reacted more to the company&rsquo;s operating momentum and outlook than to a quantified earnings surprise.

The forecast for the quarter called for EPS of $1.03 and revenue of $3.93 billion. Against that backdrop, the call emphasized stronger demand, higher backlog and unchanged full-year guidance. Those factors often matter as much as the current-quarter result for a company with long lead times and a large order book.

In practical terms, the quarter looked stronger on demand than on margins. That combination can support the stock if investors believe revenue will follow the backlog into the second half and into 2027.

The shares rose 5.67% to $175.05 from $165.65, a gain of $9.40. The stock is now well above its 52-week low of $109.80 and still below the high of $212.40, placing it in the upper part of its yearly trading range.

The move suggests investors welcomed the size of the order book and management&rsquo;s confidence that supply chain issues are temporary. The reaction was also helped by the company&rsquo;s decision to keep its full-year growth outlook unchanged. For a business tied closely to data center spending, that kind of guidance can matter more than a single quarter&rsquo;s margin pressure.

Munters kept its 2026 business outlook unchanged. Management said Data Center Technologies is still expected to grow sales by 30% for the full year, with growth tilted toward the end of the year because supply chain problems delayed the ramp-up by about one quarter.

The company expects profitability to improve in the second half as production rises and backlog converts into revenue. Management also said the Virginia facility expansion is progressing, with output already doubled and floor space increased by 60%.

For AirTech, management expects continued margin improvement as cost-saving programs work through the business and order intake remains near SEK 2 billion. FoodTech is still being prepared for separation, and management said market interest in a potential divestment is strong.

Looking further ahead, Munters said it sees a sharper, more focused company after FoodTech is separated, with future annual revenue potentially above SEK 20 billion.

Chief Executive Klas Forsstr&ouml;m said the quarter delivered &ldquo;exceptional order intake&rdquo; in both Data Center Technologies and AirTech, adding that the company is set up for &ldquo;substantial revenue growth 2027 and beyond.&rdquo; The comment underscores management&rsquo;s focus on the long term rather than the current quarter alone.

He also said the company is &ldquo;super confident in the order backlog,&rdquo; pointing to the 3.6x book-to-bill in DCT. That is a strong signal that management believes demand is not the problem; execution is.

On the company&rsquo;s broader strategy, Forsstr&ouml;m said Munters is creating &ldquo;a sharper, more focused Munters&rdquo; by optimizing its portfolio and positioning FoodTech outside the group. He said the future company will be centered on Data Center Technologies and AirTech.

Analysts focused heavily on whether supply chain issues could derail the company&rsquo;s growth plan. Management said the 30% full-year DCT sales target still stands and that the delay mainly shifts growth into the back half of the year.

Questions also centered on whether customers might turn to competitors because of production bottlenecks. Management said it has seen no signs of customer dissatisfaction and noted that the broad product portfolio allows it to adjust deliveries and product mix.

Another topic was AirTech&rsquo;s strong order intake, including a large battery-related order reversal and a surge in components. Management said the battery business is still a smaller contributor, while the component strength reflects market share gains and unusual demand from competitors&rsquo; delivery problems.

Analysts also asked about FoodTech&rsquo;s weaker order intake. Management called that temporary and said the divestment process has drawn some attention away from order generation, but the business remains attractive to potential buyers.
