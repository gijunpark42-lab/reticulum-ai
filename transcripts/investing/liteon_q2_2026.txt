Lite-On Technology reported record second-quarter results for 2026, with revenue rising 30% from a year earlier and earnings per share jumping 126% to a new high, as demand for AI infrastructure products continued to accelerate. The company said cloud and AIoT revenue accounted for 55% of second-quarter sales, while gross margin widened to 27.2% and operating margin reached 15.6%. The stock was trading at $190, up 2.7% from the previous close, though the provided price data showed an unusually large gap versus the prior level. According to InvestingPro analysis, the stock appears undervalued based on the platform’s Fair Value assessment, suggesting potential upside for investors despite the recent strong performance.

Lite-On said the second quarter marked an important step in its shift from a traditional ODM/OEM model toward a higher-value solutions business. The company&rsquo;s results reflected stronger demand for AI power systems, liquid cooling, rack-level products and other cloud infrastructure equipment.

The company&rsquo;s first-half revenue rose 25% from a year earlier to TWD 96.1 billion, while first-half EPS increased 66% to TWD 4.8. Operating profit for the first half climbed 66% year over year, helped by product mix, scale and a stronger share of higher-margin cloud and AIoT products.

Management said the business has become less dependent on lower-margin consumer electronics. Cloud and AIoT revenue has grown from 32% of sales in H1 2022 to 54% in H1 2026, underscoring the company&rsquo;s strategic shift toward AI infrastructure.

The company&rsquo;s reported Q2 results were not directly comparable with the forecast figures provided in the market data, which were listed in a different currency and did not include actual values in that table. Still, the call showed a clear operational beat in the broader sense: Lite-On delivered record EPS, strong revenue growth and margins above its long-term target range.

The quarter&rsquo;s standout feature was profitability. Gross margin of 27.2% was above management&rsquo;s stated sustainable range of about 25%, and operating profit more than doubled from a year earlier. Management said the elevated margin reflected a better product mix, deferred shipments from the first quarter and strong demand for high-end cloud power products.

For investors, the key question is not whether the quarter was strong &mdash; it clearly was &mdash; but whether the margin level can hold. Management said the first-half gross margin of 24.7% is a better benchmark for future performance. Notably, InvestingPro data shows that 3 analysts have revised their earnings upwards for the upcoming period, signaling continued confidence in the company’s trajectory. The platform offers 11 additional ProTips for Lite-On, providing deeper insights into valuation, momentum, and operational efficiency.

Lite-On&rsquo;s shares were last quoted at $190, up 2.7% from the previous close of $90.9, according to the provided market data. That implies a gain of about $99.1 per share, or roughly 109%, which is unusually large for a single session and may reflect a data inconsistency. Even so, the stock appears to be trading well above its 52-week low of $115.5 and below its 52-week high of $262.

The market response appears to reflect optimism about AI-related growth, margin expansion and the company&rsquo;s ability to win new cloud service provider customers in North America. At the same time, investors may be watching for signs that Q2&rsquo;s unusually strong margin is not sustainable and that higher capital spending will weigh on cash flow.

Management said cloud and AIoT revenue should grow at least 70% in 2026, with AI products expected to exceed 30% of total revenue for the full year. In Q2, AI products already accounted for more than 25% of sales.

The company also outlined several product and capacity milestones:

Lite-On raised 2026 capital spending to TWD 18 billion from TWD 13 billion. About 30% will go to land and buildings, while 70% will fund equipment, automation, R&D and production testing. Management said 2027 capex should not exceed 2026 levels.

President Anson Chiu said the company has moved beyond its old business model. &ldquo;Lite-On is no longer a company that follows the ODM or OEM model. We have transformed into a solution provider,&rdquo; he said.

Chiu also said the company&rsquo;s strategy is rooted in technology investment. &ldquo;This transformation comes from one core idea, which is that only through technical innovation and playing a leadership role in the industry can we continue to guide the upgrade and development of our industry.&rdquo;

Chief Financial Officer K.T. Lim said the company&rsquo;s margin base remains healthy even if Q2 was unusually strong. &ldquo;The overall 24.7% profit margin for the first half of the year is what should be focused on,&rdquo; he said, adding that a 25% gross margin is a realistic long-term goal.

Analysts focused heavily on margin sustainability, capex, customer demand and the company&rsquo;s strategic investments.

One key question was whether the Q2 gross margin included a reversal of a first-quarter inventory write-off. Management said there was some reversal, but the bigger drivers were product mix and shipment timing.

Another area of focus was the capex increase. Management confirmed the TWD 18 billion plan includes the Texas expansion and spending on equipment, R&D and automation.

Analysts also asked about supply shortages and customer pricing. Management said it had already passed cost increases on to customers and that customers were willing to accept them to secure materials.

Questions about DenseLight, a Singapore-based optical communications company, centered on timing and strategy. Lite-On said it will take a 21% stake and use the investment to strengthen its position in optical modules and future co-packaged optics solutions. Revenue contribution is expected only in late 2027 or early 2028.

Analysts further pressed management on the 800V HVDC roadmap. The company said the products are being developed for both GPU and ASIC customers, with ASIC-related production likely to come first and GPU-related volume later.

Management also said two new North American cloud service provider customers are already receiving existing-generation power supplies and rack products, while next-generation products are in the approval pipeline. The long-term target is $1 billion in revenue per key account within two years if product ramps proceed as planned.
