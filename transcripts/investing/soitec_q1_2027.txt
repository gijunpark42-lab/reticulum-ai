Soitec said first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue rose 23% from a year earlier to EUR 113 million, well above its own guidance for about 15% growth, as demand for its Photonic SOI products accelerated faster than expected. The French semiconductor materials maker also said the stock was trading at $59.75, up 9.09%, after the report. Management said Q2 revenue should rise more than 30% year over year, signaling that momentum is continuing as AI data center demand builds.

Soitec delivered a sharper-than-expected rebound in its first quarter, helped by rising demand for optical connectivity products used in AI infrastructure. The company has been working through inventory reduction, lower fab loading and weak smartphone demand, but the latest quarter showed that its growth engine in Edge & Cloud AI is gaining speed.

The company&rsquo;s performance was uneven across segments. Edge & Cloud AI led the quarter, while Mobile Communications stayed under pressure and Automotive & Industrial remained small, though it grew from a low base. The results suggest that Soitec is becoming more dependent on photonics-related demand, especially as AI data center operators expand their networks.

No EPS forecast or actual EPS figure was provided, so there was no earnings-per-share comparison to make.

On revenue, Soitec clearly outperformed its own guidance. The company had expected about 15% growth, but reported 23% growth at constant currency and scope. That is an 8 percentage point beat, or roughly 53% above the guided growth rate. For a company that has been managing inventory reduction and weak demand in parts of its business, the size of the beat is meaningful.

The result also appears stronger than recent trends in the more mature mobile market, where revenue fell 10% year over year. The main reason for the upside was the faster-than-expected ramp in Photonic SOI, which management said is now accelerating across multiple customer architectures.

Soitec&rsquo;s shares were up 9.09% to $59.75 after the update, according to the latest stock data. The move suggests investors focused on the growth acceleration in photonics and the stronger-than-expected quarterly revenue.

At $59.75, the stock remains well above its 52-week low of $13.13 and below its 52-week high of $114.37. That places it in the middle-to-upper part of its yearly range, reflecting both the recent recovery and the market&rsquo;s continuing caution about profitability and execution.

The stock data did not include volume, so it is not possible to confirm whether the move came on unusually heavy trading. Still, the size of the gain points to a positive reaction to the company&rsquo;s guidance and to the early qualification of its Singapore production site.

Soitec said Q2 fiscal 2027 revenue should rise more than 30% year over year, driven mainly by Photonic SOI. Management also said Photonic SOI revenue for the full year should more than double fiscal 2026 levels, implying revenue above $200 million if no major disruption hits the AI market.

The company expects:

Management said the company is not yet giving detailed guidance beyond fiscal 2027 because the photonics market remains dynamic. Still, it said customer capacity reservation agreements now extend beyond the current fiscal year, giving it better visibility.

Chief Executive Laurent R&eacute;mont said demand for Photonic SOI has accelerated faster than expected since the start of the second quarter. He said the company&rsquo;s action plan to increase production output has started to deliver results, giving Soitec &ldquo;materially greater visibility&rdquo; on the pace of the ramp.

R&eacute;mont also said the company achieved an important milestone by qualifying its Singapore 300mm SOI fab for high-volume Photonic SOI manufacturing with first customers. That matters because it reduces execution risk and supports the company&rsquo;s ability to meet rising demand.

Chief Financial Officer Albin Jacquemont said photonics sales carry a mix benefit because they contribute above the group average. He also said pricing should remain strong, especially given the high volumes expected.

Analysts focused heavily on Photonic SOI demand, capacity and margins. Several asked whether the strong growth outlook reflected only the Singapore fab qualification or a broader shift in demand. R&eacute;mont said three things had changed: customer demand accelerated, the company improved its ability to judge customer commitments, and execution improved through a dedicated photonics steering group.

Questions also centered on capacity. Management said there is no current bottleneck, only a &ldquo;lead time to peak,&rdquo; and that Soitec can expand further if needed. The company said the Singapore site can be extended and that it is starting to expand its V4 module in Bernin, France.

Analysts also asked about customer behavior and pre-buying. R&eacute;mont said demand is a mix of current use and preparation for next year, but not inventory building for warehouses. He said the company now has more than 10 customers in Photonic SOI and has negotiated multi-year reservation agreements with many of them.

Another topic was RF-SOI inventory digestion. Management said channel inventory has fallen to about 1.7 million wafer equivalents from roughly 2 million in March, but still needs to normalize further before the market returns to pre-COVID levels.
