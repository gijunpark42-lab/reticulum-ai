Siemens said third-quarter fiscal 2026 results were the strongest in its history, with record orders, higher revenue, improved margins and a sharp rise in free cash flow. The German industrial group also raised its full-year outlook, saying earnings before purchase-price allocation will now come in at EUR 11.20 billion to EUR 11.50 billion, up EUR 0.45 at the midpoint. Shares rose 3.82% to $36.11, leaving the stock at its 52-week high.

Siemens said it delivered &ldquo;another record third quarter&rdquo; despite a volatile geopolitical backdrop. The company&rsquo;s order book, revenue and cash generation all improved, while profitability expanded across key businesses.

The quarter showed strength in several areas at once. Smart Infrastructure posted record orders of EUR 8 billion, up 42%, helped by data-center and semiconductor projects. Mobility also had strong momentum, with orders of EUR 7.6 billion and a book-to-bill ratio of 2.35. Digital Industries, Siemens&rsquo; automation and software unit, reported 10% revenue growth and a sharp improvement in profitability.

The results suggest Siemens is benefiting from two major trends at the same time: industrial digitalization and the build-out of AI-related infrastructure. Management said nine of the world&rsquo;s top 10 data-center providers now rely on Siemens.

No consensus forecast was provided in the supplied data, so a direct comparison with analyst expectations is not available.

Even without a forecast benchmark, the quarter stands out as a strong one by Siemens&rsquo; own standards. The company described the period as record-setting across orders, profit and cash flow. The combination of 14% order growth, 8% revenue growth and a 40%-plus jump in free cash flow points to solid operating momentum.

The raised full-year outlook also suggests management sees the quarter as more than a one-off. Siemens increased its EPS pre-PPA guidance and said group revenue growth should finish in the upper half of its target range. That kind of revision usually signals confidence in the business mix and execution.

Siemens shares rose 3.82% to $36.11, matching the top of the stock&rsquo;s 52-week range of $24.25 to $36.11. The move suggests investors welcomed the stronger outlook and the company&rsquo;s record order intake.

The stock&rsquo;s position at the high end of its yearly range indicates that sentiment has improved in recent months. The gain also fits a broader market preference for industrial companies with exposure to AI infrastructure, electrification and automation. Siemens has been presenting itself as a beneficiary of all three.

No unusual trading volume was provided, so it is not possible to assess whether the move came with heavy turnover. Still, the price action points to a positive reaction to both current results and future demand visibility.

Chief Executive Roland Busch said, &ldquo;Siemens delivered another record third quarter with strong performance across all metrics, despite a persistently volatile geopolitical environment.&rdquo;

He also said the company is &ldquo;sharpening our image as the trusted technology partner for the AI age,&rdquo; underscoring Siemens&rsquo; effort to position itself as a key supplier for industrial AI and digital infrastructure.

On software, Busch said, &ldquo;The software business is moving fast, strategically, and in operations.&rdquo; He added that Siemens is &ldquo;democratizing our software away from scientists or physicists who are making models,&rdquo; suggesting the company wants broader use of its tools across industrial customers.

Chief Financial Officer Veronika Bienert said the company was prepared for the challenge of converting a large order book into revenue, noting that &ldquo;the respective ramp-up of capacity where needed is well underway.&rdquo;

- Digital Industries guidance for fourth-quarter margin was flat sequentially, which may limit near-term margin expansion.

Analysts focused on China, margins, software strategy and the pace of revenue conversion from Siemens&rsquo; large order backlog.

Questions about China centered on whether automation demand was slowing. Management said momentum dipped slightly in June but improved again in July, and that China orders in the value-for-money segment were growing 30% year to date.

Another topic was supply chains and margins in automation hardware. Siemens said it is no longer dealing with the severe semiconductor shortages seen after the pandemic, although some constraints remain. The company said pricing actions and productivity gains are offsetting cost pressure.

Analysts also pressed management on software monetization as AI changes how customers use design tools. Siemens said it is still focused on license and SaaS models, while keeping open the possibility of future usage-based pricing. The company said its software transition is largely complete and that AI should expand, not shrink, usage.

Questions on Smart Infrastructure focused on whether the company can convert its large data-center backlog into revenue. Siemens said capacity expansion is underway and that it has the operational base to handle the order surge.
