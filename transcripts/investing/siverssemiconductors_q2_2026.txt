Sivers Semiconductors said second-quarter revenue fell 12% from a year earlier, but the Swedish chip company pointed to rising product sales, a larger opportunity pipeline and new production orders as signs that its shift toward a product-led business is gaining traction. The shares rose 2.26% to $3.35 in recent trading, after closing at $3.28, though the stock has experienced remarkable volatility with a 1-year price return of 843% despite trading 67% below its 52-week high of $12.04. The company’s Financial Health score of 1.61 is rated as "WEAK" by InvestingPro, reflecting ongoing profitability challenges as it navigates its strategic transition.

Key Takeaways

Revenue fell to 53.8 million SEK from 61.4 million SEK a year earlier, as the company deliberately shifted resources away from non-recurring engineering work.Product revenue rose 13% year over year, or 18% at constant exchange rates, showing progress in the move toward recurring product sales.Adjusted EBITDA widened to a loss of 35.5 million SEK from a loss of 20.9 million SEK a year earlier.The company said its opportunity pipeline reached $1.2 billion in July, up 268% from the end of 2025.Sivers raised 825 million SEK in equity capital and converted a $12 million convertible loan into equity, strengthening its balance sheet.Management highlighted production ramps in wireless and photonics, including orders from ALL.SPACE, Tachyon Networks and a strategic LiDAR customer.

Company Performance

Sivers Semiconductors reported a mixed quarter. Headline revenue declined, but management said the drop was intentional and reflected a strategic reallocation of resources from development work to product ramps. The company is trying to move from a business driven by custom engineering toward one built around standard products and production orders.

That transition is visible in the numbers. Product revenue increased, even as total revenue fell. Management also said the company is entering a period in which several customer programs are moving closer to production, which could support revenue growth later in 2026 and into 2027.

The quarter also included a large non-cash expense tied to share-based incentive programs, which rose because of a sharp increase in the company’s share price during the period. Management said that charge did not affect cash flow.

Earnings vs. Forecast

No earnings or revenue forecast was provided for the quarter, so there is no direct comparison with analyst estimates. Based on the reported figures alone, the quarter was weaker on revenue and adjusted EBITDA than a year earlier, but management framed the results as part of a planned transition rather than a sign of operational weakness.

The most important trend was the gap between total revenue and product revenue. While overall sales declined, product revenue grew at a double-digit pace, suggesting that the company’s commercial mix is improving. That is important because product sales generally carry a better long-term profile than development work.

Market Reaction

Sivers shares were last quoted at $3.35, up 2.26% from the previous close of $3.28. The move was modest and suggested a cautious but constructive response from investors.

At the current price, the stock is far below its 52-week high of $12.04 and well above its 52-week low of $0.29. That places the shares in the lower part of their yearly range, even after the recent gain. Outlook & Guidance

Management did not provide formal earnings guidance, but it outlined several milestones it expects to drive the business over the next two years.

The company said it expects a revenue inflection in Q4 2026, followed by a broader product revenue inflection in 2027. Management also pointed to 2028 and beyond as the period when its longer-term financial model should become more visible.

Executive Commentary

Chief Executive Vickram Vathulya said the company is deliberately shifting resources toward production ramps.

“Our opportunity pipeline has expanded to $1.2 billion as of July 2026... a 268% increase from the end of 2025,” he said. “We have also identified $4 billion of new serviceable addressable market for our semiconductor optical amplifiers for use in optical circuit switches inside AI data centers.”

Chief Financial Officer Heine Thorsgaard said the company is making a planned trade-off between near-term revenue and future growth.

“We are deliberately transitioning resources from NRE projects towards upcoming product ramps,” he said. “While this affects near-term revenue, execution remains strong, product revenues are growing, and our pipeline development continues to accelerate.”

Thorsgaard also said the company’s balance sheet has improved after the capital raise and loan conversion, giving it more room to focus on execution rather than financing.

Risks and Challenges

Revenue remains dependent on customer qualification cycles, which can take time and may delay production orders.Adjusted EBITDA is still negative, so the company has not yet reached consistent profitability.The business is shifting away from NRE revenue, which can pressure near-term sales before product ramps scale.The company faces supply-chain and capacity challenges in indium phosphide, even though it says it has secured additional foundry support.A potential U.S. listing depends on market conditions and investor demand, so the timing is uncertain.The company’s pipeline is large, but it is non-binding and may not convert fully into revenue.

Q&A

Analysts asked about the pace of customer traction, capacity planning and the timing of future revenue.

Management said it is seeing active engagement in pluggables, near-pluggable optics and co-packaged optics, but is prioritizing faster routes to revenue. It also said the strategic LiDAR customer is the next likely source of a major production order.

On capacity, the company said it does not see production limits as a near-term bottleneck. It pointed to strong supplier relationships, a new foundry partner with significant available capacity and an expansion of its Glasgow facility.

Questions also focused on the planned U.S. listing. Management said the company expects to complete the required readiness work in H1 2027, after which the decision will depend on market conditions, investor demand and business momentum.

Analysts further asked about the company’s photonics strategy, including lasers, semiconductor optical amplifiers and co-packaged optics. Management said it is pursuing all three, while focusing on the fastest paths to revenue.
