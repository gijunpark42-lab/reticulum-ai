SCREEN Holdings Co. said demand is rebounding sharply in its semiconductor production equipment business, even as full-year revenue and operating income fell in the fiscal year ended March 2026. The Kyoto-based company reported record-high order intake in the fourth quarter of the prior year, with momentum continuing into the current first quarter and expected to extend into the second quarter. The stock rose 0.74% to &yen;14,970, near its 52-week high of &yen;15,720.

SCREEN Holdings said its fiscal 2026 results were mixed, with overall revenue and operating income declining from a year earlier. The main drag came from the SPE, or semiconductor production equipment, division, which saw lower sales and profit than in the prior fiscal year.

At the same time, the company described current demand conditions as strong. Orders reached a record level in the fourth quarter of the previous fiscal year and remained strong in the first quarter of the new year. Management expects that trend to continue into the second quarter, pointing to a possible recovery in coming periods.

The company&rsquo;s comments suggest that the weak reported year may not reflect current demand. Instead, SCREEN is in a heavy investment phase, with rising spending on labor, research and development, capital expenditure and depreciation. Management said those costs are part of a long-term growth plan.

Market capitalization: $17.6 billion.

Return on equity: 20% over the last twelve months.

Dividend yield: 1.56%, with the company maintaining dividend payments for 13 consecutive years according to InvestingPro data.

No consensus EPS or revenue forecast was provided alongside the results, so a direct beat-or-miss comparison is not available. The company did say, however, that the latest fiscal year was weaker than the year before, with lower revenue and operating income.

That said, the market may be looking past the reported year and focusing on the order trend. Management said order intake has been exceptionally strong, with the first quarter of the current fiscal year already above the fourth quarter of the prior year. In that sense, the earnings story appears less about the past year&rsquo;s decline and more about the strength of the pipeline ahead.

SCREEN shares rose 0.74% to &yen;14,970, up &yen;110 from the previous close of &yen;14,860. The stock is trading close to the top of its 52-week range of &yen;5,087.5 to &yen;15,720, suggesting investors have already priced in much of the company&rsquo;s recovery story.

The move was modest, which points to a measured reaction rather than a sharp repricing. Investors appeared to welcome the strong order commentary and the company&rsquo;s confidence in its capacity, but they also had to weigh the year-over-year decline in revenue and operating income and the higher cost base.

Management said SPE sales are expected to reach &yen;260 billion in the first half and &yen;340 billion in the second half of fiscal 2026, for a full-year total of &yen;600 billion. The company said current production capacity can support about double the second-half sales level, or roughly &yen;680 billion a year, and can absorb another 10% to 20% of market growth without new facilities.

SCREEN is also expanding its manufacturing footprint through layout changes and functional upgrades at its Hikone, Kyoto and Toyama facilities. A new site at Yasu is being prepared, with land leveling expected to take about a year. Management said a decision on fab construction must be made 6 to 12 months before production begins, with shipment capability likely requiring 1 to 1.5 years from the decision.

For advanced packaging, the company expects about &yen;100 billion in sales this fiscal year, with longer-term sales projected to reach &yen;400 billion to &yen;500 billion by March 2033. Management said the next midterm plan will focus less on aggressive investment and more on harvesting returns through higher profitability. InvestingPro assigns SCREEN a "GREAT" financial health score of 3.28 out of 5, reflecting strong fundamentals despite near-term margin pressure. For investors seeking deeper analysis, SCREEN is one of 1,400+ stocks covered by comprehensive Pro Research Reports, which transform complex data into clear, actionable intelligence.

&ldquo;For SPE, we received a very strong order intake,&rdquo; said Chiho Otobe, senior executive officer. &ldquo;In the fourth quarter of the previous year, we had a record high level of order intake. In the first quarter this year, we still see that same situation continues and expect this will continue into the second quarter.&rdquo;

Otobe also said the company has room to grow without immediate new construction. &ldquo;We expect JPY 340 billion in sales in the second half of the year. I think we can respond to the doubling of the sales with the current capacity,&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;If the market grows by 10% or 20% more, I think we can deal with it.&rdquo;

On capital spending and future margins, Otobe said the company is investing now to improve profitability later. &ldquo;In this period of mid-term management plan, we’re making the investment into the future growth,&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;In the next mid-term management plan period, we are expecting to harvest the fruit from this investment.&rdquo;

Analysts focused on fixed costs, capacity, China demand, pricing power and market share. One major topic was whether SCREEN&rsquo;s current facilities can support future growth without a new fab. Management said they can, at least for the near term, and that the company has room for 10% to 20% more market growth beyond current plans.

Another key issue was China. Management said demand from Chinese customers is stronger than it was at the January earnings call, but it remains skeptical of competitors&rsquo; forecasts for 20% to 30% growth in the market.

Analysts also asked about pricing. SCREEN said it does not want to compete through discounts and instead plans to improve average selling prices through better products, new functions and stronger development capability. Management said annual price talks usually happen in the October-to-December period, while day-to-day negotiations are more focused on new features than on base price increases.

Questions on market share centered on single wafer cleaners and batch cleaners. Management said some apparent weakness in fiscal 2025 was due to timing differences in revenue recognition, not a true loss of position. It expects share gains in fiscal 2026, helped by stronger FEOL demand and customer switches in BEOL applications.
