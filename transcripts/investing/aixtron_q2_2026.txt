AIXTRON SE said second-quarter revenue rose sharply from the first quarter as demand for optoelectronics accelerated, helping the semiconductor equipment maker reaffirm its full-year outlook. The German company reported Q2 revenue of EUR 115 million, in line with guidance, after EUR 59 million in the first quarter. The stock rose 6.22% to $35.415 in pre-market trading, suggesting investors welcomed the stronger order momentum and cash generation even as year-over-year sales remained lower.

Key Takeaways

Revenue nearly doubled sequentially, rising from EUR 59 million in Q1 to EUR 115 million in Q2.Optoelectronics drove the business, with management saying the market is in a broad investment wave tied to AI data centers.AIXTRON kept its full-year 2026 revenue guidance at EUR 560 million, plus or minus EUR 30 million.The company generated strong cash flow and ended June with EUR 816 million in liquidity.Shares gained 6.22% in pre-market trading, reflecting a positive early read on the call.

Company Performance

AIXTRON’s second quarter showed a clear recovery from a weak start to the year. Revenue of EUR 115 million was 16% below the same period a year earlier, but it was a strong step up from Q1 and matched the company’s own guidance. Gross profit reached EUR 47 million, with a gross margin of 41%, unchanged from Q2 2025.

The company’s performance was led by optoelectronics, where demand is being driven by the shift in AI data centers from copper-based connections to faster optical links. Management said the G10-AsP platform is increasingly becoming the standard choice for advanced photonic devices. That platform momentum helped offset continued weakness in power electronics, where silicon carbide remained soft and gallium nitride orders were still limited.

AIXTRON also benefited from a stable after-sales business and strong customer advance payments. Those advances, along with the collection of prior receivables, helped reduce working capital and strengthen cash flow.

Earnings vs. Forecast

The available forecast data pointed to revenue of $120.73 million and EPS of $0.1101, but the company did not provide directly comparable actual EPS and revenue in that format. On the call, AIXTRON reported Q2 revenue of EUR 115 million and said the result was in line with guidance.

That makes the quarter look like a solid meet rather than a clear beat or miss. The more important signal for investors was the sharp sequential rebound and the company’s confidence in its production ramp. Revenue more than doubled from Q1, while the company maintained full-year guidance and described order momentum as strong.

The call also suggested that the business is improving operationally, even if the year-over-year comparison remains negative. For a cyclical equipment maker, that combination often matters more than a small shortfall or beat against near-term expectations.

Market Reaction

AIXTRON shares rose 6.22% to $35.415 in pre-market trading, up from the previous close of $33.34. That move added about $2.08 per share. The stock remains well above its 52-week low of $11.675, though still below the 52-week high of $62.66.

The early gain suggests investors focused on the company’s strong order intake, improving cash flow and visible production ramp rather than the still-negative year-over-year revenue comparison. The stock’s move was also notable because management did not raise full-year guidance, which often tempers enthusiasm. Even so, the market appeared to take comfort in the strength of the optoelectronics cycle and the company’s ability to execute.

Outlook & Guidance

AIXTRON kept its full-year 2026 revenue guidance at EUR 560 million, plus or minus EUR 30 million. It also reaffirmed expectations for a gross margin of about 42% and an EBIT margin between 17% and 20%.

Management said Q3 revenue should reach about EUR 180 million, plus or minus EUR 20 million, with Q4 expected to be even stronger. The company described the ramp as steep, with quarterly output rising from EUR 59 million in Q1 to an estimated level above EUR 200 million in Q4.

The company also kept its capital spending outlook at about EUR 55 million for 2026, including investment in a new site in Penang, Malaysia. Management said the facility is on track and should support future production growth.

Looking beyond 2026, AIXTRON said it expects Q1 2027 to be strong, likely near Q4 2026 levels. It also said gross margin should improve further in 2027, helped by a richer product mix and better fixed-cost absorption.

Executive Commentary

Chief Executive Felix Grawert said the main driver of demand remains the shift in AI data centers toward faster optical links.

“The key driver remains the ongoing architectural shift in AI data centers,” he said, adding that the market is moving “towards high speed optical connectivity at 800G, and eventually 1.6T and beyond.”

Grawert also stressed that AIXTRON is not constrained by demand, but by how quickly it can scale operations.

“It’s a ramp rate constraint. It’s not a capacity,” he said. “What I consider capacity is how fast can we hire people on board, how fast can our suppliers hire people to operate the machines.”

On profitability, he said the company expects better margins as laser systems take a larger share of sales.

“The laser systems carry of course a bit of a better margin profile,” he said, citing the complexity of the products and customer-specific differentiation.

Risks and Challenges

Execution risk: Management said the main challenge is ramp rate, not demand, which means hiring, training and supplier coordination remain critical.Weak power electronics markets: Silicon carbide and gallium nitride are still at low levels, limiting diversification.Cyclical exposure: Revenue remains tied to semiconductor equipment spending, which can swing quickly.Geopolitical uncertainty: The company is watching developments in the Middle East for possible effects on energy, supply chains and demand.Margin pressure from expansion: Malaysia ramp costs and higher R&D spending could weigh on near-term profitability.

Q&A

Analysts focused heavily on the optoelectronics boom, the pace of the production ramp and the timing of a recovery in power electronics.

Several questions centered on whether AIXTRON’s strong order intake means demand is now ahead of supply. Grawert said the issue is not capacity in the usual sense, but how fast the company can scale operations and suppliers.

Others asked about gallium nitride in data centers. Management said the opportunity remains intact, but the timing is still uncertain. Grawert said the market has underestimated how long the transition will take, and that the next wave is likely to follow the silicon carbide phase with a delay.

Analysts also asked whether the company’s optoelectronics momentum could plateau. Management said it now expects around EUR 200 million in quarterly order intake for the coming quarters, suggesting the current wave may still have room to run.

Questions about gross margin pointed to a likely improvement in 2027, though management stopped short of giving a precise target. The company said product mix, fixed-cost absorption and higher volumes should help, but it does not expect margins to reach 50% soon.
