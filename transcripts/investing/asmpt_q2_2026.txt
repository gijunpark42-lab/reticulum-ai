ASMPT said first-half 2026 revenue and bookings rose sharply as demand from AI servers, advanced packaging and photonics continued to build, while the company guided for another solid quarter ahead. The Hong Kong-listed semiconductor equipment maker reported H1 revenue of $1.14 billion, up 42.5% year on year, and bookings of $1.63 billion, up 85.1%. In Q2, revenue reached $630 million, above the top end of guidance, and adjusted gross margin climbed to 42.5%. The stock was last unchanged at $139.5, leaving it about 114.9% above its 52-week low but 42.9% below its 52-week high. ASMPT delivered a strong first half, with growth coming from both of its main businesses, SEMI and SMT. Management said the company benefited from higher demand for advanced packaging tools, mainstream bonding equipment and photonics solutions tied to AI infrastructure.

The company&rsquo;s book-to-bill ratio reached 1.43, the highest since the first half of 2021. That suggests customers are placing orders faster than ASMPT is shipping products, a sign that demand remains healthy.

The results also showed a broadening of end-market exposure. Computer applications became the largest revenue source, accounting for about 33% of group sales in H1, up from around 10% in 2024. China accounted for 42% of revenue, supported by wire bonding and die bonding tools as well as stronger AI-related spending.

The supplied forecast table showed expected revenue of $4.45 billion and expected EPS of 0.8682, but it did not include matching actual figures for the same reporting basis. As a result, a direct beat-or-miss calculation against that forecast is not possible from the available numbers.

Even so, the operating update was clearly strong. Q2 revenue came in above the top end of guidance, and bookings were far ahead of revenue, which points to continued demand into the second half. The company also said Q3 revenue guidance exceeds current market consensus, which suggests management sees momentum continuing.

ASMPT shares were last at $139.5, unchanged from the previous close. The flat move suggests investors were not surprised enough to push the stock sharply higher, even after a strong operating update.

At the current price, the stock sits near the middle of its 52-week range, well above the low of $64.95 but still far below the high of $244.4. Without volume data, there is no sign here of unusual trading activity.

The muted reaction may reflect a familiar pattern in semiconductor equipment stocks: investors often look past a single strong quarter and focus instead on how long the demand cycle can last. In ASMPT&rsquo;s case, the market appears to be weighing strong AI-related growth against supply-chain delays and the timing of future product ramps.

Management guided for Q3 2026 revenue of $630 million to $690 million. At the midpoint, that would imply growth of 4.8% sequentially and 46.3% year on year, which the company said is above market consensus.

Bookings are expected to rise by a high single-digit percentage sequentially, led by SEMI products, especially TCB and photonics. SMT bookings may ease from Q2&rsquo;s record level, but management said they should remain elevated and still grow year on year.

Gross margin is expected to stay stable in Q3. The company said SEMI margins should continue to benefit from a favorable mix of TCB, photonics and high-end die bonders, while SMT margins should remain in the mid-30% range at current volume levels. Overall group margin is expected to stay above 40% through Q3 and for three quarters ahead.

Management also said full-year 2026 revenue should continue to grow across both SEMI and SMT, despite longer material lead times and shifting AI product schedules. Robin Ng, group chief executive, said the company is sharpening its focus on back-end packaging after several years of rapid growth. He added: &ldquo;I&rsquo;m proud of what we have achieved as a business. I look forward to the company progressing on its transformation journey as it sharpens its focus on the back-end packaging business.&rdquo;

Ng also pointed to the broader AI trend, saying semiconductor requirements are expanding beyond compute into planning, orchestration, memory access and real-time data movement. That theme helped explain demand for ASMPT&rsquo;s advanced packaging and photonics tools.

On photonics, Ng said: &ldquo;We have built a strong position across the entire photonics value chain, extending from pluggable optical transceivers to CPO.&rdquo; He added that ASMPT&rsquo;s tools can deliver placement accuracy down to the submicron level, which he described as a key differentiator.

Analysts focused on the timing of future growth drivers, especially memory TCB, chip-to-wafer TCB and photonics.

One question centered on memory TCB shipments and the timing of the 50-plus C2S tool orders announced in July. Management said most of those tools should ship in Q1 2027, though some could be delivered in the second half of 2026.

Another topic was photonics and the shift from pluggable optical transceivers to co-packaged optics. Management said pluggable products remain the larger business today, while CPO is still small and likely to inflect in 2027-2028, more likely 2028.

Analysts also asked about chip-to-wafer TCB and whether next-generation GPUs will move to chiplet architectures. Management said 2027 should be a more meaningful year for chip-to-wafer demand than 2026.

Questions on China growth and hybrid bonding drew similar answers: China&rsquo;s mainstream business remains strong, while advanced packaging there is still smaller than in other regions. Hybrid bonding remains in sampling, with a likely inflection point in 2029-2030, or possibly later.
