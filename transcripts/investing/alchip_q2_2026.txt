Alchip Technologies said second-quarter earnings edged past Wall Street expectations as revenue surged on the ramp of its N3 AI accelerator program, while the company pointed to record July sales and more growth ahead. The chip designer reported earnings per share of $20.01, slightly above the $19.91 forecast, and said revenue rose 82.6% from the prior quarter to $241.7 million. The stock was up 0.48% at $4,210, near the upper half of its 52-week range, as investors weighed strong demand against lower margins and a still-concentrated customer base.

Key Takeaways

Alchip posted a small EPS beat, with $20.01 in earnings per share versus a $19.91 forecast.Revenue jumped 82.6% sequentially to $241.7 million, helped by the early ramp of its N3 AI accelerator program.Gross margin fell to about 35% from 50% in the first quarter, reflecting a shift from higher-margin engineering work to production revenue.Management said July revenue reached a company record and expects more sequential growth in the third and fourth quarters.The company described its next-generation N2 program as a much larger opportunity, with a long-term market scale it compared with $80 billion to $100 billion.

Company Performance

Alchip delivered a strong quarter by its own standards, with revenue and profit both rising sharply from the first quarter. The company said the main driver was the ramp of its N3 AI accelerator, which began mass production in late May and started shipping in June. Even with only one month of revenue contribution, the program helped push quarterly sales higher by more than 80%.

Net income rose to $51.8 million, up 14.9% from the prior quarter and 20.7% from a year earlier. Operating income climbed to $52.1 million. The company said the business is entering a new growth phase as production revenue expands and next-generation projects move forward.

Still, the year-over-year comparison was less flattering. Revenue fell 18.7% from the same period last year, showing that the current growth is coming on top of a difficult base and a changing mix of business. Alchip said the decline in gross margin was expected because production work carries lower margins than nonrecurring engineering services.

Earnings vs. Forecast

Alchip beat the EPS forecast by $0.10 per share, or about 0.5%. That is a modest surprise, not a large one. The more important story was the scale of the revenue ramp and the company’s confidence that the growth is still early.

The quarter’s performance was stronger than the headline EPS beat suggests. Revenue rose sharply as the N3 program moved into production, and management said the company had already reached a record monthly revenue level in July. The company also said it expects another sequential increase in the fourth quarter.

Compared with earlier quarters, the beat was not dramatic, but the underlying business mix is changing quickly. Investors appeared to focus more on the trajectory of production revenue, the margin reset, and the long runway for the N2 program than on the small earnings surprise itself.

Market Reaction

Alchip shares were up 0.48% to $4,210, compared with the previous close of $4,190. The stock remains well above its 52-week low of $2,480 and below its 52-week high of $5,640.

The move suggests a restrained but positive response. The market appears to be acknowledging the strong operational momentum, while also keeping an eye on margin pressure and customer concentration. With no large earnings surprise and no major guidance raise for the current year’s N3 contribution, the stock’s reaction was measured rather than sharp.

Outlook & Guidance

Management said it expects revenue and earnings to hit record levels in the third quarter, followed by another sequential increase in the fourth quarter. The company described the current period as the start of a new growth phase, driven by the N3 accelerator ramp and a broader pipeline of AI and CPU programs.

For margins, the company guided third- and fourth-quarter gross margin to the low-to-mid 20% range, with full-year gross margin also expected in the low-to-mid 20s. The shift reflects a heavier mix of production revenue, which is less profitable than engineering work.

Looking further ahead, Alchip said the N2 accelerator program is on track for tape-out at the end of 2024, with production ramp targeted for the end of 2025. Management described the N2 opportunity as much larger than N3, with higher pricing and higher shipment volume.

The company also said its automotive ADAS business remains strong, while two ARM-based CPU programs are in mass production. Management said it is highly confident in winning another major hyperscaler customer in the near future.

Executive Commentary

“Our second quarter revenue came in line with the plan,” Chairman and Chief Executive John Shen said. “Revenue increased significantly quarter-over-quarter due to higher production revenue, while the NRE remained very strong.”

Shen said the N3 program is still early in its ramp. “Even with a month’s revenue contribution, it drove our quarter-over-quarter revenue growth up more than 80%,” he said. He added that July revenue reached a record high, which he said points to continued momentum.

Chief Financial Officer Daniel said the margin profile is likely to settle lower as production revenue grows. “For the third quarter and the fourth quarter, the blended gross margin will most likely be about 20 something percent,” he said. “For the whole year, this year, I think our blended gross margin should be in the low to mid-twenties.”

Risks and Challenges

Margin pressure: As production revenue becomes a larger share of sales, gross margin is expected to fall from the first-quarter level.Customer concentration: A large share of revenue comes from a small number of hyperscaler customers, which increases dependence on a few programs.Execution risk: The N2 program uses a more complex chiplet design and requires multiple tape-outs, which raises technical and schedule risk.Visibility limits: Several key projects are under strict customer confidentiality, making it harder for investors to judge timing and scale.Competitive pressure: Management said production-only business can become a “bloody battlefield” on price, especially if customers move toward more in-house design.

Q&A

Analysts focused on three main issues: margins, the N2 ramp, and customer concentration. Questions centered on how quickly gross margin could recover as N3 production scales, whether N2 could be even more profitable, and how much business might come from new hyperscaler customers.

Management said the N2 design is heterogeneous and will require multiple tape-outs, with packaging and interconnect work handled by Alchip. Shen said the company is the sole design partner for the project and that “everything is done by us” for the compute die, I/O die, and packaging integration.

Analysts also pressed on the company’s move toward a chip-on-wafer or COT model, in which customers do more front-end design and Alchip provides design and packaging support. Shen said the trend is already underway and will likely spread across the industry as volumes grow. He said Alchip’s design capability, schedule control, and advanced packaging experience give it an edge.

Another topic was the company’s ability to diversify away from its largest customer. Shen said revenue concentration remains a reality in the ASIC market, but he argued that the company has a broad customer base in terms of count and is working to win more large programs. He also said the company expects more demand from automotive, robotics, and CPU applications.
