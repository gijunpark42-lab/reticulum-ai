X-FAB reported second-quarter 2026 revenue of $199.8 million, down 7% from a year earlier but up 2% from the first quarter, as the semiconductor manufacturer said demand conditions were improving after a weak stretch in automotive and industrial markets. The company did not disclose EPS or a direct earnings comparison with Wall Street forecasts, but revenue came in just above the $199.63 million estimate. Shares rose 7.11% to $6.78 in after-hours trading, from a previous close of $6.33, as investors responded to signs of a broader recovery and stronger second-half guidance.

X-FAB said the quarter reflected disciplined execution in a mixed market. Revenue declined from a year earlier because automotive demand remained soft and industrial sales were volatile, but the company saw better signs in bookings and in several growth areas.

Automotive revenue fell 19% year over year to $116 million, though book-to-bill in the segment reached its highest level in almost two years. Industrial revenue dropped 4% from a year earlier to $45.2 million. Medical revenue, by contrast, rose 39% to $21 million, helped by pacemaker and ultrasound applications.

The company also pointed to growth in newer businesses. Microsystems and photonics revenue rose 14% to $28.7 million, while wide bandgap revenue increased 34% to $10.6 million. Management said these areas support its strategy of focusing on specialized technologies with longer-term growth potential.

Wall Street expected X-FAB to report a loss of $0.02 per share and revenue of $199.63 million. The company&rsquo;s reported revenue of $199.8 million was about $0.17 million higher than forecast, a difference of roughly 0.1%.

The revenue result was effectively in line with expectations. The company did not provide a reported EPS figure in the materials reviewed, so a direct EPS surprise could not be measured. Still, the quarter appears to have met the market&rsquo;s broad expectations rather than delivering a clear earnings beat.

What mattered more to investors was the tone of the call. Management said Q2 likely marked the bottom and that the second half of 2026 should be stronger than the first half. The return of underlying book-to-bill above 1.0, excluding the Erfurt end-of-life program, also suggested that demand may be stabilizing after a long downturn.

X-FAB shares rose 7.11% to $6.78 in after-hours trading, compared with a previous close of $6.33. The stock remains well below its 52-week high of $15.88, but it is also comfortably above the 52-week low of $4.09.

The move suggests investors focused less on the small revenue difference versus forecasts and more on the improving outlook. For the third quarter, X-FAB guided for revenue of $195 million to $205 million and an EBITDA margin of 17% to 20%, based on an average exchange rate of 1.14 dollars per euro. Management said the guidance does not include the impact of IFRS 15 revenue recognition.

The company said it believes Q2 was the low point and expects a gradual recovery rather than a quick rebound. Management also said it has enough capacity for the next two to three years based on current growth projections, while capacity utilization should gradually rise toward 85% to 90%.

Analysts focused on capacity, financing, pricing, and the company&rsquo;s data center opportunity.

One question centered on when X-FAB could reach fuller capacity utilization. Management said it is targeting a gradual move toward 85% to 90%, with enough capacity for the next two to three years under current projections.

Another analyst asked about the company&rsquo;s $300 million long-term data center opportunity. Management said current data center revenue is around $20 million to $30 million, mostly from silicon carbide, photonics, and CMOS, but declined to give a precise timeline for reaching the larger target.

Questions also covered financing. Management said the EUR 200 million revolving credit facility has been extended by one year, but that this alone will not be enough for future needs. The company is considering Schuldschein and other funding options.

Analysts also asked whether X-FAB could raise prices. Management said it is not ready to announce broad price increases, though it is watching wafer supply conditions closely, especially for SOI wafers and 200mm wafers. The company said it would pass through higher input costs where appropriate.
