Ibiden Co. reported stronger-than-expected first-quarter results for fiscal 2026, with earnings per share of 64.14 versus analysts&rsquo; forecast of 53.17 and revenue of 123.22 billion yen, topping estimates of 116.47 billion yen. The company also raised its full-year outlook, citing robust demand for AI server substrates, high-end CPUs and GPUs, while its shares jumped 23.12% to $20,105 from the previous close of $16,330.

Ibiden said first-quarter revenue reached 123.2 billion yen, while operating profit came to 26.9 billion yen. The electronics segment generated 75.5 billion yen in revenue and 21.4 billion yen in operating profit, supported by strong demand for GPU and high-end CPU products.

Management said the quarter benefited from stabilized production at Cell 8, better utilization at existing plants and stronger selling prices for high-value products. The company also said its business mix is shifting beyond GPUs to include switching ICs, high-end CPUs and general-purpose servers.

The results extend a run of strong performance for the Japanese substrate maker, which has been benefiting from the global buildout of AI infrastructure. Demand for advanced packaging materials remains tight across the industry, giving suppliers more room to raise prices or limit discounting.

Ibiden&rsquo;s earnings beat was broad-based. EPS came in 10.97 above the forecast, a surprise of 20.63%. Revenue exceeded estimates by 6.75 billion yen, or 5.8%.

The larger EPS beat suggests that pricing, product mix and operating leverage were stronger than analysts expected. Revenue also came in ahead of plan, but the profit outperformance was more striking, indicating that the company converted sales into earnings efficiently.

The result fits Ibiden&rsquo;s recent pattern of benefiting from tight supply in advanced substrates and strong demand from AI-related customers. The size of the beat is meaningful and should reinforce the view that the company&rsquo;s earnings momentum remains strong.

Ibiden&rsquo;s shares rose 23.12% to $20,105 in after-hours trading, up from the previous close of $16,330. The stock is now much closer to the upper end of its 52-week range of $3,216 to $27,480.

The move suggests investors welcomed both the earnings beat and the raised outlook. A gain of this size is unusual for a single session and points to strong confidence in the company&rsquo;s pricing power and demand outlook.

The stock&rsquo;s reaction also indicates that investors may be focusing less on the quarter alone and more on the possibility that AI-related demand and supply shortages can support earnings for longer than expected.

Ibiden raised its full-year revenue forecast to 550 billion yen from 500 billion yen and lifted operating profit guidance to 127 billion yen from 90 billion yen. The company also increased its electronics revenue outlook to 375 billion yen and electronics operating profit forecast to 110 billion yen.

Management said demand for AI server substrates, GPUs and high-end CPUs should remain strong, while supply shortages are expected to continue. The company said current selling prices should at least hold at present levels, with further improvement possible.

Ibiden also said it expects to decide on additional capacity investments before the end of fiscal 2026. It is evaluating expansion options at its Gama and Ono sites, including possible new production lines and possibly a separate plant building. Despite the high P/E ratio of 92, the company’s PEG ratio of 0.8 suggests reasonable valuation relative to its growth prospects, with analysts forecasting 26% revenue growth for fiscal 2027. The company said Cell 6 and Cell 8 are about 50% through equipment ordering and are expected to begin operations in fiscal 2027, with positive business contribution expected in fiscal 2028. Small-lot production for EMIB-T is expected in the second half of fiscal 2027, with mass production at Gama targeted for fiscal 2028.

Shinji Miyazaki, a director and senior executive officer, said the main reason for the higher forecast was strong demand for GPU and high-end CPU products. He said Cell 8 production had stabilized and plant utilization had improved, helping sales volume exceed initial plans.

He also said the biggest drivers were pricing and product mix. &ldquo;The biggest drivers are the ASP and sales volumes. Overall, ASP and product mix will have a positive impact of JPY 26.5 billion,&rdquo; he said.

On the outlook, Miyazaki said demand should stay above industry supply. &ldquo;The situation where demand exceeds the industry supply capacity is expected to continue,&rdquo; he said, adding that selling prices, especially for high-end products, are expected to remain strong.

Analysts focused heavily on pricing, capacity and new technology development.

Questions centered on the JPY 26.5 billion improvement in ASP and product mix, with management saying most of the gain came from pricing rather than mix alone. Executives said customers were accepting fewer price cuts, and in some cases no cuts at all, because of industry-wide shortages.

Analysts also asked about future capacity investments. Ibiden said expansion is needed across the business, not just for one product line, and that it is considering new capacity at Gama and Ono.

Another major topic was EMIB-T, an advanced packaging technology. Management said development is progressing, yield is improving and no major technical blockers have appeared. Small-lot production is expected in late fiscal 2027, with mass production in fiscal 2028.

Questions about glass cloth supply and advance payments from customers also drew attention. Ibiden said supply risks have eased and that it continues to discuss advance payments as a way to help fund future capacity.
