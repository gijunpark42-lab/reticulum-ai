KOKUSAI ELECTRIC reported record first-quarter revenue for fiscal 2027, helped by strong demand for semiconductor manufacturing equipment used in DRAM, NAND and logic foundry production. Revenue rose 46% from a year earlier to JPY 75.4 billion, topping the JPY 73.08 billion forecast, while orders jumped to about JPY 140 billion. The stock fell 3.11% to $8,224 in recent trading, after closing at $8,488, leaving it well above its 52-week low but below its peak for the year.

KOKUSAI ELECTRIC said its first quarter ended June 30 was the strongest in its history, with sales and orders both showing sharp gains. Adjusted operating income rose 59% from a year earlier to JPY 17.4 billion, while gross profit margin was 40.7%.

The company benefited from broad-based demand across its main product lines. DRAM equipment sales rose 158% from a year earlier, logic foundry sales increased 82%, and NAND sales, while down 34% from a year earlier, climbed 84% sequentially.

Management said the business is being supported by AI-related investment, especially in high-performance memory and advanced logic. The company also said non-China sales rose 75% year over year, while China sales fell 3% from a year earlier but improved 18% from the prior quarter.

KOKUSAI ELECTRIC&rsquo;s revenue beat the forecast by JPY 2.32 billion, or about 3.2%, based on the reported JPY 75.4 billion in sales versus the JPY 73.08 billion estimate. EPS was not provided in the supplied data, so a direct earnings-per-share comparison is not available.

The size of the revenue beat was not huge, but it came with a much more important signal: orders were far stronger than expected, and management lifted full-year guidance. That combination usually matters more to investors than a modest quarterly revenue surprise.

The quarter also extended the company&rsquo;s recent growth trend. Revenue was up 46% from a year earlier, showing that demand remains strong after a period of rapid expansion in semiconductor equipment spending.

The stock fell 3.11% to $8,224, down $264 from the previous close of $8,488. The move suggests investors were cautious despite the strong quarter and higher guidance.

At the current price, the shares are about 33.7% below the 52-week high of $12,220 and about 215.8% above the 52-week low of $2,607. That places the stock in the middle of a wide trading range, reflecting both strong long-term performance and recent volatility. No trading volume data was provided, so it is not possible to determine whether the move came with unusual activity. Still, a decline after a record quarter may indicate that expectations were already high, especially given the stock&rsquo;s large run-up from its low.

Management raised full-year fiscal 2027 guidance after the strong first quarter. Revenue is now expected to reach JPY 340 billion, up 21% from the prior forecast and 45% from a year earlier. Adjusted operating income was lifted to JPY 86 billion, and adjusted net income was raised to JPY 60 billion.

The company also increased its dividend outlook. The annual dividend is now expected to be JPY 65 per share, up JPY 18 from the earlier plan. The interim dividend was raised to JPY 32 and the year-end dividend to JPY 33.

KOKUSAI ELECTRIC said it expects calendar 2026 wafer fab equipment market growth to exceed 25%, or around $140 billion, up from an earlier estimate of 15% growth and $120 billion to $125 billion. For calendar 2027, the company expects the market to grow about 20%, with the possibility of further upside.

The company also highlighted several growth areas:

Chief Executive Kazunori Tsukada said the company now expects to hit its JPY 330 billion revenue target by March 2027, &ldquo;two years ahead of schedule.&rdquo;

Chief Financial Officer Yoshitaka Kawakami said, &ldquo;Revenue of JPY 75.4 billion set a new record high for a single quarter,&rdquo; and added that orders were &ldquo;particularly significant for DRAM for China, as well as for NAND sold to non-China and China.&rdquo;

Tsukada also said pricing and product mix should help margins, noting that &ldquo;pricing change is one important factor for margin improvement&rdquo; and that NAND capex is expected to become stronger.

Analysts focused on three main areas: the sustainability of demand, production capacity, and pricing.

Questions centered on whether the current surge in China was temporary or durable. Management said demand in China is strong across logic foundry, DRAM and NAND, and described it as a trend that should continue through at least this fiscal year and next.

Analysts also pressed the company on whether it could support more sales without building a new plant. Management said it can expand by increasing shifts, hiring production engineers and using existing facilities more efficiently. The company said it is already converting warehouse space at the Tonami plant and has acquired adjacent land for future expansion.

Another focus was pricing. Management said it is trying to pass through higher material and labor costs, but does not see true dynamic pricing as practical. Instead, it plans to use product-generation pricing, where newer products command higher value.
