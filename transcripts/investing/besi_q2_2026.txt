Besi reported sharply higher second-quarter profit and revenue on strong demand for hybrid bonding, AI-related packaging and traditional assembly tools, but the stock fell 6.37% to $231.4 in premarket trading as investors weighed the company&rsquo;s upbeat long-term outlook against softer near-term margin guidance and broader cycle risks. The Dutch semiconductor equipment maker said Q2 revenue rose 68.7% year over year to EUR 249.9 million, while net income climbed 177.3% to EUR 89 million. Management also said orders hit EUR 292.9 million, up 128.8% from a year earlier.

Besi delivered a strong quarter by nearly every operating measure. Revenue, profit and orders all grew at a fast pace, and the company said it continued to gain traction in hybrid bonding, co-packaged optics, data center power management and high-end smartphone applications.

The quarter showed clear operating leverage. Operating expenses fell to 18.9% of revenue from 30% a year earlier, even as the company increased spending on development and customer support. That helped lift net income and margins.

The company&rsquo;s first-half results were also strong. Revenue for H1 2026 rose 48.8% to EUR 434.7 million, while net income increased 121.1% to EUR 140.6 million. Orders for the first half climbed 116.5% to EUR 562.6 million.

Management said the business is benefiting from a mix of AI infrastructure spending, a recovery in mobile demand from a weak 2025 base, and growing interest in advanced packaging technologies used in data centers and memory.

The company&rsquo;s provided forecast data showed analyst expectations for EPS of $1.08 and revenue of $246.66 million, but no actual EPS or revenue figure was included in that dataset. That means a formal beat-or-miss calculation cannot be made from the forecast table alone.

Even so, the operating results reported on the call were strong. Q2 revenue of EUR 249.9 million was well above the revenue forecast figure in the provided data, though the currencies are not directly comparable. The company also posted sharp year-over-year gains in profit, margins and orders, which suggests the quarter was materially stronger than a typical in-line result.

The absence of a clear EPS comparison may have limited the market&rsquo;s ability to reward the stock immediately, especially if investors were focused on guidance and valuation after a strong run.

Besi shares fell 6.37% to $231.4 from a previous close of $247.15. That is a decline of about $15.75 per share. The stock traded well above its 52-week low of $105.45, but still below the 52-week high of $333.1.

The move suggests investors were not fully satisfied with the near-term setup, even though the company posted strong growth. In premarket trading, markets often react quickly to guidance, margins and order trends rather than only headline revenue growth. Besi&rsquo;s Q3 gross margin outlook of 63% to 65% points to some moderation from the very strong Q2 mix, which may have added pressure on the shares.

No trading volume data was provided, so it is not possible to confirm whether the decline came with unusually heavy activity.

Besi said it expects Q3 2026 revenue to rise 10% to 15% sequentially, which would imply EUR 275 million to EUR 287 million. Management said the forecast is based on backlog and customer feedback showing continued order momentum. Looking at the full year, analysts project revenue growth of 62% for fiscal 2026, reflecting confidence in the company’s AI and advanced packaging exposure. The company also guided for gross margin of 63% to 65%, down from Q2 levels because of a less favorable product mix. Operating expenses are expected to be flat to up 5% from Q2, mainly because of higher development spending.

Management said it expects continued orders for hybrid bonding in the third quarter and believes the second half of 2026 will help clarify which memory applications adopt the technology and at what scale. The company also expects more orders for its AP7 capacity expansion in Taiwan, where first systems are being installed.

Longer term, Besi pointed to several growth areas:

Chief Executive Richard Blickman said the company saw strong demand across both traditional and advanced packaging lines.

&ldquo;Besi reported strong second quarter and first half-year 2026 results as favorable order momentum continued for both our traditional and wafer-level assembly systems,&rdquo; he said.

He also pointed to the company&rsquo;s growing exposure to AI-related demand. &ldquo;Overall, we estimate that system orders for AI applications rose to approximately 60% in the first half of this year, versus approximately 50% in the first half of 2025 last year,&rdquo; he said.

On hybrid bonding, Blickman said adoption is broadening. &ldquo;Hybrid bonding customers adoption increased from 15 at year-end 2025 to 21 at the end of Q2 2026,&rdquo; he said, adding that use cases now include logic, memory, co-packaged optics and consumer applications.

He also said Besi believes it is well positioned in the market. &ldquo;Besi, with its hybrid bonding technology, together with Applied Materials in automated line concepts, is at the forefront of this technology being used in the memory,&rdquo; he said.

Analysts focused heavily on hybrid bonding, HBM adoption and the company&rsquo;s unusual Q3 growth outlook.

One key topic was whether the order strength was tied to memory. Blickman said hybrid bonding orders should continue in Q3 and that the second half of 2026 will help show which HBM applications adopt the technology. He said the company is working with Applied Materials on automated line concepts for memory applications.

Analysts also asked about the hyperscaler order mentioned in the quarter. Blickman said it likely relates to wearables such as smart glasses, though it may also connect to co-packaged optics or other logic applications.

Another major question was the company&rsquo;s Q3 guidance, which implies sequential growth at a time when semiconductor equipment companies often see seasonal weakness. Blickman said the pattern is changing as the business shifts from consumer and smartphone exposure toward data center applications, which have different seasonal trends.

Analysts also pressed the company on overcapacity risk. Blickman said the industry often sees overcapacity after six to eight quarters of strong growth, but added that Besi can adjust operations and supply chains quickly as demand changes.

On the new AI power management business, management said the company only highlights areas it believes can become meaningful mainstream contributors. It said multiple customers are involved and that power is becoming a more important part of the data center module market.
