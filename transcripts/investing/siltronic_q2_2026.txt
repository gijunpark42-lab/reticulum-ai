Siltronic AG said second-quarter sales rose as wafer demand improved, but the German supplier of silicon wafers continued to face pricing pressure and a net loss. The company reported sales of EUR 322 million, up 4.9% from EUR 307 million in the first quarter, while EBITDA increased to EUR 69 million from EUR 65 million. The net loss narrowed to EUR 63 million from EUR 67 million. In premarket trading, the stock was up 1.62% at $69.05, compared with a previous close of $67.95.

Siltronic&rsquo;s second quarter showed a gradual recovery rather than a sharp rebound. Sales increased 4.9% from the first quarter, driven mainly by higher wafer volumes. EBITDA also improved, while the net loss narrowed slightly. EBIT was unchanged at minus EUR 52 million, showing that higher sales were partly offset by depreciation and other costs.

The company said demand is improving across the wafer market, especially in 300-millimeter products used in advanced logic, memory and AI-related applications. Management described the overall market picture as stronger, but still uneven. The 200-millimeter business, which serves more mature chip applications, remained sluggish in the first half of the year, though the company expects a better second half.

Siltronic&rsquo;s performance also reflects the ramp-up of its FabNext Singapore plant. As utilization rises, the fixed-cost burden from the new site is falling. Management said the fab should approach group margin levels by the end of 2026.

Siltronic did not provide a reported EPS or revenue figure in the forecast data available for direct comparison with consensus. The forecast called for a loss of EUR 1.88 per share and revenue of about EUR 320 million, but the company&rsquo;s reported figures in the call were given in euro sales and operating metrics rather than a matched EPS release.

Even without a direct EPS comparison, the quarter appears to have been broadly in line with expectations for a gradual recovery. The company posted a diluted loss of $4.98 per share over the last twelve months. InvestingPro identifies weak gross profit margins as a key challenge&mdash;one of over 10 ProTips available to subscribers analyzing Siltronic’s investment potential. Sales of EUR 322 million were slightly above the EUR 320 million forecast level, while EBITDA and cash flow improved modestly. The results did not point to a major surprise, but they did support management&rsquo;s view that demand is recovering.

Siltronic shares rose 1.62% to $69.05 in premarket trading, up from a previous close of $67.95. The move suggests a cautious positive reaction to the quarter and the company&rsquo;s improved outlook. The stock remained far above its 52-week low of $31.7, but still well below the 52-week high of $108.8.

The gain was modest, which fits a report that showed better sales and margins but no clear profit turnaround. Investors appeared to focus on the stronger demand backdrop, the improved balance sheet after the capital increase and the company&rsquo;s more confident tone on the second half. At the same time, pricing pressure and continued losses likely limited the upside.

Siltronic slightly improved its full-year 2026 sales outlook. The company now expects sales to come in low to mid-single digits below 2025 levels, but on a like-for-like basis, excluding foreign exchange effects and the closure of its small-diameter business, sales are expected to be on or slightly above last year&rsquo;s level.

Management kept its full-year EBITDA margin guidance at 20% to 24%. The company said the sales outlook has improved, but the benefit is partly offset by higher freight and energy costs than originally expected.

For the rest of 2026, Siltronic expects a gradual recovery rather than a sharp jump in demand. The company sees stronger 300-millimeter demand, including requests from customers for volumes above long-term agreement levels. It also expects 200-millimeter volumes to improve in the second half, with pricing stabilizing from a low base.

The company said its FabNext Singapore site is progressing well and should approach group EBITDA margin levels by year-end. Management also said the June capital increase was opportunistic and gives the company more flexibility, but it is not tied to a specific spending plan.

Chief Executive Michael Heckmeier said, &ldquo;Demand is gaining traction. We see a market recovery in the 300-millimeter business. In 200 millimeter, conditions were still sluggish in the first half, but we are expecting a clear volume improvement for the second half of the year.&rdquo;

He also said pricing remains the key issue: &ldquo;Although demand for AI-related applications is strong, prices are still lagging behind.&rdquo;

On future investment, Heckmeier said, &ldquo;We always said prices need to come up and to cross the reinvest level. That holds true unchangeably.&rdquo;

Chief Financial Officer Claudia Schmitt said the Singapore ramp is helping profitability, noting that as loading increases, &ldquo;the fixed cost burn associated with our expanded production capacity in Singapore is gradually declining.&rdquo;

Analysts pressed management on pricing, capacity and the timing of future investment. Questions focused on whether Siltronic would wait for prices to rise above reinvestment levels before signing new long-term agreements, and how much room remained for further expansion in Singapore.

Management said the company is being selective with new volumes and wants to see stronger pricing before committing to more brownfield investment. It also said the Singapore fab still has room to ramp further, with equipment continuing to flow into the site.

Several questions centered on the 200-millimeter market. Management said demand is improving, especially from power chip makers tied to data center and power-management applications, but pricing still needs to recover from a very low base before the segment contributes meaningfully to profit.

Analysts also asked about long-term agreements. Siltronic said about two-thirds of its business is under LTAs, mostly in 300-millimeter wafers, and it does not expect a major expiration cliff in 2027 or 2028. The company said smaller agreements may be renegotiated under better terms if pricing improves.
