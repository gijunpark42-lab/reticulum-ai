Infineon Technologies said fiscal third-quarter revenue reached a record 4.172 billion euros, topping 4 billion euros for the first time in two and a half years as demand improved across automotive, industrial and, especially, artificial-intelligence-related power systems. The German chip maker said the recovery is broadening and raised its full-year revenue outlook to about 16.3 billion euros. The stock rose 3.88% in regular trading to $73.96, then held steady after hours.

Infineon said the quarter marked a clear turning point after a prolonged semiconductor downturn. Revenue rose 9% sequentially and nearly 13% from a year earlier, helped by stronger volume and a better product mix. Management said the recovery is now visible across more end markets, not just in isolated pockets.

The strongest momentum came from power systems for AI data centers, where demand continues to outpace available supply. Automotive also improved, supported by design wins and restocking, while industrial and energy infrastructure remained solid. Consumer demand, by contrast, stayed weaker than other segments.

The company also completed a major organizational change on July 1, moving from four divisions to three business units: Automotive, Power Systems and Edge Systems. It opened its Smart Power Fab in Dresden in early July and completed the acquisition of ams OSRAM&rsquo;s non-optical sensor portfolio, both moves aimed at strengthening future growth.

The provided forecast called for earnings per share of $0.52 and revenue of 4.78 billion dollars, but no actual EPS or revenue figures were supplied in that same format. Based on the earnings call, Infineon reported record quarterly revenue of 4.172 billion euros and said the result slightly exceeded expectations.

Management framed the quarter as a strong operational beat rather than a dramatic surprise. The company pointed to the return of revenue above 4 billion euros for the first time in 2.5 years, which is significant given the length of the semiconductor downturn. Margin improvement also suggests the business is regaining operating leverage as volumes rise.

Infineon shares closed at $73.96, up 3.88% from the previous close of $71.20. The stock finished the regular session near the upper end of its recent range, though still well below its 52-week high of $103.28. After hours, the shares were unchanged. The stock has delivered impressive returns, up roughly 82% over the past year and 53% over the last six months, according to InvestingPro data. Yet with a P/E ratio of 77.6, the company is trading at a high earnings multiple, and InvestingPro analysis suggests the stock is currently overvalued relative to its Fair Value&mdash;placing it among companies on the Most Overvalued list. The move suggests investors welcomed the stronger quarter and the company&rsquo;s upbeat comments on AI-related demand and future cash flow. The stock&rsquo;s reaction was positive but measured, indicating that some of the recovery story may already be reflected in the share price.

For fiscal 2026, Infineon kept its revenue outlook at about 16.3 billion euros and said it still expects a segment operating margin of about 20%, despite roughly 650 million euros in idle capacity costs. The company raised adjusted free cash flow guidance to about 1.85 billion euros.

For the next quarter, management expects revenue of about 4.7 billion euros and a segment profit margin of about 23%, both above typical seasonal patterns. The company also said its AI data center power supply revenue for fiscal 2026 should exceed 1.6 billion euros, up from a prior forecast of 1.5 billion euros.

Looking further ahead, Infineon said it will significantly raise its fiscal 2027 forecast for data center power supplies when it reports again in November. Management said the new figure will include both AI and traditional data centers, reflecting how agent-based AI is blurring the line between the two. Wall Street analysts remain bullish, with a consensus Buy rating and price targets suggesting roughly 58% upside potential from current levels, according to InvestingPro data.

Chairman Jochen Hanebeck said, &ldquo;The recovery is gaining momentum, the upswing is in full swing,&rdquo; adding that broader traction is now visible across multiple end markets.

He also said, &ldquo;Demand for our AI power supply solutions continues to exceed available supply. This part of our business is currently operating on a first come, first served basis.&rdquo; The comment underscores how tight supply conditions are helping support pricing and visibility.

On customer agreements, Hanebeck said the company has secured multi-year capacity reservation deals that represent &ldquo;a cumulative revenue volume in the high single-digit billion EUR range&rdquo; and include advance payments. He described the arrangement as a &ldquo;win-win situation&rdquo; because it improves planning and customer commitment.

Chief Financial Officer Sven Schneider said the stock price of around $60 to $65, in his view, was &ldquo;not a bad share price at all&rdquo; given the geopolitical backdrop and sector trends. He also noted that the quarter&rsquo;s margin increase from 19% to 23% was &ldquo;quite good.&rdquo;

Analysts focused on three main themes: AI demand, customer capacity agreements and China.

In response to questions about multi-year capacity reservations, Hanebeck said customers across the data center supply chain want to secure power components early because their own investments are large and interdependent. He said the agreements help Infineon plan better, increase visibility and bring in advance payments.

Asked about AI data center revenue, management said fiscal 2026 revenue for that business should rise above 1.6 billion euros, while the comparable figure for fiscal 2027 will be lifted significantly from the current 2.5 billion-euro baseline. Hanebeck said the final number will be disclosed in November.

Questions about China centered on competitive pressure and product mix. Hanebeck said Chinese rivals have become more aggressive in some segments, especially IGBTs, and that Infineon is shifting capacity toward AI-related products where demand is stronger and pricing is better. He also said automotive-qualified products remain in demand in China because some local suppliers cannot match Infineon&rsquo;s integrated manufacturing model.
