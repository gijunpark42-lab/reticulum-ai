MediaTek said second-quarter revenue rose above the top end of its guidance range, helped by stronger demand in smart edge products and early progress in its push into data center artificial intelligence. The company reported Q2 2026 revenue of TWD 152.2 billion, up 2% from the prior quarter and 1.2% from a year earlier, while earnings per share came in at TWD 15.28. The stock rose 2.7% to $3,235, according to the latest trading data, placing it well above the low end of its 52-week range but still below the high.

Key Takeaways

Revenue of TWD 152.2 billion topped the company’s guidance range and reflected solid demand in smart edge products. Mobile phone revenue remained weak, falling 14% sequentially and 20% from a year earlier. Smart Edge Platforms grew 19% quarter over quarter and 26% year over year, becoming the largest growth engine. MediaTek raised its 2027 AI accelerator market share target to 15% to 20% from 10% to 15%. The company expects first-generation AI accelerator production to start in the fourth quarter of 2026.

Company Performance

MediaTek’s second quarter showed a business that is still anchored by smartphones but increasingly shaped by growth in AI-related and connected devices. Revenue grew modestly from the first quarter and stayed slightly ahead of the year-earlier period. That was enough to beat the company’s own outlook, even as margins softened and mobile phone demand weakened.

The company said smart edge products, including connectivity, computing, automotive and TV chips, drove much of the improvement. That mix helped offset a difficult smartphone environment, where rising bill-of-materials costs continue to pressure demand. MediaTek said the global smartphone market is still expected to fall about 15% in unit terms in 2026.

At the same time, the company is trying to build a larger business in data center AI chips. Management said the first custom AI accelerator ASIC is on track for production in the fourth quarter, while a second-generation chip is moving toward high-volume production in 2028. The company also said it expects the 2027 serviceable addressable market for AI accelerator chips to reach TWD 80 billion.

Financial Highlights

Revenue: TWD 152.2 billion, up 2% sequentially and 1.2% year over year. Gross margin: 46.2%, down 0.1 percentage point from the prior quarter and 2.9 points from a year earlier. Operating income: TWD 22.9 billion, down 0.1% sequentially and 22.2% year over year. Operating margin: 15.0%, down 0.3 point sequentially and 4.5 points year over year. Net income: TWD 24.6 billion, up 0.9% sequentially and down 12.3% year over year. Net profit margin: 16.2%, down 0.1 point sequentially and 2.5 points year over year. EPS: TWD 15.28, up from TWD 15.17 in the previous quarter and down from TWD 17.5 a year earlier. Operating expenses: TWD 47.4 billion, up from TWD 46.2 billion in the first quarter and TWD 44.5 billion a year earlier. Smart Edge Platforms revenue: up 19% sequentially and 26% year over year. Mobile Phone revenue: down 14% sequentially and 20% year over year.

Earnings vs. Forecast

MediaTek’s revenue beat expectations. The company reported TWD 152.2 billion in quarterly revenue versus a forecast of TWD 145.51 billion, a difference of about TWD 6.69 billion, or roughly 4.6% above estimates. The quarter also came in above the high end of management’s guidance range.

That kind of beat is meaningful, though not large enough to suggest a dramatic reacceleration. Instead, it points to steady execution in a mixed environment. The company’s revenue growth was modest, but it was achieved despite clear weakness in mobile phones and margin pressure from higher costs.

EPS comparison is less straightforward because the provided forecast is not directly aligned with the company’s reported currency and format. Still, the call showed EPS of TWD 15.28, down from TWD 17.5 a year earlier, which suggests that profit growth lagged revenue growth. That makes the revenue beat more important than the earnings figure alone.

Market Reaction

The stock rose 2.7% to $3,235 in the latest trading data. That move suggests investors responded positively to the revenue beat and to the company’s larger AI ambitions. The share price sits well above the bottom of its 52-week range of $1,130 to $4,970, but it remains below the high, leaving room for further upside if execution stays on track.

Without detailed volume data or a precise pre-earnings price, it is difficult to measure the full trading pattern. Still, the available move points to a constructive reaction rather than a sharp re-rating. Investors appear to be balancing near-term softness in mobile phones against the longer-term opportunity in AI accelerators and smart edge products.

Outlook & Guidance

For the third quarter, MediaTek guided revenue to TWD 152.2 billion to TWD 159.8 billion, which implies flat to 5% sequential growth and 7% to 12% growth from a year earlier. The company expects gross margin of 46% plus or minus 1.5 percentage points and an operating expense ratio of 31% plus or minus 2 percentage points.

Management said it still aims to reach the high end of its full-year revenue target and to keep gross margin within the current guidance band. The company also said it expects first-generation AI accelerator revenue to exceed TWD 2 billion in 2026, with much larger scaling in 2027.

Several product launches and projects are expected to support growth in the coming quarters:

A 2-nanometer flagship smartphone chip is set to launch in the third quarter. New connectivity and automotive projects are scheduled to enter mass production in the third quarter. NVIDIA RTX Spark PCs are expected to reach retail shelves during the 2026 holiday season. Second-generation AI accelerator production is targeted for early 2028.

MediaTek also said its board approved a discretionary $5 billion financing budget to support strategic expansion and supply chain capacity if needed.

Executive Commentary

Chief Executive Rick Tsai said the company’s second-quarter revenue “achieved TWD 152 billion, exceeding the high end of our guidance range,” and credited smart edge products for the result. His comments suggest the company is still benefiting from broad product momentum outside its core smartphone business.

Tsai also said the company’s first AI accelerator ASIC is scheduled to begin production in the fourth quarter and that data center revenue should exceed TWD 2 billion in 2026. He added that MediaTek now sees the 2027 AI accelerator market at TWD 80 billion and has raised its share target to 15% to 20%.

On the longer-term opportunity, Tsai said MediaTek believes 2028 will be “quite a bit higher than 2027” and that having both first- and second-generation ASICs in production should enhance market share “in a very significant manner.”

Chief Financial Officer David Ku said pricing actions are meant to pass through higher supply chain costs rather than expand margins. “The goal should be to passing through the cost pressure and share it with our customer,” he said.

Risks and Challenges

Weak smartphone demand: Mobile phone revenue fell sharply, showing that the core business remains under pressure. Margin pressure: Gross margin and operating margin both declined year over year, reflecting cost and mix challenges. Execution risk in AI chips: The company is entering a complex market with advanced packaging, memory and supply chain demands. Supply chain dependence: MediaTek relies on foundry, substrate and memory partners to support its AI plans. Pricing pressure: Management said it is passing through higher costs, which may limit margin expansion.

Q&A

Analysts focused heavily on MediaTek’s AI accelerator strategy, especially the size of the market, the timing of second-generation production and the company’s long-term share potential. Management said the 2027 market estimate of TWD 80 billion includes only AI accelerator chips, excluding HBM memory and other components.

Questions also centered on packaging technology. MediaTek said its current approach for second-generation production is on track, with EMIB technology improving in yield and maturity. The company said 448G SerDes IP should be ready in the second half of 2027, while optical solutions are likely to follow later.

Analysts asked about the $5 billion financing budget, and management said it is meant to give the company flexibility to support growth and, if needed, help expand key suppliers. The company also said its near-term data center revenue target is mainly driven by the first AI project and that supply chain capacity appears sufficient for 2026 and 2027.

Several questions addressed AI smartphones and edge devices. MediaTek said it sees multiple device formats emerging and believes its architecture and computing capabilities position it well across those possibilities. Management also said it is seeing strong interest in agentic AI devices, especially in China, where changing use cases may influence future chip design.
