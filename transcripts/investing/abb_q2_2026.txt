ABB reported record second-quarter 2026 orders, revenue and backlog, but its shares fell 2.8% in pre-market trading as investors weighed strong operating momentum against margin pressure, a large acquisition and the absence of a clear EPS comparison in the initial data. The Swiss industrial company said revenue rose to $9.5 billion, slightly above the $9.47 billion forecast, while operational EBITA climbed 20% to $1.9 billion and the margin improved to 20.2%. Orders reached a record $12.0 billion, and the backlog rose to $30 billion. The stock last traded at $80.84, down from $83.18, leaving it about 9% below its 52-week high of $89.14.

ABB said it delivered record-breaking results in the second quarter, with growth across all three business areas. The company described demand as broad-based and tied to long-term themes such as energy expansion, energy efficiency and energy resilience.

The strongest performance came from Electrification, where orders topped $7 billion for the first time and data centers remained a major growth driver. Motion also posted solid order growth, while Automation benefited from marine, electrification and oil-and-gas demand.

The quarter reinforced ABB&rsquo;s position as a major industrial supplier with exposure to power infrastructure, factory automation and data center build-outs. The company said it is investing in capacity in the U.S., Europe, China and India to support future demand.

ABB&rsquo;s revenue came in slightly above expectations. Analysts had forecast revenue of $9.47 billion, while the company reported $9.5 billion, a beat of about $30 million, or roughly 0.3%.

The EPS comparison was not available in the forecast data provided, so a direct earnings-per-share surprise could not be calculated. Still, the company said EPS rose 8% from a year earlier, which points to continued profit growth.

The revenue beat was modest, but the broader operating picture was strong. ABB delivered record orders, record backlog and a higher operating margin, which may matter more to investors than the small gap versus sales estimates.

ABB shares fell 2.81% to $80.84 from a previous close of $83.18 in pre-market trading. The move came despite the strong quarterly report, suggesting investors may have focused on other issues, including margin pressure, acquisition spending and the stock&rsquo;s recent strength.

At the current price, ABB is trading near the top of its 52-week range of $49.80 to $89.14. The stock is about 9.4% below its high and more than 60% above its low, showing that it has already had a strong run over the past year. The company’s shares carry a P/E ratio of 37.5, and InvestingPro analysis indicates the stock is currently overvalued relative to its Fair Value&mdash;a factor that may be contributing to investor caution despite strong operational results. For investors tracking valuation metrics, ABB appears on InvestingPro’s most overvalued stocks list.

No trading volume data was provided, so it is not possible to assess whether the move came with unusually heavy activity. The decline appears to reflect a cautious first reaction rather than a clear change in the company&rsquo;s long-term story.

ABB raised its full-year 2026 outlook for comparable revenue growth to low double-digit to low teens growth. The company also said operational EBITA margin should improve from 2025, excluding the benefit from a real-estate gain booked in the first quarter. According to InvestingPro, which tracks 18 additional tips for ABB, the company has raised its dividend for five consecutive years and maintains a perfect Piotroski Score of 9, indicating strong financial health. Analysts surveyed by InvestingPro have set price targets ranging from $73 to $118.

For the third quarter, ABB expects comparable revenue growth in the low to mid-teens range and sequential margin improvement. In Electrification, the company guided for revenue growth at least similar to the second quarter and said margins should improve. Motion is expected to post mid- to high-single-digit revenue growth, while Automation should see mid-single-digit growth and better margins year over year.

ABB also said free cash flow should improve from 2025&rsquo;s $4.6 billion level. The company is expanding manufacturing capacity to support its order backlog and said it only accepts orders it believes it can deliver on time.

&ldquo;We delivered on our guidance, I&rsquo;m pleased with the results,&rdquo; Chief Executive Morten Wierod said.

&ldquo;We converted the 12% comparable revenue growth to a 20% increase in Operational EBITA to $1.9 billion. This reflects a margin improvement of 90 basis points to 20.2%,&rdquo; he said, highlighting the company&rsquo;s operating leverage.

On the acquisition strategy, Wierod said, &ldquo;This is an important step to expand the ABB automation offering. It broadens our scope in what we call the Sense, Control, Act automation loop.&rdquo;

He also said ABB is not returning to the general power transformer business, clarifying that its recent purchases are focused on custom-engineered industrial and energy components.

Analysts focused heavily on the durability of Electrification demand, especially in data centers. Management said the growth is driven by real customer investment rather than longer lead times or pre-buying, and it said the pipeline remains strong.

Questions also centered on pricing, with ABB saying pricing rose about 2% in the quarter and varied by region, with the strongest increases in the Americas. Analysts asked whether the company could sustain current order levels, and management said the backlog is healthy but quarterly order intake will remain uneven.

The Rotork acquisition drew several questions about timing, valuation and synergies. ABB said the deal fits its &ldquo;Sense, Control, Act&rdquo; strategy and will be run as a separate division within Automation. Management said synergies will come mainly from revenue growth and service expansion, with some procurement and manufacturing benefits.

Analysts also asked about ABB&rsquo;s 800V DC data center technology. Management said there are no orders in backlog yet, but it sees a commercial opportunity later in 2027 or 2028 and is investing heavily in research and development.
