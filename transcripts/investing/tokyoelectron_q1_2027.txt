---
Tokyo Electron reported mixed but broadly solid first-quarter results for fiscal 2027, with earnings per share of 361.36 yen topping the 357.17 yen forecast, while revenue of 732.39 billion yen fell short of the 760.35 billion yen estimate. The semiconductor equipment maker said net sales and gross profit both reached record highs, and the stock rose 6.24% to $55,500 from the previous close of $52,240, suggesting investors focused on the company’s upbeat outlook and AI-related demand rather than the revenue miss.

Key Takeaways

EPS beat estimates by 1.17%, even as revenue missed forecasts by 3.68%.Net sales rose 2.9% sequentially to a record 732.3 billion yen.Gross profit also hit a record, while operating income increased 2.8% from the prior quarter.Management raised its outlook for the WFE market and said AI data center demand is driving growth.The company expects first-half sales above 1.6 trillion yen and sees full-year sales of 3 trillion yen or more.

Company Performance

Tokyo Electron said first-quarter fiscal 2027 was its strongest quarter yet for net sales and gross profit, helped by robust demand for semiconductor manufacturing tools used in advanced logic and memory production. Sales rose 2.9% from the previous quarter to 732.3 billion yen, while gross profit climbed to 342.7 billion yen, also up 2.9%.

Operating income increased to 211.4 billion yen, reflecting steady margins and strong demand. The company’s operating margin held at 28.9%, showing that profitability remained high even as the business continued to scale.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent fell 23.3% to 164.3 billion yen, but management said the decline mainly reflected the absence of extraordinary income from strategic share sales booked in the previous quarter.

The company’s performance remained tied to the broader semiconductor capital spending cycle, which management said is being driven by AI data center buildouts, advanced DRAM, NAND, and leading-edge logic. Tokyo Electron also pointed to strength in Taiwan and China, where sales rose and the share of total revenue increased.

Financial Highlights

Revenue: 732.3 billion yen, up 2.9% sequentially and a record high.Gross profit: 342.7 billion yen, up 2.9% sequentially and a record high.Gross margin: 46.8%, unchanged from the prior quarter.Operating income: 211.4 billion yen, up 2.8% sequentially.Operating margin: 28.9%, steady from the prior quarter.Net income: 164.3 billion yen, down 23.3% sequentially.Free cash flow: 80.5 billion yen.Cash and cash equivalents: 409.6 billion yen.Total assets: 2,955.4 billion yen.Taiwan sales: up 18.9% sequentially, reaching 25.4% of total sales.Return on equity: 29% over the last twelve months, demonstrating strong profitability.Dividend yield: 1.43%, with the company maintaining dividend payments for 35 consecutive years.

Earnings vs. Forecast

Tokyo Electron’s earnings beat was modest. EPS came in at 361.36 yen, compared with a forecast of 357.17 yen, a difference of 4.19 yen, or 1.17% above expectations. Revenue was 732.39 billion yen, below the 760.35 billion yen forecast by 27.96 billion yen, or 3.68%.

The quarter was therefore mixed rather than cleanly positive. Still, the market appeared to give more weight to the company’s record sales, strong margin profile, and confident outlook. The EPS beat was small, but it helped offset the revenue miss, which was also not large enough to suggest a major slowdown.

Compared with the company’s recent trend, the quarter showed continued resilience. Tokyo Electron has been benefiting from strong demand in AI-related semiconductor equipment, and management said the current cycle still has room to run. The modest scale of the beat and miss suggests investors likely saw the results as consistent with a company executing well in a strong market, rather than as a major surprise.

Market Reaction

Tokyo Electron shares rose 6.24% to $55,500 from $52,240 after the report. The move added $3,260 per share and left the stock well above the lower end of its 52-week range of $19,870, though still below the high of $81,260.

The stock’s rise suggests investors were encouraged by the company’s record quarterly sales, strong AI-related demand commentary, and guidance for continued growth. The market also appeared to look past the revenue miss, likely because management’s outlook pointed to stronger second-half demand and improving profitability.

No volume data was provided, so it is not possible to determine whether the move came with unusually heavy trading. Even so, the size of the gain indicates a clear positive reaction.

Outlook & Guidance

Management raised its outlook for the wafer fabrication equipment market, saying it now expects the market to reach $150 billion or more in calendar 2026 and $190 billion or more in calendar 2027. The company said both years should deliver growth of 20% or more.

For Tokyo Electron itself, management said first-half fiscal 2027 sales are expected to exceed 1.6 trillion yen, a record level. The company also said it is becoming more confident in reaching 3 trillion yen or more in full-year sales.

The company expects new equipment sales in the first half to rise 45% year over year to 1.24 trillion yen, with the second quarter likely to set a quarterly record above 700 billion yen. Management said operating income could approach 1 trillion yen within fiscal 2027.

Management said it aims to reach a gross margin of 50% or more within about two years and maintain a 35% operating margin target under its mid-term plan.

Executive Commentary

President and CEO Toshiki Kawai said the company’s sites in Kumamoto were safe after the recent earthquake and added that operations should return to normal early the following week with only a minor business impact.

Kawai also highlighted the company’s market outlook, saying, “WFE market outlook has been updated from April projection to $150 billion or more in CY 2026 and $190 billion or more in CY 2027. In both years, we expect 20% or more year-over-year growth.”

On the company’s longer-term strategy, he said, “We will launch high value-added new models and upgrades in a timely manner. At the same time, we will enhance services by leveraging AI and robotics so that we can contribute to the improvement of yield and equipment uptime ratio in our customers’ fabs.”

CFO Hiroshi Kawamoto said the quarter reflected customer demand and strong execution, noting that net sales and gross profit both hit record highs.

Risks and Challenges

Revenue volatility: The quarter missed revenue expectations, showing that demand can still vary by timing.Margin pressure: Management said second-quarter operating margin may decline slightly because of R&D, labor costs, and product mix.Currency effects: Tokyo Electron said exchange-rate shifts have weighed on market share and reported results.Export controls: U.S.-China trade restrictions continue to affect competitive dynamics, especially in etch systems.Earthquake and supply chain risk: The Kumamoto quake caused some disruption, even if the company expects limited damage.

Q&A

Analysts focused heavily on market growth, pricing, margins, and capacity. One key topic was the company’s conservative WFE market forecast. Management said its estimates reflect real capacity and distribution constraints, even though some analysts see the market reaching about $200 billion in 2027.

Pricing was another major theme. Management said price revisions are being negotiated with multiple customers and are being applied to existing models as well. The company said the effects should become visible in the second half and could have a larger impact in the fourth quarter.

Analysts also asked whether Tokyo Electron can meet future demand if the market expands further. Management said it is preparing through customer roadmaps and the new Miyagi smart fab, which is expected to triple capacity.

Questions about profitability centered on the path to a 50% gross margin and a 35% operating margin. Management said the 50% gross margin target could be reached in early fiscal 2028, though strong demand could bring that forward.

Analysts also pressed management on market share decline. Tokyo Electron pointed to currency effects, export controls, and changing customer investment patterns, while saying its new management structure and five profit-improvement initiatives should help reverse the trend.
