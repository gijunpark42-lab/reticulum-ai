---
Alfa Laval reported a record second quarter, with order intake climbing to SEK 22 billion and the order book reaching SEK 53.5 billion, but the stock fell 1.5% in pre-market trading to $551.8. The company said demand was strong across most of its businesses, led by data center cooling, marine equipment and food and pharma systems. It also kept its full-year capital spending guidance unchanged, while warning that third-quarter order intake should be somewhat lower than the record pace in Q2.

Key Takeaways

Order intake hit a record SEK 22 billion, up 35.2% from a year earlier.The order book rose to SEK 53.5 billion, giving the company strong visibility into future sales.Data center cooling remained a major growth driver, with 12-month orders running at about SEK 5 billion.Alfa Laval booked a landmark SEK 1.1 billion biofuel project in Brazil and new orders in the LR tanker segment.Shares fell 1.5% before the open, suggesting investors focused more on margin pressure and guidance than on the strong demand picture.

Company Performance

Alfa Laval said Q2 was one of the strongest quarters in its history. Order intake rose sharply, sales increased, and the company said demand was broad-based across nearly all parts of the business. Management described the quarter as another step toward its SEK 100 billion growth plan for 2030.

The Energy division was the standout, with order growth of 70%, helped by data center cooling and the Cryo acquisition. Food & Pharma posted all-time high quarterly results, while the Marine division won new business in the LR tanker segment, a market where Alfa Laval had little presence before. Service also returned to solid growth, with 10% organic growth.

The company’s performance reflects a broader trend in industrial technology: customers are still spending on energy efficiency, infrastructure and specialized equipment, even as some end markets remain uneven. Alfa Laval said the U.S. and China together accounted for more than 40% of 12-month order intake.

Financial Highlights

Order intake: SEK 22 billion, up 35.2% year over year; 28.5% organic growth.Sales: SEK 18.1 billion, up 7.7% year over year; 5.9% organic growth.Order book: SEK 53.5 billion, with SEK 29.1 billion scheduled for 2026 delivery and SEK 24.5 billion for 2027.Book-to-bill ratio: 1.23, indicating orders outpaced sales.Operating income: SEK 2.9 billion.Adjusted EBITDA margin: 17%.EPS: SEK 4.91 in the quarter, SEK 9.5 year to date.Cash flow from operating activities: SEK 2.4 billion in Q2.Net debt-to-EBITDA ratio: 1.1x, including leases.Market capitalization: $23.6 billion.Return on equity: 18% over the last twelve months.Debt-to-equity ratio: 0.41, reflecting moderate leverage.Dividend: SEK 3.7 billion, described as a record payout.

Earnings vs. Forecast

The company’s reported EPS and revenue were not provided in the same currency and format as the consensus forecast, so a direct like-for-like comparison is not available from the supplied figures. The forecast called for EPS of $5.55 and revenue of $17.93 billion for the period.

Even without a direct comparison, the quarter appears to have been operationally strong. Alfa Laval delivered record orders, a large backlog and continued sales growth. The main investor question is less about demand and more about how much of that demand can turn into profit, given the pressure from mix, inflation, provisions and restructuring costs.

Market Reaction

Alfa Laval shares were down 1.5% to $551.8 in pre-market trading, compared with the previous close of $560.2. That puts the stock about $8.4 lower on the day. The shares remain near the upper end of their 52-week range of $399.2 to $592, trading about 7.8% below the high.

The modest decline suggests investors were not alarmed by the results, but they may have been cautious after a quarter that was strong on orders yet less impressive on profitability. The stock’s reaction also points to a common market pattern: when expectations are already high, even very good demand numbers may not be enough to lift the shares if margins are under pressure or guidance is conservative.

Outlook & Guidance

Management said Q3 order intake should be somewhat lower than the record Q2 level, though it still expects strong activity. The Marine division is expected to stay around Q2 levels, while Energy and Food & Pharma are expected to be lower sequentially.

The company kept its full-year guidance unchanged:

Alfa Laval also said capital spending will stay elevated in the medium term to support customer agreements beyond 2027, especially in data center cooling and marine capacity expansion. The company said the data center business is running at about a SEK 5 billion annual order rate and still has room to grow.

Executive Commentary

CEO Tom said the quarter showed broad demand strength and record orders. “It was a quarter with solid demand across almost all of the part of the business, with a new all-time high of just above SEK 22 billion,” he said.

He also highlighted the company’s position in data centers. “We are very confident that our market share has increased during this year,” he said, adding that Alfa Laval is the “go-to partner and the anchor partner” for many expansion plans.

On the marine business, he pointed to new opportunities in LR tankers. “It was also a breakthrough quarter with orders for the LR tanker segment,” he said.

CFO Fredrik said the company’s restructuring was meant to support growth, not simply cut costs. “The reorganization [is] an enabler to scaling for growth,” he said.

Risks and Challenges

Margin pressure: Alfa Laval said profitability was affected by product mix, inflation, provisions and restructuring costs.High utilization: Most factories are running at very high levels, which can strain operations and limit near-term upside.Capital spending: Elevated CapEx supports growth, but it can weigh on free cash flow in the short term.Sequential slowdown: Q3 order intake is expected to be lower than the record Q2 level.Execution risk in new markets: The company is expanding in data centers, LNG and LR tankers, which brings opportunity but also operational risk.

Q&A

Analysts focused on whether the strong order growth was sustainable and how much margin pressure the company might face from new business areas. Questions also centered on data center profitability, the pace of pharma investment, and the outlook for marine demand.

Management said it does not expect broad margin deterioration from data center business, but it also avoided giving detailed margin comments by segment. On pharma, it said the investment is a long-term strategy that will take a couple of years to work through.

Analysts also asked about the LR tanker opportunity, shipyard capacity, and whether the company is seeing more underlying market activity than before. Management said yes, but it also stressed that some of the strength reflects speculative contracting and new entrants rather than a simple cyclical boom.

Another focus was manufacturing capacity. Management said most plants are already running at very high utilization, and that new capacity will ramp gradually rather than all at once. That means the company may need time before record orders translate into faster invoicing and stronger profit growth.
