Kioxia Holdings posted record quarterly revenue and profit in Q1 FY2026, driven by a sharp rise in average selling prices, stronger volumes and booming demand from data centers and enterprise customers tied to artificial intelligence. Revenue climbed 415.5% from a year earlier to JPY 1,767.1 billion, while non-GAAP operating profit reached JPY 1,326.2 billion, more than the company earned in all of FY2025. The stock rose 17.72% to $46,500, reflecting investor enthusiasm for the results, a JPY 800 billion buyback plan and a planned 3-for-1 stock split.

Kioxia said the quarter marked record highs across revenue, gross profit, operating profit, net income and cash flow. The company benefited from a favorable NAND market, where AI-related demand has been lifting data center and enterprise storage purchases.

Management said the strongest demand came from SSD and storage products used in servers and enterprise systems. That segment generated JPY 1,174.7 billion in revenue, up 95.7% from the prior quarter and 440.3% from a year earlier. Smart devices also improved, with revenue of JPY 525.7 billion, up 55.8% sequentially.

The company also highlighted a major improvement in its balance sheet. It repaid all senior debt in the quarter and said it had moved to a net cash position. Cash and cash equivalents reached a record JPY 791.0 billion.

Kioxia&rsquo;s Q1 revenue of JPY 1,767.1 billion came in below the forecast of JPY 1,803.98 billion, a gap of JPY 36.88 billion, or about 2.0%. The company did not provide a matching actual EPS figure in the forecast table, so a direct EPS surprise could not be calculated from that data.

Even so, the quarter was far stronger than the revenue miss alone suggests. Revenue rose 76.2% sequentially and more than fivefold from a year earlier, while operating profit and cash flow both set records. In that context, the shortfall versus the forecast appears modest and unlikely to change the broader view of the company&rsquo;s momentum.

Kioxia shares rose 17.72% to $46,500 from $39,500 in the previous session, a gain of $7,000. The move pushed the stock well above its recent close and left it far above the 52-week low of $2,270, though still below the 52-week high of $112,700.

The sharp jump suggests investors focused on the scale of the profit improvement, the stronger outlook for AI-related storage demand and the company&rsquo;s capital-return plans. The buyback announcement likely added support, since repurchases can lift earnings per share and signal management confidence.

No trading volume figures were provided, but the size of the move points to unusually strong interest in the shares.

For Q2 FY2026, Kioxia guided for revenue of JPY 2,390.0 billion, which would be up 35% from Q1. The company also forecast non-GAAP operating profit of JPY 1,900 billion and non-GAAP net income of JPY 1,280 billion. Analysts expect earnings per share to reach $55.07 for fiscal 2027, and InvestingPro data shows consensus anticipates continued sales growth in the current year. Management said about 70% of the expected Q2 growth will come from higher average selling prices, with low-single-digit volume growth also contributing. The company expects continued strength in data center and enterprise demand, while smartphone demand should remain stable and PC demand may be slightly weaker.

Kioxia said it is investing in next-generation NAND technology, including BiCS10 production at its Kitakami plant and development of BiCS11. It is also working on AI inference SSDs and UFS 5.0 for edge AI devices.

The company said it plans average annual capital spending of JPY 470 billion from FY2026 through FY2028 and annual research and development spending of about JPY 200 billion in FY2026.

Toshiaki Fujikawa, head of corporate communication, said AI demand is now the main force in the NAND market. &ldquo;Currently, growth in AI demand is driving the NAND market, with data centers and enterprise applications leading the growth,&rdquo; he said.

He also said the company sees long-term demand strength ahead. &ldquo;For calendar year 2026, in terms of bit growth, we currently anticipate the NAND market to grow in the high teens percentage range,&rdquo; Fujikawa said. &ldquo;Looking ahead to calendar year 2027, we forecast that demand will exceed supply.&rdquo;

Chief Financial Officer Yoshihiko Kawamura pointed to the scale of the quarter&rsquo;s improvement. &ldquo;Across the board, we achieved record high numbers in both revenue and profit,&rdquo; he said.

Kawamura also said the company is prioritizing quality over volume. &ldquo;Instead of pursuing the quantity, that we will pursue the quality and profitability,&rdquo; he said.

Despite these challenges, InvestingPro assigns Kioxia a "GOOD" Financial Health score of 2.99 out of 5, with particularly strong marks in profitability and growth. Analysts focused on the drivers of Q2 growth, shareholder returns, long-term agreements with hyperscaler customers and the outlook for AI inference demand.

On growth, management said about 70% of Q2&rsquo;s expected increase will come from pricing, with volume growth still positive. On capital returns, executives said the JPY 800 billion buyback was timed to take advantage of a lower share price and improve EPS.

Questions also centered on long-term agreements, or LTAs, with major customers. Kioxia said it wants 50% of shipment volume covered by LTAs by 2028 and said it is &ldquo;pretty much on track&rdquo; to reach that goal.

Analysts asked about AI inference demand and whether Chinese open-source AI models could change storage demand. Management said inference AI is still early but has significant room to grow, and argued that lower AI costs could expand usage and ultimately increase storage demand.

The company also addressed concerns about competition and capacity spending. Executives said they are not trying to win share through aggressive investment, but instead are focusing on higher-speed, more energy-efficient and higher-quality products.
