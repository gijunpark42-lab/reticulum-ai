ADVANCED PACKAGING process-control systems revenue now expected ~$1.1 BILLION in CY2026, UP MORE THAN 70% YoY (raised from high-50s% growth) — almost 2x the advanced-packaging market's growth. [Q&A highlights]
- C.J. Muse (Cantor): GM trajectory — memory-pricing headwind ~100bps and 'probably a little bit more,' LIKELY CONTINUES THROUGH 2027 (buying memory at higher prices as demand strengthens), then normalizes from headwind to TAILWIND; CY26 GM ~62%+; 60-65% incremental GM model through 2027; new products reset cost + pricing. Services: new LT model 13-15% growth — at the BOTTOM this year, toward the HIGHER END next year on rising shipments; 80% contract-based.
- Harlan Sur (JPM): WFE raised ~4 times this year. Broadening leading-edge logic: 'the world needs more advanced logic' — customers wanted suppliers beyond the leader and it's happening (analyst cites Intel PULLING IN 14A BY A YEAR with KLA share rising, Samsung foundry engagements incl. Broadcom at 2nm/3nm, Rapidus, new 'Terafab' initiatives); part of why the 2027 setup is strong.
- Vivek Arya (BofA): China/process-control moat — high-mix/low-volume market (unlike litho), algorithm depth, 1,600-1,700 worldwide applications engineers, leading-edge learning loops; 'where we can compete, we generally win'; '6x our nearest rival.' 2027 rank-order: broadening leading-edge logic; HBM process-control intensity 'RIVALING WHAT WE SEE IN ADVANCED LOGIC' (base-die customization, DRAM integration, device value, performance specs); conventional DRAM intensity lower than HBM; GREENFIELD fabs (new tools, not just upgrades); advanced packaging; possible upside from legacy customers entering HPC.
- Krish Sankar (TD Cowen): pricing — input costs up, customers recognize it; price resets come WITH NEW PRODUCTS ('hard to change prices on taken orders'); memory headwind flips to tailwind as pricing normalizes.
- Blayne Curtis (Jefferies): could KLA support a $300B WFE? — actively doing SUPPLIER CAPACITY AGREEMENTS NOW FOR 2029-AND-BEYOND needs (optical components take 12-24 months to capacitize); own facilities/headcount not the gate; industry signals would be visible in time. RPO/backlog: ~$12.5 BILLION expected in the imminent 10-Q, growing consistently over recent quarters.
- Tim Arcuri (UBS): GM flat on +10% revenue = memory/tariff absorption; December GM in similar range, mix-dependent (63.5% +/- 500bps frame).
- Joe Quatrochi (WF): CY26 foundry/logic ~low-to-mid 60s% of semi PC systems; memory share RISES in December quarter and stays through H1 2027. LEAD TIMES: ~12 months company average, 18-24 months on certain products; customer conversations now about H2-2027 DELIVERIES.
- Stacy Rasgon (Bernstein): 'sizing the company to serve the more bullish scenarios' = scenario-planning demand +10-20% above the ~$190B consensus view for 2027 and securing capacity/inventory for it (low risk on long-lead components given product value). 2030 model ($215B WFE, $1.4T semi revenue — the latter likely hit ALREADY in 2026, partly price-driven): if the market normalizes higher, KLA EXCEEDS the 2030 model; share to grow ~150bps by 2030; 'we're really good at process control... we are not very good at forecasting.'
- Melissa Weathers (Deutsche): EPC/Orbotech long-term CAGR raised to HIGH single digits (from mid-to-high); HPC changing a historically mobility-tied PCB/substrate business — 'I wish I could ship more.' Advanced-packaging market growth view raised from ~20% to MID-TO-HIGH 30s% for 2026, KLA ~2x that; front-end-designed systems pulled into packaging (SOIC, hybrid bonding, die-to-wafer next); shorter lead times.
- Atif Malik (Citi): 2027 confidence = customer demand-sharing + cross-checks with compute buyers; memory supply-demand equilibrium 'a ways out'; 'very few concerns about 2027 — the build-out continues.' SpaceX Terafab engagement: no comment on specific customers.
- Shane Brett (MS): 2027 intensity tailwinds: new-node investment returns, DRAM greenfield + retooling (yield value very high at current memory prices), packaging strong, some NAND greenfield; supply availability better than today. Competition: 'net share gainers for the foreseeable future'; e-beam momentum; share would be higher still if allowed to compete in restricted China fabs.
